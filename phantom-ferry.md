## What Changed in v5.1

Six games on the Live ladder, three wins and three losses, and two complaints from the pilot: Energy was not reaching the hand, and the Okidogi turns kept going wrong.

The first is arithmetic. Every accelerator in v5 except Crispin reads the discard pile, so turns one through four run on raw draws, and ten Energy in sixty is thin for four turns of manual attachments.

| Energy in deck | Zero in the opening 7 | One or fewer in the first 9 cards | Two or fewer by turn 4 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10 | 26% | 53% | 74% |
| 12 | 19% | 42% | 62% |

Two Energy Search take the deck to the second row without adding a dead late-game draw. It is an Item, so it costs no Supporter; it is live on turn one when the discard is empty; it fetches the exact color the turn needs; and Petrel can find it.

| Out | In | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Wally's Compassion | Energy Search | Healing Gengar puts every Energy on it back in hand, and the ferry stops for two turns. The card fought the plan. |
| Haunter (Perfect Order 049) | Energy Search | Candy insurance on a line with three Rare Candy and Dawn for two Megas. [Test and Tune](#test-and-tune) has it back if the Candy keeps ending up in the Prizes. |

The second complaint was the page's fault. _Poisonous Musculature_ costs one Energy of any type, and the v5 text never said so; an empty Okidogi cannot use it. [Okidogi ex](#okidogi-ex) and [How To Play Okidogi](#4-how-to-play-okidogi) now lead with the cost, and the mistakes table carries the three ways the Poison gets lost.

## What Changed in v5

The Live list that went 0-6 was not losing on card quality. It was losing on three numbers and one design choice. The numbers were 8 Basics, 7 Items, and 14 Energy with no way to attach more than one a turn. The design choice was two Megas both trying to be the attacker, so the single attachment got split between two colors and neither wall was ever loaded.

| What the games showed | The list that showed it | v5 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mulligans one game in three, dead hands full of Stage 2s | 8 Basics under 13 Evolution cards | 10 Basics under 9 Evolution cards. Gengar ex, the third Mega Gengar, and two Haunter leave; two Okidogi ex and a fourth Litwick arrive. |
| Every setup play cost the Supporter | 1 Poffin, no Ultra Ball, no Poké Pad | 2 Poffin and 2 Ultra Ball beside 3 Dawn. Seven Item-or-Supporter outs to a Basic instead of five. |
| Neither Mega ever had two of its own color | 14 Energy, one attachment a turn, Recycler putting the discard back in the deck | 10 Energy, and four ways to attach more than one: the ferry, 2 Crispin, 2 Rosa's Encouragement, 2 Wondrous Patch. Recycler is gone because it empties the pile those cards feed on. |
| Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon ex walled the whole deck | Every attacker had an Ability | Okidogi ex. No Ability, 260 for one attack turn plus one attachment, and Cornerstone's own attack ignores Weakness so it survives the return hit. |
| Munkidori drew fire and dealt none | 1 Munkidori | Out. Battle Cage already does its Bench-protection job. |
| Stranded Actives | 1 Switch | 2 Air Balloon, one per Mega, plus Prime Catcher. |

Fourteen cards out of the Live list: Gengar ex, the third Mega Gengar, two Haunter, Munkidori, one Dawn, both Hilda, Energy Recycler, Switch, Telepathic Psychic Energy, two Darkness, and one Psychic. Fourteen in: the fourth Litwick, two Okidogi ex, two Rosa's Encouragement, the second Poffin, two Ultra Ball, two Wondrous Patch, two Night Stretcher, and two Air Balloon.

## What The Research Says

Five findings shaped this 60, and each one is checkable.

**Nobody plays this, and the records say why.** Per [Limitless online play](https://play.limitlesstcg.com/decks?game=PTCG&format=standard&time=1month) over the last month, 536 tournaments and 39,181 players, Mega Chandelure is 1.15% of the field at a 43% win rate and Mega Gengar is 0.03% at 16%. The [Mega Chandelure overview](https://limitlesstcg.com/decks/375) and the [Mega Gengar overview](https://limitlesstcg.com/decks/358) each hold exactly one recorded finish this year, and no list on either page runs the other ghost. The one Chandelure list with a Worlds finish, [Alexandre Solé's 115th](https://limitlesstcg.com/decks/list/28845), is the meta's shape: four Fire Chandelure as the draw engine, four Hilda, four Ultra Ball, four Telepathic, and Neo Upper Energy. This deck borrows its Item counts and nothing else.

**Void Gale moves any Energy, not just Darkness.** The text is "Move an Energy from this Pokémon to 1 of your Benched Pokémon." Read as a ferry rather than a tax, it means the deck's one manual attachment can go onto Mega Gengar as a Psychic, ride a 230 swing, and land on the Lampent behind it. Gengar needs a third Energy aboard once; after that every attachment passes through it. That is the engine, and [The Ferry](#2-the-ferry) is the timetable.

**Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon ex, read exactly.** Cornerstone Stance prevents all damage from attacks by your Pokémon that have an Ability. Demolish is [F][C][C] for 140 and its damage is not affected by Weakness or Resistance. Retreat 1, Weak to Grass, 210 HP, and its Tera rule protects it from attack damage on the Bench. As its own archetype it is 0.01% of the ladder; it appears as a tech in Crustle lists, including one that finished at Worlds 2026, and a handful of others. Every attacker in the last three builds had an Ability, so the wall was total. Okidogi ex has none, hits 260, and takes a flat 140 back.

**Exactly four cards in Standard attach more than one Energy to this deck's colors on a turn the deck can afford.** A sweep of the full legal pool for every attach effect, filtered to Psychic, Darkness, or type-blind, and to costs that do not eat the attack for a Mega:

# Pokémon

### Litwick

The Basic the deck stands on, and the only Psychic Litwick ever printed. Four copies, up from three; the fourth is the cheapest Basic the mulligan rate could buy. 70 HP keeps it inside Poffin range. Going second, _Will-O-Wisp_ for 20 is a real turn-one attack, and Litwick benched on turn one is the Litwick Rare Candy lifts on turn two.

### Lampent

**_Spreading Light_ schedules the Megas.** Search your deck for up to 3 Lampent and put them onto your Bench. They arrive as Lampent, so each one is a Mega Chandelure next turn with no Rare Candy spent, which is why the Candy count can sit at three for two Stage 2 lines. Three copies, so the fetch caps at two in practice, which is the number of chairs the Bench has once Gengar, Okidogi, and a Gastly are seated.

### Mega Chandelure ex

**Stage 2 from Lampent. Gives up 3 Prize cards.** 350 HP, Retreat 2, Resistance to Fighting, and a Darkness Weakness the other half of this deck exists to cover.

_Binding Flame_ needs no Energy and works from the Bench; every retreat the opponent pays costs one more, and every _Phantom Maze_ hits 50 harder. **It stacks.** Two in play is plus 2 and plus 100. _Phantom Maze_ itself does 130 plus 50 for each [C] in the opponent's Active Pokémon's Retreat Cost, and [The Numbers](#the-numbers) is the full menu. In this build the lantern is the gun, not the wall. It charges on the Bench off the ferry, comes forward for a Knock Out the toll makes certain, and steps back on its Balloon. [The Gun](#3-the-gun) is the timetable.

### Gastly

The bottom of the Gengar line, and the **Phantasmal Flames print** on purpose: 70 HP against the Temporal Forces print's 60, which is the difference between surviving one Phantom Dive and dying to it. _Petty Grudge_ is a guaranteed 10 for [D]. It is Darkness, so Concealment prices it at zero against an ex. Four copies for two Megas, because the spare Gastly is the deck's best Bench body: a free target that pays nothing and becomes the next Gengar when the first one falls.

### Mega Gengar ex

**Stage 2 from Haunter, and this deck runs no Haunter, so it arrives by Rare Candy. Gives up 3 Prizes, and discounts everyone else's.** _Shadowy Concealment_ takes 1 Prize off every Knock Out an opposing Pokémon ex scores on your Darkness Pokémon: Gastly pays 0, Okidogi pays 1, and the Mega itself pays 2. The Psychic half gets no discount, which is exactly why the Dark wall stands in front.

_Void Gale_ is 230 for [D][D], then **move an Energy from this Pokémon to 1 of your Benched Pokémon**. The move is mandatory and the Energy can be any type. That sentence is the deck. Gengar holds [D][D] plus one more; you attach a Psychic to it each turn, swing for 230, and the Psychic lands on the Lampent behind it. Gengar never drops below [D][D], the lantern charges without spending a card, and the deck's one attachment a turn does two jobs. It needs the third Energy aboard once, and [The Ferry](#2-the-ferry) is how it gets there. Two copies, because 19% of games prize one and a third was a dead card in every game that did not.

### Okidogi ex

**The attacker with no Ability, and the reason the Cornerstone games are winnable.** 250 HP Darkness Basic, Retreat 3, Weak to Fighting. Two Prizes, one under Concealment.

_Poisonous Musculature_ is an attack, and **it costs one Energy of any type**; an empty Okidogi cannot use it. Pay the [C] with whatever is in hand, a Psychic included, and it searches your deck for up to 2 Basic Darkness Energy, attaches them to itself, and Poisons itself. It now holds three Energy, which is _Chain-Crazed_'s whole [D][D][C]. _Chain-Crazed_ is 130, plus 130 more while it is Poisoned, so the next turn it hits for **260** with no attachment at all, and it hits for 260 again every turn it survives. That one-shots every 210 HP Ogerpon, Raging Bolt ex, Latias ex, Fezandipiti ex, and every single-prize body on the ladder. Against Cornerstone specifically, it is the only card here that does damage at all, and Demolish's 140 ignores Weakness, so the return hit leaves it standing on 110.

The Poison is the condition, so protect it. Special Conditions clear when a Pokémon goes to the Bench, and Festival Grounds cures them outright. Use _Poisonous Musculature_ from the Active Spot, stay there, and swing next turn; play Battle Cage over Festival Grounds first. The ferry can pre-load it with a Darkness on the Bench, so the _Musculature_ turn's manual attachment goes to Gengar instead. [How To Play Okidogi](#4-how-to-play-okidogi) is the turn-by-turn. Two copies, because a one-of tech is prized one game in ten and only Dawn and Ultra Ball can find it.

# Trainers — Supporters

### Lillie's Determination

Shuffle your hand into your deck and draw 6; at exactly 6 Prizes remaining, draw 8. Three copies. This is the card that fixes the hand, and the shuffle half returns the spare Stage 2s to where Dawn and Ultra Ball can find them again. The fourth seat went to a Rosa's; if hands still clog with ghosts, [Test and Tune](#test-and-tune) has the swap.

### Dawn

Search your deck for a Basic Pokémon, a Stage 1 Pokémon, and a Stage 2 Pokémon. A whole line in one Supporter: Litwick, Lampent, and Mega Chandelure; Gastly, Lampent, and Mega Gengar; or Okidogi, Lampent, and whichever Mega the room wants. Lampent is the only Stage 1 in the deck, so the middle slot is always a Lampent. Both Megas are Stage 2 Pokémon, so Dawn and Rare Candy both see them. Three copies, down from four, because the Item search took over the turn-one job.

### Boss's Orders [Ghetsis]

Switch in 1 of your opponent's Benched Pokémon to the Active Spot. The toll only collects from what is standing in front of it, and [The Numbers](#the-numbers) is a list of who to drag there. Two copies plus Prime Catcher. Spend them on Prizes, never on tempo.

### Crispin

Search your deck for up to 2 Basic Energy of **different types**; put one in hand and attach the other to one of your Pokémon. A mono deck wastes half this card. This deck is the reason it was printed. The canonical turn is Crispin attaching a Darkness to the fresh Mega Gengar while the manual attachment puts a Psychic on it, which is the [D][D] plus one the ferry needs, in one turn. Two copies, and the deck's opening curve is a turn faster in the games that have one by turn two.

### Rosa's Encouragement

**The rebuild.** You can use this card only if you have more Prize cards remaining than your opponent; attach up to 2 Basic Energy from your discard pile to 1 of your Stage 2 Pokémon. The moment they take three off your first Mega, this is live, the dead Mega's Energy is sitting in the discard, and both Megas are Stage 2. Candy the next Gastly or evolve the next Lampent, Rosa's puts two Energy on it, and it attacks the same turn. Two copies. It is the reason the Energy count could drop to ten, and the reason Energy Recycler had to go.

### Lana's Aid

Put up to 3 in any combination of Pokémon that don't have a Rule Box and Basic Energy cards from your discard pile into your hand. Three cards for one Supporter: two Energy and the Litwick or Gastly that died with them is the whole next line. It cannot touch a Mega or an Okidogi; Night Stretcher covers those. It also shrinks the discard on purpose, because N's Darmanitan's _Back Draft_ does 30 for each Basic Energy sitting in it.

### Team Rocket's Petrel

Search your deck for a Trainer card. One copy, the wildcard: an Energy Search on a turn-one hand with no Energy, Prime Catcher on the turn the gust wins, the second Battle Cage when theirs is down, a Rosa's the turn after the first Mega falls. It is the card that makes the two-copy Trainers behave like three.

# Trainers — Items

### Buddy-Buddy Poffin

Bench up to 2 Basic Pokémon with 70 HP or less. Litwick and Gastly both qualify, so one Poffin seeds both engines on turn one. Okidogi at 250 does not; do not go looking for it with this. Two copies beside two Ultra Ball and three Dawn.

### Rare Candy

Litwick straight to Mega Chandelure, Gastly straight to Mega Gengar. Three copies serving five Stage 2s, with _Spreading Light_ covering the lantern side. The Gengar side is Candy or nothing; there is no Haunter, so a Gengar hand without a Candy is a Lillie's or a Dawn turn first. The two riders bite as always: never on your first turn, and never on a Basic that was benched this turn. The fastest Gengar in the deck is a Gastly benched turn one, a Darkness attached turn one, and Candy plus a second Darkness on turn two.

### Ultra Ball

Discard 2 other cards from your hand, then search your deck for any Pokémon. The only Item that finds a Mega or an Okidogi. The discard is not a cost here. A Psychic pitched to Ultra Ball is a Wondrous Patch target the same turn, and any Energy pitched is a Rosa's target later, so the card is half of an attachment as often as it is a search. Two copies, which is the minimum that makes Patch live before the first Knock Out.

### Energy Search

Search your deck for a Basic Energy card and put it into your hand. Two copies, new in v5.1, and the only card in the deck that finds Energy before anything has hit the discard. Every other Energy card here reads the discard pile or spends the Supporter for the turn; this one is an Item, it is live on turn one, and it takes the exact color the turn needs. Play it before you decide the attachment. Hold it on a turn the hand already has Energy rather than thinning the deck for its own sake, because the turn it matters is the one with none.

### Wondrous Patch

Attach a Basic Psychic Energy from your discard pile to 1 of your Benched Psychic Pokémon. An Item, so play as many as you hold. A Mega Chandelure that dies loaded leaves two Psychic in the discard; two Patches put both onto the Lampent beside it, the evolution finishes the Mega, and it attacks the same turn. Two copies. It reads "Benched," so it never charges the Active; charge the Bench, then bring it forward.

### Night Stretcher

Put a Pokémon or a Basic Energy card from your discard pile into your hand. Two copies, because this is the only card here that recovers a Mega or an Okidogi. A Knocked Out Mega first, a Darkness second, and an Okidogi third.

### Prime Catcher

The ACE SPEC. Their Bench to the Active Spot, and your Active to your Bench, one Item, no Supporter spent. Spend it on the turn that does both jobs: drag the Retreat 4 body into the toll booth and rotate the lantern forward to collect. It is the only gust that does not cost the Supporter, so the same turn can still play Crispin or Rosa's.

# Trainers — Tool & Stadium

### Air Balloon

The Retreat Cost of the Pokémon this is attached to is two less. Both Megas retreat for two, so a Balloon on each makes the rotation free: Gengar steps back, Chandelure steps up and collects, Chandelure steps back, Gengar resumes the ferry. Two copies, one per Mega, and attach each the turn its Mega evolves. Jamming Tower turns both off, and that is the one room that misses the Switch.

### Battle Cage

**The Stadium, and it does two jobs.** Prevent all damage counters from being placed on Benched Pokémon, both sides, by effects of attacks and Abilities from the opponent's Pokémon; damage from attacks is still taken.

The first job is Stadium removal. Any Stadium you play discards theirs, and this deck has three it needs gone: N's Castle, which turns the toll off for every N's Pokémon; Festival Grounds, which cures Okidogi's Poison; and Jamming Tower, which turns off both Balloons. Two copies because a Stadium with the same name cannot be played over itself, so the second only matters once theirs has replaced the first, which is exactly when you need it.

The second job is the one Munkidori used to do. Phantom Dive's six counters on your Bench, Froslass's Checkup counter on every Ability Pokémon, their Munkidori's three onto a Litwick, Dusknoir's thirteen onto anything: all of it stops at the Bench line while the Cage stands.

## 1. Pick the Wall

**Read the room, then lead the half the room cannot punish. The default is Gengar.**

| They attack with | Lead | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Metal (Mega Excadrill) | **Gengar ferries, Chandelure collects** | Retreat 4 is 380 for one Flame. Metal hits both halves neutral, and their 330 leaves either Mega on 20; step it back on the Balloon and let the other one collect. |
| Fighting (Mega Lucario, Lucario Hariyama, Cynthia's Garchomp) | **Chandelure only** | Gengar and Okidogi are both Weak. The lantern resists it. Attach by hand, skip the ferry, and trade carefully. |
| Darkness (N's Zoroark, Grimmsnarl Froslass) | **Gengar, then Okidogi** | Darkness doubles into every lantern. The Dark wall takes it neutral and pays 2; keep Chandelure on the Bench as a Flame until the Castle is gone. |
| Dragon (Dragapult and its Blaziken and Dusknoir builds) | **Gengar, Cage down early** | Every attacker is an ex, so Concealment is on. Dragapult retreats for 1 and takes Okidogi then Void Gale; Boss the Dusknoir when they bench one. |
| Cornerstone in the Active Spot | **Okidogi** | The only attacker here that does damage to it. 260 in one hit. |
| Single-prize grinders (Festival Lead, Slowking, Alakazam, Dhelmise, Toucannon) | **Okidogi and one Mega, reluctantly** | Both Megas pay three and the discount is blank. Okidogi one-shots every body they own for two Prizes of exposure. Build the second Mega only when the first is gone. |

**Do not build both Megas by reflex.** Every game wants the toll engine on the Bench, because a Flame works from there; only some games want the lantern to attack at all. Two Megas exposed at once is six Prizes standing in the open.

## 2. The Ferry

**How one attachment a turn feeds two Megas.**

_Void Gale_ must move an Energy off Gengar after it hits, and it moves any Energy. So Gengar wants [D][D] plus one more aboard before its first swing, and after that every manual attachment goes onto Gengar and rides the ferry to the Bench.

1. Turn one, Poffin for Litwick and Gastly. Attach a Darkness to the Gastly; Energy Search finds it if the hand has none.
2. Turn two, Rare Candy the Gastly into Mega Gengar; the Darkness rides up. Crispin attaches the second Darkness and puts a Psychic in hand; the manual attachment puts that Psychic on Gengar. Balloon on. _Void Gale_ for 230, and the Psychic ferries onto the Litwick benched on turn one.
3. Turn three, attach a Psychic to Gengar. _Void Gale_ again; the second Psychic lands beside the first. Rare Candy the Litwick, and Mega Chandelure is standing on the Bench with [P][P] while Gengar has hit for 460.
4. Turn four, Boss's Orders or Prime Catcher drags the heavy body forward. Gengar retreats for free, the lantern steps up, and _Phantom Maze_ collects.

**Without Crispin, the first ferry carries Darkness.** Gengar at exactly [D][D] still swings, but the Energy it ships is a Darkness, so aim it at Okidogi or the spare Gastly, both of which want one. The lantern then charges by hand on a turn Gengar does not attack, by Patch, or by Rosa's after the first Knock Out. Slower, and the deck still functions.

**The rhythm to internalize.** The attachment goes on Gengar. Gengar decides where it lands. The Bench never waits on a card.

## 3. The Gun

**When the lantern comes forward, and when it goes back.**

The lantern attacks when three things are true: it has [P][P], the toll table says the shot kills, and a gust or a free retreat gets it into the Active Spot this turn. Two Flames plus a gust reaches every Retreat 2 body at 330 and every Retreat 3 body at 380; check [The Numbers](#the-numbers) before you commit.

After the Maze, they hit the lantern back. It is 350 HP and pays three Prizes, so do not leave it there to be two-shot. Its Balloon makes the retreat free: step back, promote Gengar, and the ferry resumes while the lantern waits for the next kill. A lantern that stays in front is a lantern volunteering half their game.

If the Maze lands short, the closers are Okidogi's 260 and Void Gale's 230, in that order.

## 4. How To Play Okidogi

**The turn-by-turn for a card you have never run.**

**Get it down.** Okidogi is 250 HP, so Poffin cannot fetch it. It reaches the board three ways: drawn and played, Dawn's Basic slot, or Ultra Ball. On the Bench it is a two-Prize body that pays one under Concealment; do not bench it early into a room that snipes.

**Load it in front, with one Energy already on it.** _Poisonous Musculature_ costs [C], so Okidogi needs one Energy of any type before it can use the attack. Attach it this turn if the ferry has not already dropped a Darkness on it; a spare Psychic is fine. Then _Musculature_: two Darkness from the deck onto Okidogi, and it is Poisoned. It now holds three Energy, which is the whole cost of _Chain-Crazed_. Poison ticks 10 at every Checkup, theirs and yours, so it stands on 230 by your next turn. If the ferry already fed it, this turn's manual attachment goes to Gengar or a Lampent instead.

**Keep it there.** The Poison is the condition. Special Conditions clear the moment a Pokémon goes to the Bench, so never retreat it, never Prime Catcher your own Okidogi out, and never leave Festival Grounds standing when Okidogi is loaded; the Grounds cures every energized Pokémon. Their Boss's Orders or Prime Catcher pulling one of your Benched Pokémon forward benches Okidogi too, and that one you cannot prevent. Battle Cage first, Okidogi second.

**Fire, and keep firing.** _Chain-Crazed_ for 260, with no attachment; the Energy is already aboard. Say the target's HP out loud first; anything at or under 260 dies now, anything at or under 490 dies to Void Gale next turn. If Okidogi survives the return hit, it hits 260 again next turn for nothing. Loaded and Poisoned, it is the cheapest repeating damage in the deck, so the manual attachment never goes to it again.

**Let it die, or Janine's it.** Under Concealment it pays one Prize and takes three Darkness to the discard, where Rosa's and Stretcher want them. If it survives and gets benched, the Poison is gone and the next _Chain-Crazed_ is 130; Janine's Secret Art re-poisons an Active it feeds, and that is the card [Test and Tune](#test-and-tune) offers if the Okidogi turns keep breaking.

| Turn shape | Okidogi or a Mega? |
| :--- | :--- |
| Cornerstone is Active | **Okidogi, only.** 260 into 210. |
| Target is 260 or less and a Mega is not loaded | **Okidogi.** Two Prizes of exposure against a three-Prize Mega. |
| Target retreats for 3 or 4 | **The lantern.** The toll is 380 for free. |
| Single-prize room | **Okidogi first, always.** It pays two; a Mega pays three for the same kill. |
| Fighting room | **Neither.** Okidogi takes double. The lantern's turn. |
| Festival Grounds is down | **Cage first.** Then Okidogi. |

## 5. The Rebuild

**The first Mega will die. This is the turn after.**

1. Their attack Knocks it Out. Two or three Energy go to your discard, and you now have more Prizes remaining than they do.
2. Your turn. Rosa's Encouragement onto the next Stage 2: the second Gengar you Candied from the spare Gastly, or a Lampent you evolve this turn. Two Energy from the discard, one card.
3. If the dead Mega was the lantern, Wondrous Patch instead: two Psychic onto the Benched Lampent, evolve, and it Mazes the same turn.
4. Bring it forward with Prime Catcher or a Balloon retreat and collect.

Night Stretcher returns the Mega card itself when the third copy is prized. Lana's Aid rebuilds the line under the next Mega. Wally's Compassion was the other shape of this plan in v5, healing a wounded lantern before it died at all; it left because its Energy comes back to hand, and a Gengar with its Energy in hand is a ferry that has stopped.

**Step one, do you have a Basic?** At 10 Basics you mulligan about one game in four; dig accordingly.

| **Litwick Active and a Psychic** | Attach. Going second, _Will-O-Wisp_ for 20. |

| **Ultra Ball and two Psychic** | Pitch the Psychic you cannot attach this turn. It is a Patch away from the Bench. |

**The first attachment defaults Darkness, onto the Gastly.** The ferry starts on turn two, and everything else waits for it.

## Versus the Card Shop

**Free retreat is the off switch, and two cards flip it.** "No Retreat Cost" overrides every increase, so _Phantom Maze_ floors at 130 against anything it covers. Latias ex's _Skyliner_ does it for their Basics; Boss's Orders the Latias itself, 210 HP at Retreat 2, one Flame, 280, gone. **N's Castle** does it for every N's Pokémon, and it is a Stadium, so the answer is a Stadium: Battle Cage discards it, and N's Zoroark ex at Retreat 2 pays 280 to one Flame, which is its whole HP. Until the Castle is gone the closers that never cared about retreat carry the game: Void Gale's flat 230 and Okidogi's 260.

**Back Draft reads your discard.** N's Darmanitan's _Back Draft_ does 30 for each Basic Energy in your discard pile, and N's Zoroark ex copies it. Six Energy in the discard is 180, and 360 into a Dark-weak lantern. Against the N's room the discard is a resource to keep empty: Rosa's takes two out, Patch takes one, Lana's takes three, and Ultra Ball should pitch Pokémon rather than Energy. Gengar, which takes Darkness neutral, stands in front.

**Froslass and Munkidori.** _Freezing Shroud_ puts a counter on every Pokémon with an Ability at each Checkup, both sides, and their Munkidori moves three of those a turn onto whatever you benched. Battle Cage stops both at your Bench line. Your Active still takes them; both Megas have Abilities, Okidogi does not.

**Ability lock hurts once, not twice.** Iron Thorns ex's _Initialization_ shuts off every Rule Box Ability while it is Active: Concealment and Binding Flame go dark together, and the Maze falls back to 130 plus printed Retreat. Okidogi's attacks are attacks, not Abilities, so it is the attacker for those turns.

**Festival Grounds** cures and prevents Special Conditions on every Pokémon with Energy attached, which is Okidogi's Poison. It is the Stadium the Festival Lead room runs by name. Cage over it before the _Musculature_ turn, and hold the second Cage for their replacement.

**Jamming Tower** turns off every Tool, which here is both Balloons, and the Mega rotation costs two Energy a step until a Cage removes it. Prime Catcher is the one rotation that does not care.
