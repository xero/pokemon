# Gengar Gang: Guard Dogs

### The 60 where Gengar stopped being support, rebuilt around the 30th Celebration Chaotic Pain

> [!NOTE]
> **What this file is for.**
>
> This is Gengar Gang after the 30th Celebration set landed, tuned for the [TCG Live](https://tcg.pokemon.com/en-us/tcgl/) ladder and legal to sleeve for league night once the set clears its two week window. Every card carries regulation mark H, I, or J. There is no sideboard, so the sixty answer the whole field on their own.

## The Thesis

Every Pokémon in this deck is Weak to Fighting, and Mega Lucario ex's *Aura Jab* costs one Energy and does 130, which Weakness doubles to 260. That kills Okidogi ex, Toxtricity, the plain Gengar, Haunter, and Gastly outright. There is no Weakness answer printed in Standard. The deck stopped trying to survive and started making the deaths worthless instead.

**Mega Gengar ex's *Shadowy Concealment* charges the opponent one Prize for every Darkness Pokémon they knock out with a Pokémon ex.** Most of the format attacks with ex Pokémon, so the tax applies to most of the attacks anyone makes against you.

| Your Pokémon | Prizes normally | Prizes under Concealment |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Mega Gengar ex | 3 | **2** |
| Okidogi ex, Gengar ex | 2 | **1** |
| Gastly, Haunter, Gengar, Toxel, Toxtricity | 1 | **0** |

**Thirteen of the twenty-two Pokémon in this deck are worth nothing** to the person attacking them. A board holding one Mega Gengar ex, two Okidogi ex, and three of anything else pays out four Prizes total. They need six.

That half is unchanged. What changed is the other half, because the deck now has an attacker that cannot be walled.

***Chaotic Pain* places 13 damage counters on 1 of your opponent's Pokémon.** Placing counters is not dealing damage, and that single distinction is the whole card. It ignores Weakness and Resistance in both directions, it reaches any Pokémon on the board rather than only the Active, and it goes straight through every "prevent all damage" effect printed in the format. A sweep of the legal pool turns up five cards in all of Standard that actually stop it, and four of them are fringe.

The result is a deck with three prices for damage and a wall-breaker at the top of the curve.

| Attacker | Cost | Output | Prizes it pays |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Gengar, *Mind Jack* | **[D]** | 10 plus 30 per Benched Pokémon, so 130 to 160 | **0** |
| Gengar ex, *Chaotic Pain* | **[D][D]** | 130 as counters, any target, through any wall | **1** |
| Mega Gengar ex, *Void Gale* | [D][D] | 230, and **460** into a Psychic Pokémon | 2 |
| Okidogi ex, *Chain-Crazed* | [D][D][C] | 260 while Poisoned | **1** |

Okidogi is still the fastest clock in the deck and still the turn-one play going second. It is no longer the only plan.

# Pokémon

### Okidogi ex

**250 HP Basic, Fighting Weakness, Retreat 3.** It cannot run away, it folds to one Fighting Energy, and it gives up two Prizes. Concealment answers the third. Nothing answers the first two, so the deck stops trying.

***Chain-Crazed* does 130, and 130 more if this Pokémon is Poisoned**, so 260. That number is lower than this page used to quote, because Binding Mochi came out of the list when Gengar took over as the attacker; [Test and Tune](#test-and-tune) has the Mochi back if 300 turns out to be the number you miss.

*Poisonous Musculature* costs one Colorless, searches two Basic Darkness Energy out of the deck, attaches them here, and Poisons this Pokémon. Going second, that is a real turn-one attack: one Energy pays for it, and the dog ends the turn holding three and already Poisoned. Turn two it swings 260.

**Poison persists.** Once this dog is Poisoned it stays Poisoned as long as it stays Active, so Chain-Crazed keeps hitting 260 every turn with no further help. You Poison each Okidogi once, not once per turn. The cost is ten damage at every Checkup, which puts a clock on how long a copy lives. The other thing that ends the Poison is leaving the Active Spot, and every deck at the shop runs a gust; [game plan 4](#4-when-the-dog-gets-gusted) is the way back.

Four copies. The plan is for this card to die and be replaced, and it carries the Basic count that holds the mulligan rate down.

### Gastly

70 HP keeps it inside Buddy-Buddy Poffin range, and it is Darkness, so Risky Ruins never touches it and Janine can attach to it.

**Four copies, up from three, and it is the single highest-leverage count in the deck.** Seven Stage 2 cards sit on top of this one Basic. Every Gengar you build spends a Gastly, the plain Gengar spends another one every time it comes back, and Gwynn eats the spares for cards. Sacred Ash is what makes that sustainable, but only a Gastly in the deck can be searched, so the deck wants as many as the rules allow.

### Haunter

**Three copies, up from two.** The non-Candy route to a Stage 2, and the reason the count went up is an Energy trick rather than an insurance policy. *Sinister Surge* attaches to a **Benched** Darkness Pokémon, and a benched Haunter is a legal target, so you can load a Haunter with two Energy and then evolve it into a Gengar ex that arrives already able to attack. Rare Candy skips a turn; a fed Haunter skips an Energy attachment. The deck wants both routes.

Nothing here wants to attack with a Haunter.

### Mega Gengar ex

**Stage 2 from Haunter. 350 HP. Gives up 3 Prizes, or 2 once its own Ability applies to it.**

***Shadowy Concealment* is the spine of the deck.** Any Darkness Pokémon of yours knocked out by an attack from an opponent's Pokémon ex costs that player one Prize. It covers your whole board including this card, **and it works from the Bench**, which is how it should usually be played. A 350 HP body on the Bench is very hard to remove, and the Ability does not care whether it ever attacks.

**The effect does not stack, so run one on the board and never two.** A second copy in play is two more Prizes sitting in the open for no extra benefit. The second copy in the deck exists for when the first one is prized or dead.

Two copies rather than three, because the third became the third Gengar ex. At two copies one is prized in roughly a fifth of games and both in about one in forty, and Dawn finds the spare as its Stage 2.

*Void Gale* does 230 and moves an Energy from this Pokémon to a Benched Pokémon. That clause reads like a drawback and works like a ferry: the Energy lands on whatever you are arming next. **Against Psychic Pokémon, Weakness doubles Void Gale to 460**, and it is the only attack in the deck that gets that, because Chaotic Pain places counters and counters ignore Weakness.

### Gengar ex

**Stage 2 from Haunter, 280 HP, Retreat 2. 30th Celebration 090, regulation mark J.**

***Chaotic Pain* costs [D][D] and places 13 damage counters on 1 of your opponent's Pokémon.** Any Pokémon. Active or Benched, your choice.

**Counters are not damage, and everything good about this card follows from that.** A sweep of the entire Standard pool for bench protection comes back almost unanimous: the text reads *prevent all **damage***, and damage counters walk through all of it.

- **Every Tera rule.** Dragapult ex, Cornerstone, all five Ogerpons, every Eeveelution ex. Their Bench immunity is worded as damage.
- **Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon ex.** *Cornerstone Stance* prevents damage from Pokémon that have an Ability. Gengar has one and does not care.
- **Crustle's *Mysterious Rock Inn*.** Prevents all damage from attacks by your opponent's Pokémon ex. Okidogi ex, Mega Gengar ex, and this card are all ex, so Crustle walls the rest of the deck completely. Chaotic Pain is the only thing here that touches it.
- **Drednaw's *Impervious Shell*** and **Pikachu ex's *Resolute Heart***, both keyed to damage from an attack.

Five cards in Standard actually stop it: **Battle Cage**, **Rabsca**, **Poltchageist** and **Sinistcha**, **Skeledirge**, and **Misty's Magikarp**. Of those, only Battle Cage is common, and [it is a partial answer, not a wall](#risky-ruins).

130 is also exactly the right size against the current field, because it one-shots the support layer **on the Bench**, where it normally cannot be reached at all.

| Target | HP | |
| :--- | ---: | :--- |
| Budew | 30 | the Item lock |
| Dreepy | 70 | |
| Drakloak | 90 | the card that draws their deck |
| Dusclops | 90 | |
| Munkidori | 110 | |
| Slowking | 120 | |
| Alakazam | 140 | with 20 from Risky Ruins |
| Crustle | 150 | with 20 from Risky Ruins |

***Fainting Spell*: if this Pokémon is Knocked Out by damage from an attack, flip a coin; if heads, the Attacking Pokémon is Knocked Out too.** On a 280 HP body that pays only one Prize under Concealment, that is the best trade in the deck. They spend a turn and an attacker, they collect one Prize, and half the time their attacker dies with it.

Three copies, because this is the card that attacks and therefore the card that dies.

### Gengar

**Stage 2 from Haunter, 130 HP, no Rule Box.** The wildcard, and the cheapest damage in the deck.

***Mind Jack* costs one Darkness Energy and does 10, plus 30 for each Pokémon on your opponent's Bench.**

| Their Bench | Mind Jack |
| ---: | ---: |
| 3 | 100 |
| 4 | 130 |
| 5 | 160 |
| 8, under Area Zero Underdepths | 250 |

The format fills Benches, so 130 to 160 is the usual number for a single Energy, from a body that pays **zero Prizes** under Concealment. It is also the attacker for the two rooms where the rest of the deck struggles: a single-prize opponent, where Concealment is a blank card, and Neutralization Zone, where your ex Pokémon cannot damage anything without a Rule Box.

***Infinite Shadow*: if an attack knocks it out, it goes to your hand instead of the discard.** Everything under it is discarded, so each rebuild spends a Gastly, but the Gengar itself never runs out. One copy, because it comes back on its own and Sacred Ash returns the Gastly it burned.

**Mind Jack and Chaotic Pain pull against each other.** Sniping their Bench shrinks the Bench that Mind Jack counts. Pick a lane per turn rather than alternating.

### Toxel

*Call for Family* searches two Basic Pokémon out of the deck and benches them for one Darkness Energy. That is a Buddy-Buddy Poffin on a stick, and it reaches Okidogi ex, which Poffin cannot at 250 HP. The two share the setup, which is why the deck runs two Poffin rather than four.

Three copies. It is the turn-one attack going second when no dog showed up, it holds the Basic count, and it is a zero-Prize body on the Bench.

### Toxtricity

***Sinister Surge* attaches a Basic Darkness Energy from the deck to a Benched Darkness Pokémon once a turn**, and puts two damage counters on it. It is an Ability, which is the whole argument for it: it accelerates on turns when your Supporter is busy being Lillie's Determination or Dawn.

Point it at the body you plan to promote next. Two damage counters on something that dies for zero or one Prize is a fine price for arriving pre-loaded. A benched Haunter is a legal target, and that is the fastest Gengar in the deck.

Two copies, because each copy's Surge is its own once-per-turn and the card carries no "you can't use more than one" clause. *Gentle Slap* does 100 for three, and it is the emergency attack when nothing else is armed.

# Trainers — Supporters

### Lillie's Determination

Shuffle your hand into your deck and draw six, or eight while you still hold all six Prizes. Three copies. It is the card that rescues a dead hand, and it is the reason the draw count is survivable at all.

The cost is real in a deck that plans two turns ahead: Lillie's throws away the pieces you were holding for next turn along with the dead ones. [Gwynn](#gwynn) is the half of the draw package that does not.

### Dawn

**Search your deck for a Basic Pokémon, a Stage 1 Pokémon, and a Stage 2 Pokémon.** In this deck that is Gastly, Haunter, and whichever Gengar the matchup wants, or Toxel, Toxtricity, and a Gengar. One card sets up an entire line, and it is the only card that finds a Gengar ex at all, because Poké Pad and Gwynn both read "no Rule Box".

Three copies, up from two. Seven Stage 2 cards on one Basic line makes this the most important Supporter in the deck, and it is the turn-two play in almost every game.

### Janine's Secret Art

Choose up to two of your Darkness Pokémon, search a Basic Darkness Energy out of the deck for each, attach them, and if one of them was your Active, it is now Poisoned.

**One Energy per Pokémon, two different Pokémon, never two on one.** The Live client plays it that way and a judge confirmed it. So Janine can only ever put a single Energy on your Active; a manual attachment plus Janine is two, and Chain-Crazed costs three.

**It is the only card in the deck that re-Poisons a dog.** That is its real job, not acceleration. Poison ends the moment Okidogi leaves the Active Spot, so a gust costs you 130 damage until the Poison is back, and *Poisonous Musculature* can only restore it by spending your attack for the turn. Janine restores it and attacks the same turn.

Two copies. Nothing else in Standard Poisons your own Pokémon except Okidogi's own attack and Pecharunt ex, and Pecharunt was tried and cut.

### Gwynn

**Discard up to 2 Pokémon that don't have a Rule Box from your hand, and draw 3 cards for each one discarded.** Six cards for two cards, and you choose which two.

The cards it eats are the ones that are genuinely dead in hand: the fourth Gastly, a spare Toxel, the third Haunter. It cannot touch Okidogi ex or either ex Gengar, so it never takes a card you needed. Unlike Lillie's it leaves the rest of your hand alone, which matters if you are holding a Rare Candy for a Gengar two turns out.

**Sacred Ash is what makes it nearly free.** Gwynn buries line pieces, Ash shuffles five of them back into the deck, and Dawn and Poffin find them again. Two copies, and the pair with Ash is the reason the deck can afford a draw Supporter that costs Pokémon.

### Boss's Orders

Drags a Benched Pokémon into the Active Spot.

**One copy, down from two, because Chaotic Pain does most of this job for free.** The classic reason to gust is to kill a support Pokémon hiding on their Bench, and Gengar now kills it where it stands. What is left for Boss's is the case Chaotic Pain cannot cover: dragging a damaged body up to take the last Prize, or stranding an attacker that just got loaded.

A single copy is thin on its own. Petrel is the second copy, and Prime Catcher is the third.

### Team Rocket's Petrel

**Search your deck for a Trainer card.** One copy, and it is worth more than its count suggests, because **every one-of in this deck becomes a virtual two-of**.

| Holding | Alone | With Petrel |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Boss's Orders, opening seven | 11.7% | **22.1%** |
| Boss's Orders, by turn eight | 25.0% | **44.1%** |

It finds the Boss's Orders, the Prime Catcher, the Energy Switch, a Rare Candy, an Energy Recycler, and above all **a Risky Ruins when they land a Battle Cage**, which is the one stadium that turns Chaotic Pain off at the Bench.

The cost is honest. Petrel is a Supporter, so a turn spent tutoring is a turn not spent drawing or re-Poisoning. Play it on setup turns.

# Trainers — Items

### Rare Candy

Four copies. Seven Stage 2 cards over four Gastly and three Haunter, and Candy is the route that gets a Gengar down a turn early. Two timing rules decide openings: never on your first turn, and never on a Basic that entered play this turn, so the fastest Gengar is turn two off a Gastly benched on turn one.

### Buddy-Buddy Poffin

**Two Basic Pokémon with 70 HP or less, straight to the Bench.** Gastly and Toxel are the only two in the deck, seven targets for two copies, and it is the turn-one play going first when Toxel cannot attack.

Poffin cannot reach Okidogi at 250 HP. Toxel's *Call for Family* can. The two share the setup, which is why the count is two and not the four every other list runs.

### Sacred Ash

**Shuffle up to 5 Pokémon from your discard pile into your deck.** Two copies.

**Recovery has to land where the searchers look.** Dawn, Buddy-Buddy Poffin, and Rare Candy all read the **deck**. Night Stretcher returns a card to your **hand**. Sacred Ash is the only card that refills the pool the search engine actually fishes in, and in a deck whose plan is to trade bodies, that pool drains fast.

It does three jobs at once. It returns dead Gastly and Haunter so the line never runs out, it returns a knocked-out Gengar ex so Dawn can find it again, and it pays for [Gwynn](#gwynn).

> [!TIP]
> **Buy the Destined Rivals 168 print.** Sacred Ash is also in Perfect Order at 115, but that set's printed total is 88, so 115 is a secret-rare print at collector prices. Same card, same text, and DRI 168 is inside its set's printed total of 182.

### Energy Recycler

**Shuffle up to 5 Basic Energy cards from your discard pile into your deck.** The Energy half of the same principle, and the reason it is not optional:

| Engine | Where it looks |
| :--- | :--- |
| *Sinister Surge* | **deck** |
| *Janine's Secret Art* | **deck** |
| *Poisonous Musculature* | **deck**, two at a time |
| Night Stretcher | discard, to hand |

All three accelerators search the deck, and every attacker that dies buries two or three Darkness Energy in the discard. Okidogi is the worst offender in both directions, because *Poisonous Musculature* pulls two out of the deck every time it fires.

| | Buried | Left in the deck |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Start, 10 Energy less about one prized | 0 | 9 |
| Okidogi #1 dies holding three | 3 | 6 |
| Okidogi #2 dies holding three | 6 | 3 |
| Okidogi #3 dies holding three | 9 | **0, and Janine whiffs** |

Two copies takes the deck from three fully armed attackers in a game to six.

### Night Stretcher

A Pokémon or a Basic Energy card out of the discard pile and into your hand. **One copy**, and the count is deliberate now that Sacred Ash and Energy Recycler cover the deck side.

What is left is the half neither of them can do, which is **speed**. Ash and Recycler put things back in the deck, where you still have to find them. Night Stretcher puts one thing in your hand this turn, and the turn that matters is the one after a dog dies holding three Energy.

### Energy Switch

Moves a Basic Energy from one of your Pokémon to another. One copy.

*Sinister Surge* only ever reaches the Bench, so this is the card that brings that Energy up to the Active that needs it. It is also the third Energy on a fresh Okidogi: Janine puts one on the Active and one on the Bench, a manual attachment makes two, and Energy Switch brings the last one up for Chain-Crazed. Petrel finds it when the turn needs it.

### Prime Catcher

**The ACE SPEC.** It gusts a Benched Pokémon up **and** switches your own Active for free, which is two jobs in one card and the reason plain Switch is not in the list.

The old list ran Switch to bring a gusted dog back. That turned out to be paying a card to avoid something the deck gets paid for: when they gust a Gastly up, the Gastly dies for **zero Prizes** under Concealment and you promote the dog for free on the following turn. Prime Catcher covers the case where you cannot wait a turn, and it drags something of theirs up while it does it.

# Trainers — Tool & Stadium

### Risky Ruins

**Two damage counters on every Basic non-Darkness Pokémon either player benches.** Every Pokémon in this deck is Darkness, so it is completely one-sided.

**Three copies, and the count is about the stadium war rather than the damage.** Battle Cage is the one common card that stops Chaotic Pain from reaching their Bench, and the only way to remove a Stadium is to play one over it.

| Risky Ruins copies | Opening 7 | By turn 3 | By turn 8 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 22.1% | 30.8% | 44.1% |
| **3** | **31.5%** | **42.7%** | **58.5%** |

The 20 damage matters too, and it stays on the card when it evolves. Twenty on their Dreepy carries forward onto the Dragapult ex it becomes. Twenty on an Abra or a Dwebble is what turns Chaotic Pain's 130 into a kill on Alakazam at 140 and Crustle at 150.

> [!NOTE]
> **Battle Cage is a tax, not a wall.** It reads *prevent all damage counters from being placed on **Benched** Pokémon*. Chaotic Pain still does 130 to their Active straight through it. When they land one you lose the sniping, not the card, and their Cage also shuts off Dragapult's *Phantom Dive* counters on your own Bench. Sometimes the right play is to leave it alone.

**A Stadium with the same name cannot be played over itself,** so your own Ruins is dead in hand while your own Ruins is in play. That is fine; the copies are there for the war, not for stacking.

## The Prize Tax

Concealment is worth doing the arithmetic on, because it changes how you build your Bench.

| Board | What it pays them |
| --- | ---: |
| Mega Gengar ex, Okidogi ex, Gengar ex, Toxtricity, Gastly | 2 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 0 = **4** |
| Mega Gengar ex, Okidogi ex, Okidogi ex, Gengar ex, Toxtricity | 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 0 = **5** |
| Two Mega Gengar ex, Okidogi ex, Gengar ex, Gengar ex | 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 = **7** |

**Every Pokémon ex you bench is a hole in the tax.** Keep one Mega Gengar ex on the board, at most two other ex, and fill the rest with Gastly, Haunter, Toxel, Toxtricity, and the plain Gengar. The deck runs thirteen of those zero-Prize bodies, so the seats fill themselves.

*Fainting Spell* stacks with this in a way that is easy to underrate. A Gengar ex dying to an ex attacker pays **one** Prize and then flips for the attacker's life. Trading a one-Prize body for a coin flip at their three-Prize Mega is the best exchange rate the deck offers.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Concealment only covers attacks from Pokémon ex.** A single-prize attacker like Slowking bypasses the tax completely, and so does damage from an Ability or from Poison. Alakazam's *Powerful Hand* places damage counters rather than dealing damage, so it skips the tax even though nothing about it is an ex. Against decks that win with non-ex attackers you are a midrange deck with a Fighting Weakness, and you should expect a race. That is the room the plain Gengar was kept for.

## The Energy Engine

Four sources put Darkness Energy on the board, and only one of them uses your hand.

| Source | From | To | Costs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Manual attachment | hand | anywhere | your attachment for the turn |
| *Sinister Surge* | deck | a **Benched** Darkness Pokémon, once per Toxtricity | nothing, it is an Ability |
| *Janine's Secret Art* | deck | up to 2 Darkness Pokémon, one Energy each, Poisons your Active | your Supporter |
| *Poisonous Musculature* | deck | Okidogi ex only, Poisons it | your attack |

Chaotic Pain and Void Gale both cost two, which the engine reaches in a single turn. Chain-Crazed costs three, which needs two sources in one turn or a body that came up pre-loaded. Energy Switch is the connective tissue, because Surge only ever delivers to the Bench.

**Feed the Haunter.** Surging onto a benched Haunter and then evolving it is the cheapest Gengar in the deck, because the Energy is already aboard when the Stage 2 arrives.

## The Recursion Loop

The deck spends Pokémon on purpose, so it carries two cards that put them back, and they land in different places for different reasons.

| Card | Returns | To | Use it for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sacred Ash | up to 5 Pokémon | **deck** | refilling what Dawn, Poffin, and Candy search |
| Night Stretcher | 1 Pokémon or Basic Energy | **hand** | the thing you need this turn |
| Energy Recycler | up to 5 Basic Energy | **deck** | refilling what Surge, Janine, and Musculature search |

The loop that carries long games is **Gwynn, Sacred Ash, Dawn**. Gwynn turns two dead line pieces into six cards, Ash puts five Pokémon back where the searchers can reach them, and Dawn pulls a whole line back out. Nothing in the deck is ever truly spent except the Energy in the discard, and Recycler answers that.

# Game Plans

## 1. Turn one, going second

You can attack on your first turn when you go second, and the deck has two openers.

**With a dog and an Energy**, which is 27.9% of opening hands: attach to an Active Okidogi ex and use *Poisonous Musculature*. It searches two more Darkness onto the dog and Poisons it, so the turn ends with three Energy aboard and the Poison already set. Turn two is Chain-Crazed for 260 with no further help.

**With a Toxel instead**: *Call for Family* for one Energy benches two Basics out of the deck, which is how an Okidogi that never showed up arrives. Bench a Gastly with it so Rare Candy is live next turn.

Either way, bench a Gastly on turn one. Candy cannot be played on a Basic that arrived the same turn, so turn one's Gastly is turn two's Gengar.

## 2. Rearming after a knockout

Your attacker dies. Promote the next one and get it swinging the same turn.

**For a Gengar ex**, which only needs two: promote it, attach by hand, and Surge from a Toxtricity, or promote one that a Toxtricity already fed while it was a benched Haunter. Chaotic Pain does not care about Poison, Weakness, or what is in front of it.

**For an Okidogi**: promote, attach by hand, play Janine to put one Energy on it plus one on a benched body, then Energy Switch that one up. Three Energy and Poisoned, swinging 260 on the turn it arrives. *Poisonous Musculature* can never do this, because using it means not attacking.

## 3. Which Gengar to build

The line builds two Gengars in a good game and one in most. Three at once happens in about 7% of games by turn eight, so treat it as a bonus rather than a plan; the Bench has six seats and three Gengars plus a Toxtricity and a spare Gastly fills five of them.

| They are playing | Build | Because |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anything with Pokémon ex attackers | **Mega Gengar ex first** | the tax. One on the Bench, never two, and it does not need to attack |
| Support Pokémon on the Bench, or any wall | **Gengar ex** | 130 counters reach anything and go through everything |
| Psychic attackers | **Mega Gengar ex, and attack with it** | Void Gale doubles to 460 |
| A single-prize deck, or Neutralization Zone | **Gengar** | Concealment is blank, and a one-Energy 160 from a zero-Prize body wins that race |

Mega Gengar on the Bench and a Gengar ex in front of it is the default board. Build the Mega first when they are an ex deck, because it is the card that makes everything else cheap.

## 4. When the dog gets gusted

They gust a Gastly into your Active Spot. The dog is on the Bench with its three Energy and without its Poison, and it costs three to retreat.

**Usually, do nothing.** The Gastly dies for zero Prizes under Concealment, and you promote the dog for free on the next turn having lost nothing but the Poison. This is the trade the deck is built to win, and it is why plain Switch is not in the list.

**When you cannot wait**, Prime Catcher swaps your Active for free and drags something of theirs up at the same time. Then Janine to restore the Poison, and Chain-Crazed for 260.

If they gust the Mega instead, do not retreat it. Void Gale for 230, and the Energy it moves goes to whatever you are arming next.

## 5. Choosing the stadium

You run three Risky Ruins and Petrel finds a fourth, so the question is usually when to play one rather than which.

- **Lead Risky Ruins against Dragapult.** Twenty on every Dreepy carries onto the Dragapult ex it becomes, and once your Ruins is down, theirs are dead for the game.
- **Hold one for their Battle Cage.** The Cage is the only common card that stops Chaotic Pain reaching their Bench. Bump it the turn you need the snipe, not the turn it lands.
- **Leave their Battle Cage alone when your own Bench is what is dying.** It stops *Phantom Dive* counters on your side too.
- **Against Festival Grounds, play any of them.** The job is being a Stadium you can drop on top of theirs so Janine can Poison the dog again.
- **Against Neutralization Zone, bump it once.** It is an ACE SPEC and cannot come back from the discard.

## 6. Bench discipline

Read [The Prize Tax](#the-prize-tax) and keep the board's payout at four. One Mega Gengar ex, never two. At most two other Pokémon ex. Everything else should be a zero.

The Bench holds five, and a spare Gastly is worth a seat in almost every game, because it is next turn's Gengar.

## Versus the Card Shop

Worlds 2026 was more than a third Dragapult, with Basic Box, Alakazam, N's Zoroark, and Slowking behind it. The ladder runs a little more Festival Lead and a little more Fighting.

| Dragapult ex, 36% | 320 HP that takes no damage on their Bench, six counters onto yours every turn, three Boss's Orders, and four Crushing Hammer | **Chaotic Pain ignores the Tera rule**, so their Drakloak dies on the Bench for two Energy. Risky Ruins first so Dreepy carries 20. Recycler for the Hammers |

| Alakazam, 9% | *Powerful Hand* places two counters per card in their hand, from a Stage 2 with no Rule Box | 140 HP, so Ruins plus one Chaotic Pain kills it on the Bench before it ever attacks. Concealment is blank here, so race it |
| N's Zoroark ex, 8% | 280 HP and two extra cards a turn | Chain-Crazed 260 plus 20 from Ruins is exactly 280. Two Chaotic Pains also gets there |
| Slowking, 6% | a 120 HP single-prize attacker copying whatever it discards | One Chaotic Pain kills it anywhere on the board. Tax the ex behind it |
| Mega Excadrill ex, 5% | 340 HP, and *Maximum Drilling* hits for 330 off five Energy | Too big for one hit. Chaotic Pain the Drilbur before it evolves, or trade one-Prize bodies into it and let Fainting Spell flip |
| Festival Lead, 4% | single-prize Grass with three Festival Grounds and a Shaymin, so the dog cannot be Poisoned and the tax is blank | Chaotic Pain and Void Gale both work without Poison. Shaymin's *Flower Curtain* prevents damage, so counters go through it |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Festival Grounds turns the dog off.** Any Pokémon with an Energy attached cannot be affected by Special Conditions, so Okidogi can never be Poisoned and Chain-Crazed is stuck at 130. Three Ruins plus Petrel is the answer, and both Gengar attacks never needed the Poison in the first place.
