## The Thesis

Mega Zygarde ex is a Basic with 310 HP that hits for 200 and takes 30 less damage on the way back. The catch is the price. *Gaia Wave* costs three Energy, *Geobuster* costs four, and *Nullifying Zero* costs five, so a deck that attaches one Energy a turn throws its first real punch on turn three.

**Cinderace makes it turn two.** It is a Stage 2 with no Basic anywhere in the deck, and it never needs one: *Explosiveness* lets it start the game face down in the Active Spot. Going second, its *Turbo Flare* hits for 50 on turn one and pulls 3 Basic Energy out of the deck onto the Bench. With the hand attachment, that is 4 Energy on a benched Zygarde before the opponent has taken a second turn. Cinderace has Retreat 0, so on turn two it walks off for free and the loaded Zygarde steps in.

| Attack | Cost | Damage | With Premium Power Pro | One-shots |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | ---: | :--- |
| *Gaia Wave* | [F][F][F] | 200 | 230 | anything Fighting-weak up to 460, which is every Darkness Pokémon in Gengar Gang |
| *Geobuster*, with [Core Memory](#core-memory) | [F][F][F][F] | 350 | 380 | Dragapult ex 320, Cynthia's Garchomp ex 330, Mega Excadrill ex 340 |
| *Nullifying Zero* | [F][F][F][F][F] | 150 per heads | 180 on the Active | a coin for every Pokémon they have in play |

**Gaia Wave is the attack Fox makes most turns.** It keeps its Energy, it leaves Zygarde taking 30 less, and a Zygarde with three Energy still attached is one [Jumbo Ice Cream](#jumbo-ice-cream) away from healing 80. Geobuster is the finisher. It is the only number that kills the field's big ex in one hit, and it throws every Energy on the Zygarde into the discard pile to do it.

A Mega gives up 3 Prizes, so losing both Zygardes loses the game. The other side of that math is kinder. Most of the big attackers in the field are 2-prize ex or 3-prize Megas, and against those, Fox's six Prizes are two or three knockouts. Single-prize decks are the exception, and [Versus the Card Shop](#versus-the-card-shop) covers them.

# Pokémon

### Mega Zygarde ex

**The core, at Fox's two.** Basic, 310 HP, 3 Prizes, Retreat 2, Grass Weakness, no Ability.

*Gaia Wave* does 200 for [F][F][F], and during the opponent's next turn this Pokémon takes 30 less damage from attacks, after Weakness and Resistance. *Nullifying Zero* costs [F][F][F][F][F] and flips a coin for each of the opponent's Pokémon; every heads is 150 to that Pokémon, with no Weakness or Resistance on the Bench. Against a full board of six, the average is 450 damage spread across it.

The third attack lives on [Core Memory](#core-memory). Attached to a Mega Zygarde ex, it adds *Geobuster*: 350 for [F][F][F][F], and discard all Energy from this Pokémon.

Two copies is what Fox has and what nearly every published list runs. At two, one is prized in about 19% of games and both in under 1%. [Fighting Gong](#fighting-gong) and [Mega Signal](#mega-signal) both find it, so the one left in the deck is never far away.

### Cinderace

**The launch pad.** Stage 2 Fire, 160 HP, 1 Prize, Retreat 0, Water Weakness.

***Explosiveness*: if this Pokémon is in your hand when you set up to play, you may put it face down in the Active Spot.** The deck runs no Scorbunny and no Raboot, so this is the only way Cinderace ever reaches the board. A Cinderace drawn after the opening hand is a dead card. Pitch it to an [Ultra Ball](#ultra-ball) or let a Lillie's shuffle it away.

*Turbo Flare* costs [C] and does 50, then searches the deck for up to 3 Basic Energy and attaches them to your Benched Pokémon in any way you like. That is the whole engine: three Energy onto the Bench, on turn one, for an attack that costs one.

Four copies, because Cinderace only counts in the opening seven, and four is a 40% chance of holding one there. Its Fire type does two things. [Premium Power Pro](#premium-power-pro) adds nothing to Turbo Flare, and Crustle is Fire-weak, so the 50 lands as 100 on the one card in the field that walls every ex in this deck.

### Mega Audino ex

**The backup launch, for the half of games with no Cinderace.** Basic Colorless, 270 HP, 3 Prizes, Retreat 1, Fighting Weakness.

*Kaleidowaltz* costs [C]: flip 3 coins, and for each heads, search your deck for up to 2 Basic Energy and attach them to your Pokémon in any way you like. Unlike Turbo Flare it can load the Active, itself included. It averages 3 Energy and comes up empty 1 time in 8.

*Ear Force* is [C][C][C] for 20, plus 80 for each Energy attached to the opponent's Active Pokémon. A Mega Excadrill ex loaded to five for *Maximum Drilling* takes 420.

Audino is a 3-Prize body with 270 HP and a Fighting Weakness, which is the worst stat line in the deck. Keep it in hand until the turn it attacks. Against Elliot's Garchomp, keep it in the deck.

### Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon ex

**The wall.** Basic Fighting, 210 HP, 2 Prizes, Retreat 1, Grass Weakness, Tera.

***Cornerstone Stance*: prevent all damage from attacks done to this Pokémon by your opponent's Pokémon that have an Ability.** Against an attacker with an Ability, Ogerpon takes nothing, forever. Check their attacker's card before you commit. *Demolish* is [F][C][C] for 140, and its damage ignores Weakness, Resistance, and any effect on the opponent's Active Pokémon, so a Resistance or a damage shield on their side does not blunt it.

Being a Tera Pokémon, it takes no damage from attacks while it sits on the Bench, from either side. Spread attacks that place damage counters still reach it, because counters are not damage.

### Riolu

One copy, and its job is becoming [Mega Lucario ex](#mega-lucario-ex). Basic, 80 HP, Retreat 2, Psychic Weakness. *Accelerating Stab* is [F] for 30 and cannot be used on your next turn. Do not open on it when another opener is in the hand. Fighting Gong finds it.

### Mega Lucario ex

**The refuel.** Stage 1 from Riolu, 340 HP, 3 Prizes, Retreat 2, Psychic Weakness.

*Aura Jab* costs [F], does 130, and attaches up to 3 Basic Fighting Energy from your discard pile to your Benched Pokémon. Geobuster throws four Energy into the discard; the next turn, Aura Jab puts three of them back on the next Zygarde. It is the only card in the deck that pulls from the discard pile, and that is exactly why it is here. Turbo Flare and Kaleidowaltz both pull from the deck.

*Mega Brave* is [F][F] for 270, 300 with Premium Power Pro, and cannot be used on your next turn.

A 1-1 line is thin, and it is thin on purpose. Mega Signal finds the Lucario, Fighting Gong finds the Riolu, and a Riolu benched early is the whole cost of having the answer ready.

# Trainers — Supporters

### Lillie's Determination

Shuffle your hand into your deck and draw 6, or 8 with exactly 6 Prize cards remaining. Four copies. With Harlequin it is the only draw in the deck, and it also shuffles away the Cinderace you can no longer play. Going second, the turn-one Lillie's for 8 is legal and good.

### Team Rocket's Petrel

**Search your deck for any Trainer card.** Four copies, which turn every one-of in the list into five copies: Core Memory, Hero's Cape, Battle Cage, Premium Power Pro, Mega Signal. On the turn Geobuster needs its Tool, Petrel is the card that finds it.

### Boss's Orders

Switch one of the opponent's Benched Pokémon into the Active Spot. Three copies. A 350 is only worth what it lands on, and the decks that beat this one are the decks that hide the thing worth killing.

### Xerosic's Machinations

Your opponent discards cards from their hand until they have 3. Two copies, best the turn after they refill with a draw Supporter. At home it lands hardest on Xero, who plans turns ahead out of his hand. [Alternatives](#alternatives) has the swap for nights he would rather not face it.

### Wally's Compassion

Heal all damage from one of your Mega Evolution Pokémon ex, then put all Energy attached to it into your hand. The Energy comes back one attachment a turn, so this is Prize denial rather than a heal-and-swing.

**The best target is the Zygarde that just used Geobuster.** It has no Energy left to lose, so Wally's is a full heal for free, and the 3 Prizes the opponent was lining up disappear. One copy.

### Harlequin

Each player shuffles their hand into their deck, then you flip a coin. Heads, you draw 5 and they draw 3. Tails, it is the other way round. One copy. It is a gamble, and it is the out when the hand is dead and no Lillie's is coming.

# Trainers — Items

### Fighting Gong

Search your deck for a Basic Fighting Energy card or a Basic Fighting Pokémon. Four copies. Mega Zygarde ex, Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon ex, and Riolu are all Basic Fighting Pokémon, so Gong is a Zygarde search on turn one and an Energy search every turn after. It cannot find Audino, which is Colorless, or Cinderace, which is a Stage 2.

### Jumbo Ice Cream

Heal 80 damage from your Active Pokémon that has 3 or more Energy attached. Four copies, and it is the reason Gaia Wave is the default attack. A Gaia Wave Zygarde keeps its three Energy, so every turn it can take 30 less and heal 80. That is 110 of damage undone a turn on a 310 HP body. After a Geobuster, the Zygarde has no Energy and Jumbo is dead until it reloads.

### Switch

Switch your Active Pokémon with one of your Benched Pokémon. Two copies. The main use is dragging Cinderace back into the Active Spot for a second Turbo Flare. The other is rescuing an Audino that Boss's Orders pulled forward.

### Energy Switch

Move a Basic Energy from one of your Pokémon to another. Two copies. Turbo Flare and Kaleidowaltz spread Energy wherever they land; Energy Switch gathers it onto the Zygarde that is about to attack.

### Premium Power Pro

This turn, your Fighting Pokémon's attacks do 30 more to the opponent's Active Pokémon, before Weakness. One copy. Zygarde, Ogerpon, Lucario, and Riolu are Fighting; Cinderace and Audino are not. The two numbers it buys are Geobuster at 380 and Gaia Wave at 230, which doubles to 460 into a Fighting Weakness.

### Mega Signal

Search your deck for a Mega Evolution Pokémon ex. One copy. It finds Zygarde, Audino, or Lucario. With Ultra Ball, it is one of the two cards that can find the Audino opening.

### Team Rocket's Transceiver

Search your deck for a Supporter with "Team Rocket" in its name. The only one in the deck is Petrel, so this is a fifth Petrel that costs an Item instead of a Supporter.

### Ultra Ball

Discard 2 other cards from your hand, then search your deck for any Pokémon. One copy. The dead Cinderaces are what you discard.

# Trainers — Tool & Stadium

### Core Memory

**The Mega Zygarde ex this card is attached to can use *Geobuster*.** One copy. Being a Tool, it stays attached after the Energy goes, so a second Geobuster from the same Zygarde needs only four more Energy. If that Zygarde is Knocked Out, the Tool goes to the discard with it. Petrel and Transceiver bring the outs to six.

### Hero's Cape

**The ACE SPEC.** The Pokémon this card is attached to gets 100 more HP. It goes on the Zygarde that is not holding Core Memory: 410 HP, and after a Gaia Wave, the next attack has to deal 440 to kill it. On a night the Audino opening happens, the Cape on Audino makes it 370.

### Battle Cage

Prevent all damage counters from being placed on Benched Pokémon, yours and your opponent's, by the effects of the opponent's attacks and Abilities. One copy, and it is the Dragapult card. *Phantom Dive* spreads 6 counters across the Bench, and Cinderace at 160 and Riolu at 80 are what it hunts. It does not block *Nullifying Zero*, which does damage rather than placing counters.

# Energy

### Basic Fighting Energy

Twelve copies. *Turbo Flare*, *Kaleidowaltz*, and Fighting Gong all fetch Basic Energy from the **deck**, and *Aura Jab* fetches it from the discard. None of the four can touch a Special Energy, so every Basic in the deck is a card the engine can still find.

### Rocky Fighting Energy

**Two copies, down from the source list's four.** It provides [F], and it prevents all effects of attacks done by the opponent's Pokémon to the Fighting Pokémon it is attached to. Damage is not an effect, so it stops riders and damage-counter placement, not hits.

The source ran 4 Rocky and 10 Basic Fighting. This page runs 2 and 12, because Rocky reaches the board only through the one hand attachment a turn, none of the three searchers can fetch it, and once Geobuster discards it, it is gone for good. Two covers the Zygarde that most needs shielding. Twelve Basics keeps the searchers fed.

## The Energy Engine

The deck wants four Energy on a Zygarde, ideally by turn two. Here is how they arrive.

| Opening | Turn 1 | Turn 2 | Turn 3 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cinderace, going second | *Turbo Flare* for 3, plus the hand attachment: **4** | Cinderace retreats for free; attach a fifth; Zygarde attacks | |
| Cinderace, going first | no attack allowed; attach **1** | *Turbo Flare* for 3, plus the hand attachment: **5** | Cinderace retreats for free; Zygarde attacks |
| Audino | *Kaleidowaltz*: 3 on average, 0 one time in 8 | *Kaleidowaltz* again, or Energy Switch the pile onto Zygarde | Zygarde attacks |

Two rules keep it honest.

**Choose to go second.** The coin flip happens before anyone draws, and going second is the only way Turbo Flare fires on turn one. It is a whole turn faster, and it lets the turn-one Lillie's draw 8.

**The searchers pull from the deck; Aura Jab pulls from the discard.** Every Geobuster throws four Energy away. Three Turbo Flares and a Kaleidowaltz can empty the deck of Basic Fighting by the late game, and Aura Jab is the card that turns the discard pile back into fuel. If Turbo Flare starts whiffing late, see [Test and Tune](#test-and-tune).

# Game Plans

## 1. The Cinderace Opening

**The line the deck is built to reach, and the one Fox gets about half the time.**

1. Win the coin flip, choose to go second.
2. Put Cinderace face down in the Active Spot. Bench a Zygarde if the hand has one.
3. Turn one: Fighting Gong for a Zygarde if it is not already down. Attach an Energy to the Zygarde, attach Core Memory, then *Turbo Flare* for 3 more onto it. The Zygarde now has 4.
4. Turn two: Cinderace retreats for free. Attach a fifth Energy, then choose the attack.

Choose the attack by one question: does a hit need to be a knockout this turn? If it does, *Geobuster* for 350. If it does not, *Gaia Wave* for 200 and keep all five Energy. The Zygarde that keeps its Energy is harder to kill and hits again next turn.

## 2. The Audino Opening

**No Cinderace in the seven. Audino launches instead.**

Open on Audino if you have it; otherwise Mega Signal for it on turn one and Switch it into the Active Spot. *Kaleidowaltz* attaches straight onto any of your Pokémon, so load the benched Zygarde, or Audino itself if a follow-up *Ear Force* is the plan. Energy Switch moves whatever lands in the wrong place.

The rule is to get Audino back off the table as soon as the Zygarde is loaded. It is 270 HP, Fighting-weak, and worth 3 Prizes, and it is doing nothing once its job is done. Switch it out and let Boss's Orders find something else.

## 3. Gaia Wave Every Turn

**The steady state, and the plan Fox should default to.**

A Zygarde on three or more Energy swinging Gaia Wave takes 30 less from every hit and heals 80 from a Jumbo Ice Cream on each of your turns. The opponent has to find 340 in one hit to kill it, 440 if it wears the Cape, and most decks cannot. Meanwhile 200 a turn is two-hit math on every big ex in the field.

## 4. After Geobuster

**Geobuster costs everything. Here is how to pay it back.**

The Zygarde that swung is empty but alive. That is the moment for Wally's Compassion: a full heal on a Pokémon with no Energy left to lose. Then rebuild with one of these, fastest first:

- **Aura Jab.** Mega Lucario ex hits for 130 and puts three of the discarded Energy onto a benched Zygarde.
- **A second Turbo Flare.** Switch Cinderace back into the Active Spot and pull 3 more from the deck.
- **Jump to the second Zygarde.** If a Turbo Flare split Energy onto it earlier, it may already be close.

Do not Geobuster twice in a row out of habit. Two Gaia Waves kill everything Geobuster does, and they keep the Energy.

## 5. Three Prizes a Mega

**Five of the twelve Pokémon give up three Prizes. That is the cost of the deck, and it is paid in Bench space.**

Zygarde, Audino, and Lucario are all Megas. Ogerpon gives up 2, Cinderace and Riolu give up 1. The rules follow from that:

- **Never bench a Mega you are not about to use.** A benched Audino with its job done is 3 Prizes for a Boss's Orders.
- **Only one Zygarde forward at a time.** The second one waits in hand or on the Bench wearing the Cape, never both hurt at once.
- **Let Cinderace absorb a hit when it can.** A Cinderace knocked out is 1 Prize, and it has already done its job.

## 6. What This Deck Gives Up

- **Grass Weakness on Zygarde and Ogerpon.** Crustle's *Superb Scissors* is 120 and doubles to 240, and Cynthia's Roserade's *Leaf Step* is 80 and doubles to 160.
- **Crustle walls it.** *Mysterious Rock Inn* prevents all damage from Pokémon ex, and every attacker here except Cinderace and Riolu is an ex. Cinderace doubling into Fire Weakness is the whole answer.
- **Half the openings are the slow one.** Four Cinderace is the cap, and 51% is as good as it gets.
- **The deck runs out of Energy late.** Every search pulls from the deck, and Geobuster empties the Zygarde into the discard.

## Versus the Kitchen Table

**[Xero's Gengar decks](./dark-gang.md).** Every Darkness Pokémon in Gengar Gang is Fighting-weak, so *Gaia Wave* lands as 400 and one-shots all of them: Okidogi ex at 250, Gengar ex at 280, and Mega Gengar ex at 350. The one body the Gang believes survives is a Mega Gengar in Hero's Cape at 450. Premium Power Pro kills that too, because the 30 goes on before Weakness: 230 doubled is 460.

He has three real answers, and Fox should know all of them.

- ***Shadowy Concealment*.** While a Mega Gengar ex is in play, every Darkness Pokémon an ex knocks out gives one Prize less. Every attacker in this deck except Cinderace and Riolu is an ex, so the Mega is the first thing to Boss and kill.
- ***Fainting Spell*.** When a Gengar ex is Knocked Out by an attack, Xero flips a coin, and heads knocks out the attacker too. A Zygarde that kills a Gengar ex is betting 3 Prizes on a coin. Finish a damaged one with Cinderace or Ogerpon when you can, and the bet drops to 1 or 2.
- **Ogerpon walls both Gengars.** Mega Gengar ex and Gengar ex both have Abilities, so neither can damage a Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon ex. Okidogi ex has no Ability and can.

**[Xero's lantern decks](./psychic-lanterns.md).** The lanterns resist Fighting, so Gaia Wave lands as 170 on a Mega Chandelure ex at 350. Geobuster with Pro lands as exactly 350, which is the one-shot. *Demolish* ignores Resistance and hits for a flat 140, and *Turbo Flare* is Fire, which they do not resist. Mega Chandelure ex has an Ability, *Binding Flame*, so it cannot damage Ogerpon at all. *Phantom Maze* does 50 more for every [C] of retreat, so Zygarde at Retreat 2 takes 230 from it, and Cinderace at Retreat 0 takes the minimum.

Several of Xero's pages carry their own "Fox's Ground Zero" sections, written from the other chair. Read them before a game; they are the plan he is bringing.
