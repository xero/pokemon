## The Balance Brief

Two decks are balanced when each one's best card is answered by something the other actually draws. Four levers do that here, and each one cuts against the deck that would otherwise run away with the game.

**Your prize denial is deliberately weak this time.** *Shadowy Concealment* only fires when the knockout comes from a Pokémon ex. Fox's best attacker against you is plain Flareon, which is not an ex, so his strongest line dodges your tax entirely. He earns that by giving up a Prize every time you kill it.

**His best attack is uncastable.** Both *Carnelian* and *Coruscating Quartz* cost [R][W][L], and the collection holds **zero basic Water Energy and zero basic Lightning Energy**. Flareon ex is capped at 130 and Eevee ex cannot attack at all. Without that ceiling his two ex Pokémon would simply outclass everything you own.

**Your damage scales off his board, and his deck has to go wide.** *Mind Jack* does 30 more for each of his Benched Pokémon, and a Hoothoot and Eevee deck cannot play narrow. If he keeps a small Bench to duck it, his evolutions stall.

**Tera walls your attacks but not your counters.** His Eevee ex and Flareon ex take zero damage from attacks while Benched. Every counter-placing effect in your deck ignores that completely, so the answer exists but costs you cards and setup rather than being free.

Nobody gets a card the other side cannot interact with. That is the whole design.

### How his deck works

**Noctowl is the consistency engine.** *Jewel Seeker* searches 2 Trainer cards every time he evolves a Hoothoot, as long as a Tera Pokémon is in play. Eevee ex and Flareon ex are his only Tera Pokémon, so getting one benched early is his real first priority, not attacking.

**Eevee is the fast lane.** *Boosted Evolution* lets it evolve the same turn it is played while Active, including turn one, so a Flareon can arrive immediately. Its own attack costs [C][C], which three Fire Energy pay without trouble.

> [!WARNING]
> **Eevee ex never attacks in this deck, and that is deliberate.** Its only attack, *Coruscating Quartz*, costs [R][W][L]. The collection holds **zero basic Water Energy and zero basic Lightning Energy**, and the one rainbow Energy in the database, Legacy Energy, is a card you do not own. There is no way to pay that cost tonight.
>
> Eevee ex is in the list as a **Tera battery and a wall**, nothing else. It is one of only two Tera cards in the collection, so *Jewel Seeker* on Noctowl does not work without it, and a 200 HP body that takes zero damage on the Bench is a fine thing to be holding. Tell Fox up front that this one blocks and enables rather than fights, or he will spend a turn trying to power it up.
>
> The same limit clips exactly one other attack. *Carnelian* on Flareon ex is also [R][W][L] and is also dead. **Everything else in the deck is payable with Fire alone**, because [C] means any type, so [R][C][C] is one Fire plus two of anything rather than three different colors.

**Flareon is his answer to you.** *Fighting Blaze* does 90, or **180 if your Active is a Pokémon ex or a Pokémon V**. Against your Mega Gengar ex and Crobat ex it hits like a heavy attacker from a 1-Prize body, and it is not an ex, so *Shadowy Concealment* never reduces what he collects for it.

**Leon is the whole damage curve.** 30 more on every attack, four copies. It turns Noctowl into 90, Flareon into 120 against your single-prizers, and Flareon ex into 160.

**Flareon VMAX is the boss card.** 320 HP is more than anything you own can knock out in one turn. *Max Detonate* discards the top 5 and does 100 per Energy found, which with 12 Fire in the deck averages one hit of 100 and occasionally spikes to 300. It gives up 3 Prizes, so it is a gamble, and it should be.

**Magma Basin does two jobs.** It reattaches discarded Fire to a Benched Fire Pokémon, and it is his only way to bump your Risky Ruins off the table.

> [!NOTE]
> **Sparkling Crystal only helps Flareon ex.** It makes a Tera Pokémon's attacks cost 1 less, which turns *Burning Charge* into a one-Energy 130. It does not rescue *Coruscating Quartz*, because dropping one Energy from [R][W][L] still leaves a Water or Lightning requirement he cannot pay.

### How your deck works

**Gengar from Perfect Order is the main attacker, and it costs one Energy.** *Mind Jack* does 10 plus 30 for each of his Benched Pokémon. Against the Bench his deck needs, that is 130 at four and **160 at five**, from a 1-Prize Pokémon, for a single Darkness. *Infinite Shadow* then returns it to your hand instead of the discard when it is knocked out, so the same Gengar attacks again next game turn off a fresh Gastly.

**One Basic feeds two payoffs.** Gastly and Haunter carry both Gengar and Mega Gengar ex. You are not paying extra Basics for the heavy option.

**Mega Gengar ex is support first.** *Void Gale* at 230 matters less here than the two things it does passively: *Shadowy Concealment* on the board, and switching on Seviper's *Excited Power* for 240 from a 1-Prize body.

**Crobat ex is the Tera answer.** *Biting Spree* places 2 damage counters on each of two of his Pokémon whenever Crobat comes down from your hand, and *Assassin's Return* puts it back in your hand to do it again. Counters are not damage from an attack, so a Benched Flareon ex is not safe from it.

**Risky Ruins is one-sided.** Every Basic in your deck is Darkness and therefore exempt. Every Basic in his is Colorless or Fire. He runs 4 Buddy-Buddy Poffin that Bench two at a time, so every Poffin costs him 40 damage.

**Shadowy Darkness Energy protects the setup.** On a Benched Gastly it makes that Gastly untouchable by attacks. Rare Candy it into Mega Gengar ex on the Bench, still protected, already holding an Energy, then walk it up armed.

## Four Interactions Decide This Game

**1. Bench size is the dial on your damage.** *Mind Jack* is 40 with an empty Bench and 160 with a full one. He wants Hoothoot, Eevee, and a Tera body down early. Every one of those makes your one-Energy attacker hit harder. If he starts keeping his Bench at two, his Noctowl engine stops and you have already won the exchange.

**2. Risky Ruins taxes his setup, Magma Basin takes it back.** His Eevee is 50 HP and his Hoothoot is 70. Two Ruins triggers plus one *Biting Spree* kills a Hoothoot without an attack. He has exactly two Magma Basin to bump it, and you have two Ruins to put back. Watch that trade; whoever holds the Stadium at the end of a turn is winning the chip war.

**3. Tera on the Bench is answered by counters, not attacks.** His Eevee ex and Flareon ex take zero attack damage while Benched. *Biting Spree*, *Sneaky Bite*, Punk Helmet, and Risky Ruins all place counters instead, and all four go through. You also have three Boss's Orders and a Prime Catcher to drag one out. He has three Switch to put it back.

**4. Flareon is the card that beats you, and killing it is cheap.** 180 into your ex Pokémon, 1 Prize, and immune to your tax. It has 130 HP, which your *Mind Jack* clears at a four-Pokémon Bench. Prioritize it over anything else on his board, including the Flareon ex.

## If It Plays Out Lopsided

Play three or four games, then turn one dial at a time. Do not turn two.

**If Fox cannot deal enough damage.** Give him the second Sparkling Crystal slot back as a third Magma Basin, or move Flareon to 3 by dropping a Hoothoot. The real fix is Leon access, so 2 Pokegear 3.0 or 2 Sonia over 2 Nest Ball puts a Supporter in his hand more often.

**If holding a Pokémon that cannot attack annoys him.** Cut 1 Eevee ex for Lugia, Darkness Ablaze 140, a 130 HP Colorless Basic with *Gust* at [C] for 30 and *Energy Loop* at [C][C][C] for 120. He keeps one Tera battery so *Jewel Seeker* still works, and he gains a body that fights. The cost is that his single remaining Eevee ex gets prized about 10% of games and Noctowl's Ability goes with it.

**If Fox runs away with it.** Cut Flareon VMAX and Flareon V for 2 Sudowoodo. That trades his 320 HP boss for two 100 HP bodies and removes 3 Prizes of liability, which sounds like a downgrade and is actually a nerf to his ceiling.

**If your Gengar never gets going.** The line is 4 Gastly and 2 Haunter for two different Stage 2s, which is thin. Go to 3 Haunter over 1 Sableye, or add the third Gengar over a Seviper.

**If your Mega Gengar ex is dead weight.** It is there for *Shadowy Concealment* and to switch on Seviper. If neither is paying off, cut both Mega Gengar ex and both Seviper for 2 Toxel and 2 Toxtricity, and let *Sinister Surge* accelerate a pure single-prize build. That version gives up nothing bigger than Crobat ex.

**If the games end too fast in either direction.** Both Stadiums out, both Punk Helmets out, and add Energy. Chip damage is what compresses these games.
