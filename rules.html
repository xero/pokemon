What this file is for.

This is the rulebook for the kitchen table. Every deck page in this repo uses these words without stopping to explain them, and every deck page links here instead. You do not have to read it top to bottom — just look things up when a page says something weird.

It grows as the table needs it. If a game teaches us a rule the hard way, it gets a row here.

Word List

Every game word you need, in order of how much you'll use it.

The board

Word
What it means
Active Spot
The one Pokémon out front. It's the only one that can attack, and it's the only one that can be attacked.
Bench
Your other Pokémon, waiting behind. You can have up to 5. They're safe from most attacks, but they can't attack either.
Hand
The cards you're holding.
Deck
Your face-down pile you draw from.
Discard pile
Where used and knocked-out cards go. Not gone forever — some cards fish things back out.
Prize cards
6 cards you set aside at the start. Every time you knock out a Pokémon, you take one — or two or three, if it was one of the big cards. Take all 6 and you win.

Pokémon

Word
What it means
Basic
A Pokémon you can play straight from your hand onto the Bench.
Stage 1
Evolves from a Basic. You put it on top of the Basic.
Stage 2
Evolves from a Stage 1. The biggest ones.
Evolve
Put an evolution card on top of the Pokémon it grows from. It keeps its Energy and its damage. You can't evolve a Pokémon the same turn you played it — unless a card says you can. Evolution goes by name, so any card that says "Evolves from Lampent" fits on any Pokémon named Lampent.
HP
How much damage a Pokémon can take before it's knocked out.
Damage counter
A little marker worth 10 damage. So 3 damage counters = 30 damage. Placing counters is not the same as attack damage: placed counters ignore Weakness, Resistance, and anything that says "prevent damage."
Knocked Out (KO)
When damage reaches a Pokémon's HP, it goes to the discard pile and your opponent takes their Prize card or cards.
Weakness
If a Pokémon is Weak to a type, attacks of that type do double damage to it.
Resistance
The opposite — attacks of that type do less damage.
Retreat cost
How much Energy you discard to move your Active Pokémon back to the Bench. Once per turn.
Ability
Free text on a Pokémon that just works. It is not an attack. Using an Ability does not end your turn.

Energy and attacks

Word
What it means
Energy
Cards you attach to Pokémon so they can attack. You may attach only 1 Energy per turn from your hand. Some cards attach extra — those are the good ones.
Type symbols
Fire · Water · Lightning · Grass · Psychic · Darkness · Fighting · Metal. Each one is paid only by an Energy of that type.
Colorless
The symbol. Any Energy pays for it — including every coloured one.
Attack cost
The symbols next to an attack. You need that much Energy attached to use it.
Attacking ends your turn.
Do everything else first. Once you attack, your turn is over.
Special Energy
Energy with extra text on it. Capped at 4 copies like any other card, and most recovery cards can't bring it back — "basic Energy" means the plain kind.

Trainer cards

Word
What it means
Trainer
Any card that isn't a Pokémon or an Energy. There are four kinds:
Supporter
Only 1 per turn. These are the powerful ones. Choosing which one is your biggest decision each turn.
Item
Play as many as you want in a turn.
Stadium
Stays on the table and changes the rules for both players. Only one Stadium can be out at a time — playing a new one throws the old one away.
Pokémon Tool
Attaches to a Pokémon and stays there.

Rules that trip people up

Word
What it means
Mulligan
If your first 7 cards have no Basic Pokémon, show your hand, shuffle it back, and draw 7 again. Your opponent draws 1 extra card. It's not a loss — just a do-over.
Going first
Whoever goes first cannot attack on their first turn. Use that turn to set up instead.
Confused
A Special Condition. When a Confused Pokémon tries to attack, flip a coin. Tails = the attack does nothing, that Pokémon takes 30 damage, and the turn ends. It only wears off when the Pokémon leaves the Active Spot.
Special Condition
Confused, Asleep, Paralyzed, Poisoned, or Burned. They all go away when the Pokémon leaves the Active Spot.
4-copy limit
You may have at most 4 cards with the same name in a deck. Basic Energy is the one exception — you can have as many as you want. The limit counts names, not pictures: every print of Litwick is still Litwick.
"Up to"
When a card says "up to 3," zero, one, or two is also legal. Fetch what you can use, not what you can fetch.

The big-card words

Word
What it means
ex
A really strong Pokémon. The catch: it's worth 2 Prize cards when it gets knocked out, not 1. "ex" is part of the card's name, so Eevee and Eevee ex count as different cards for the 4-copy limit.
Mega Evolution ex
The biggest ones, marked Mega. Worth 3 Prize cards when knocked out. Two knockouts on Megas is a whole game.
Rule Box
The grey box of extra rules at the bottom of an ex card. Some cards care whether a Pokémon has one — search cards that skip them, discard costs that refuse them.
Tera
An Ability some ex Pokémon have. While that Pokémon is on the Bench, attacks do zero damage to it. Not less. Zero. Placed damage counters still land, though — Tera is a rule about attacks.
ACE SPEC
A card so strong you may only have one in your whole deck — one total across all ACE SPEC cards, not one of each.
Search your deck
Look through your whole deck and take out the exact card you want. Then shuffle. The decks that do this a lot almost never get stuck.

Standard Legal, and Why Some Decks Aren't

Real tournaments only allow newer cards. Every card printed since 2023 carries a regulation mark — a little letter near the bottom — and each spring the oldest letter stops being legal. Right now the legal letters are H, I, and J.

At home, none of that matters. We play whatever the box holds, and some of the house decks use old cards on purpose, because the old ones are cheap and really fun. Each deck page says which kind it is: the tournament decks carry only legal letters, and the home decks tell you exactly which cards would have to stay in the binder at a real event.

Two more shop rules worth knowing before a tournament night:

  • Japanese prints are not legal at US events, even when the card is the same. The house owns a few; the deck pages flag every one.
  • A deck is exactly 60 cards with at least one Basic Pokémon — no more, no fewer, everywhere.

How To Play ex Style

This is the part that transfers. Everything below applies to any ex deck, including Fox's.

1. You are not fighting. You are racing to six.

The single biggest mental shift from the casual deck. In Gengar Gang every Pokémon is worth 1 Prize, so "knock out more stuff than they do" and "win" are the same sentence. In an ex format they come apart:

Two Mega Knock Outs is a whole game. You are not playing a war of attrition; you are playing a sprint where each turn is worth a third of the game.

2. Build a Prize map before you start

Before the first attack, decide which six Prizes you are taking. Three Knock Outs on their 2-prize attackers is the standard plan. Two on their Megas is faster if the chip math is there. Six on single-prize Pokémon is a race you will lose; do not plan it.

Then ask the mirror question, which Shadowy Concealment rewrites in your favor: which of yours can they afford to Knock Out? In the Dark Box the answer is "almost everything," and that asymmetry is the strategy.

4. The two-shot problem beats HP every time

If your attacker needs two turns to Knock Out and theirs needs one, you lose, regardless of HP. Chip damage is not a nice extra in ex decks; it is the difference between one-shotting and losing. The Dark Box's answer is game plan 3.

5. Gust effects are the most valuable cards in the format

Boss's Orders wins more ex games than any attacker, because in an ex format the whole game is deciding what you are allowed to Knock Out. The Dark Box runs three, and drawing one starts a plan; it does not fill a turn.

6. Rules that bite harder in ex decks

  • Going first, you cannot attack. Going second, Toxel's Call for Family makes the free attack a real turn.
  • ACE SPEC: exactly one per deck, total. Not one per name.
  • Shadowy Concealment does not stack. Three Megas in play still reduce by exactly 1.
  • Rule Box Pokémon cannot be discarded to Gwynn. The Dark Box does not run her, and her presence in Gengar Gang is one more reason the two decks stay separate.
