What this file is for.

This is not a fourth deck. It is Gengar Gang re-tuned for the rooms where the opponent attacks with Pokémon ex, the way the lantern deck becomes the Night Parade at home and the Eevee box swaps ten cards between Sun and Moon. Forty-nine cards never leave their sleeves. Eleven swap. Every card in both modes is an owned English print with a legal regulation mark, so either 60 can walk into a tournament.

Game words live in the table rules; the ex-format habits live in How To Play ex Style. The full-detail entries for the shared cards live in Gengar Gang and are not repeated here; each entry below says what changes about the card in this mode.

The Curse Toll is a different, bigger plan that needs two Gengar ex we have not found yet. This mode needs nothing we do not own.

The Thesis

Seviper is the only card in Standard whose text names a Darkness Mega Evolution Pokémon ex, and we own the Mega it names.

I searched the full legal pool, all 2,947 cards. One card keys off a Darkness Mega ex being in play, and it is a 120 HP common we already own two of. Excited Power adds 120 to Seviper's attacks while Mega Gengar ex is anywhere in play, so Pitch-Black Fangs swings for 240 from a body worth one Prize. Nobody plays it online. Nobody plays standalone Mega Gengar online either; its recorded season is 3 wins and 17 losses, because everyone builds the Mega as an attacker and feeds three Prizes to whoever kills it. This deck builds it as a wall that happens to hit for 230, and hides the real damage behind a snake the opponent is embarrassed to attack.

The reason the opponent is embarrassed is Shadowy Concealment. When a Darkness Pokémon of yours is Knocked Out by damage from an opposing Pokémon ex's attack, they take one fewer Prize card. Every Pokémon in this 60 is Darkness, so against a deck that attacks with ex Pokémon, the math collapses:

Your PokémonPrizes normallyKO'd by their ex
Gastly, Haunter, Seviper, Eternatus, Sableye, Toxel, Toxtricity10
Mega Gengar ex32

Their only path to six Prizes runs through the Mega itself, three separate times, against a 350 HP body that Wally's Compassion heals to full and Night Stretcher rebuilds. Meanwhile every ex they play is worth two or three Prizes to a snake that hits 240 and costs them nothing to lose.

Against a deck that attacks with single-prize Pokémon, Concealment reads as a blank card and the Mega is a three-Prize gift. Those rooms belong to Gengar Gang. Reading which room you are in is game plan 3.

Both modes live in one sleeved deck. Converting takes about a minute at the table.

Eleven cards out, eleven in, and the other forty-nine never move. The ACE SPEC stays Scramble Switch in both modes, so the one-per-deck rule holds without thinking about it. The English Prime Catcher stays with the lantern deck, which already claims it.

Two things fall out of the swap that are worth saying out loud.

Infinite Shadow leaves with the Gengars. In this mode a Knocked Out ghost stays knocked out unless Night Stretcher fetches it back. Play the Mega like the three-Prize investment it is, not like the self-recycling 130 that used to live in that slot.

Dark Bell leaves because the fuse left. The Confusion engine existed to serve Weezing's two-turn combo. Without Weezing, the slots buy the search that a two-copy Stage 2 core cannot live without.

Optional two-card sub. The binder holds a spare Perfect Order Haunter (Haunt: place 3 damage counters) and a promo Haunter (Spooky Shot 40). Swapping them in for the two Phantasmal Flames copies turns the middle stage into a counter-placer, and placed counters ignore Weakness and Resistance. Haunt is exactly the 30 that turns Seviper's 240 into a 270 kill. Skip the sub when teaching; take it when playing to win.

The wall the whole mode leans on. 350 HP, Stage 2 from Haunter, worth three Prizes when it falls, two under its own ability. It evolves like any other Stage 2; modern Mega cards do not end your turn.

Shadowy Concealment is the deck. One fewer Prize whenever your Darkness Pokémon dies to an opposing ex's attack, and every Pokémon here is Darkness. The ability works from the Bench and the Active Spot alike, and it does not stack, which is one reason two copies is the count rather than three. The other reason is that we own two.

Void Gale is 230, then it moves an Energy from this Pokémon to one of your Benched Pokémon. The move is not optional, and this deck turns the tax into the engine: the Energy lands on the Benched Seviper, which needs three. Attack with the Mega and you are simultaneously loading the snake. That loop is game plan 2.

The honest numbers. 230 one-shots nothing in the current ex tier except what Weakness doubles it into, and it needs a fresh Energy every turn to keep attacking. Play the Mega as a wall that counterattacks, not a sweeper. It survives every common hit below 350, Wally's undoes the ones it takes, and everything it fails to kill is 230 closer to Seviper's range.

The reason this file exists. A 120 HP Basic common with the only ability in Standard that looks for a Darkness Mega Evolution Pokémon ex. While the Mega is in play, Excited Power adds 120, so Pitch-Black Fangs does 240 before Weakness. One Prize when it dies, zero when an ex kills it under Concealment.

240 one-shots every single-prize body in the format and most non-Mega ex up to Team Rocket's Mewtwo, which its 480 after Weakness obliterates. Together with the Mega it two-turns everything printed: 230 plus 240 is 470, and no card has 470 HP.

The cost is the cost. Three Darkness is a lot of Energy for a 120 HP body, and paying it from hand takes three turns. Do not pay it from hand. Sinister Surge feeds the Benched Seviper from the deck, Void Gale banks the Mega's Energy onto it, and Scramble Switch carries a dying attacker's Energy forward. A Seviper that steps into the Active Spot with fangs already paid is the deck working; a Seviper you are charging in front is the deck stalling.

The check reads "in play," not "on your Bench." The Mega can be fighting in front and the snake still hits 240 behind it. That is the difference between Excited Power and Sableye's Cocky Claw, and it is why Seviper is the primary attacker and Sableye is the spare.

Game Plans

Five plans. The first two are the deck; the third is the reason the mode swap exists.

1. The Cage

The Prize math, done in both directions before every trade.

Their side, against ex attackers. Concealment turns every one-Prize body here into a zero-Prize body and discounts the Mega from three to two. Add it up: the only Prizes on your board are two Megas at two apiece, which is four. A pure ex deck cannot reach six Prizes without Knocking Out a rebuilt or healed Mega for the third time. Night Stretcher rebuilds it four times over; Wally's Compassion means even the second Knock Out is behind schedule.

Your side. Their ex Pokémon pay two or three apiece, so your six is two or three kills. Void Gale plus one Surge counter-drop kills 250; Void Gale plus Pitch-Black Fangs kills 470 across two turns; Boss's Orders picks which body does the paying.

The discipline. The cage only holds while a Mega is in play, and the Mega's own death still pays two. Never send it forward when 230 is not lethal and the Bench cannot rebuild it. A Mega that attacks into nothing volunteered a third of their game.

The escape hatch to respect. Concealment triggers on Knock Outs by damage from an attack from a Pokémon ex. A non-ex attacker on their side collects full Prizes off your board. One splashed single-prize attacker does not break the cage; a deck built on them does, and that deck is game plan 3's problem.

2. The Charmer's Loop

How three Energy costs get paid without three turns of attachments.

hand attachment      --> the front, whoever is fighting
Sinister Surge       --> the Benched Seviper, from the deck, plus 2 rent
Void Gale            --> 230, and the Mega's Energy lands on the snake
Scramble Switch      --> a dying attacker's Energy carries to the next one

Two attachments a turn is the baseline, and the Mega's attack is secretly a third: every Void Gale exports one Energy to the Bench, and the export is aimed. The snake charges while the wall fights.

The sequence the deck wants. Turn one, Poffin for Gastly and Toxel, Shadowy on the Gastly. Turn two, Candy the Gastly into the Mega, evolve Toxtricity, Surge the Benched Seviper. Turn three, the Mega attacks and banks an Energy onto Seviper; Surge pays the rest. By the time the wall has taken two hits, the snake is loaded and the opponent has to choose which threat to feed Prizes to.

Where the rent goes. Surge's two counters land on whoever got the Energy. On Seviper and Sableye that is nothing; they pay zero into an ex anyway. On the Mega it is 20 off a 350 HP pool that Wally's refunds in full. There is no bad answer, only a lazier one.

3. Reading the Room

One sleeved deck, two modes. Choose before the game, not during.

They attack withSleeveWhy
Pokémon ex and Mega exSnake CharmerThe cage is on. Their Prizes shrink; yours double.
Single-prize PokémonGengar GangConcealment is blank and the Mega gifts three Prizes. Weezing's 170 clears their whole tier instead.
A mixCount their attackersThe cage pays off when most of their damage comes from ex. One Flareon does not break it; a deck of them does.

The tell is their attacker lineup, not their deck's best card. A deck that runs one big ex but attacks with cheap bodies is a single-prize room wearing a costume.

Swap discipline. The eleven-card swap in The Mode Swap is the whole conversion. Keep both lists printed with the box, count 60 after every swap, and never sleeve half a mode; a deck with one Weezing and one Mega does neither job.

Versus the Kitchen Table

Fox's Hostile Takeover is the matchup this mode was built for. Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex is Psychic, 280 HP, and Weak to Darkness: Void Gale lands 460 and Pitch-Black Fangs lands 480, so either ghost or snake one-shots his best card while Concealment zeroes almost everything he can kill in return. Crobat ex at 310 Darkness takes no Weakness and needs two hits; Punk Helmet and Surge counters close the gap. Respect Spidops and Tarountula, which are Grass and erase Sableye, and remember half his board is Darkness, so Risky Ruins collects less than usual.

Fox's Metal deck plays the cage straight. Mega Excadrill ex hits 330, and the Mega survives it at 350; Wally's Compassion then undoes the whole hit, which breaks his two-shot math completely. His Fezandipiti and Munkidori pay him nothing back when they die to your ex, but your Seviper and Sableye pay him nothing either, because his attacker is a Mega. It becomes a pure wall race, and only one side heals.

Fox's Flareon engine is the trap matchup. Plain Flareon does 180 against ex Pokémon and is not an ex, so it dodges Concealment entirely and two-shots the Mega for one Prize of risk. Wally's resets the two-shot; Seviper one-shots every Flareon print he owns except Flareon ex at 270, which is one Helmet proc or one Ruins counter away from 240's reach. Winnable, but this is the room where reading the room says to think hard about sleeving Gengar Gang instead.
