# Xero's Lucky Haunt

### The Gengar Gang with a Blissey ex on the Bench, carrying the Energy forward

> [!NOTE]
> **What this file is for.**
>
> The Blissey build of [the Gengar Gang](./dark-gang.md), the list Xero has played on [TCG Live](https://tcg.pokemon.com/en-us/tcgl/) since 2026-09-29. Every card carries regulation mark H, I, or J. There is no sideboard, so the sixty answer the whole field on their own.
>
> It is eight cards off the Gengar Gang. Two Chansey and two Blissey ex came in for an Okidogi ex, a Toxel, a Boss's Orders, and an Energy Recycler, and Neo Upper Energy took Hero's Cape's ACE SPEC slot. *Happy Switch* answers the deck's oldest problem, that *Sinister Surge* never reaches the Active Spot, and Neo Upper pays a whole *Chaotic Pain* with one card. The deck list and the cards come first, then [The Thesis](#the-thesis) and [The Hard Rules](#the-hard-rules). The roads not taken are in [Alternatives](#alternatives), and game words are defined in [the table rules](./rules.md).

**22 + 27 + 11 = 60.** ✓ Eleven Basics, fourteen Supporters, one ACE SPEC, and nothing over four copies.

**Eleven Basics is a 22% mulligan**, the same as the Gengar Gang, because the two Chansey replaced an Okidogi and a Toxel one for one. The opening seven holds an Okidogi 22% of the time, and a dog with an Energy 16% of the time. It holds at least one Gastly 40% of the time. About a fifth of opening hands (22%) hold no Energy at all, which is what Hilda, *Poisonous Musculature*, and Janine's are for. Poffin finds the seven small Basics, and Poke Pad finds those and Chansey; the dogs come from *Call for Family*, Dawn, and Grimsley's.

# Pokémon

### Okidogi ex

**250 HP Basic, Fighting Weakness, Retreat 3.** The shield, and on Live the most dependable early attacker in the deck.

***Poisonous Musculature* costs [C].** It searches up to two Basic Darkness Energy out of the deck, attaches them to the dog, and Poisons it. One Energy from the hand becomes three on the board, which is the best Energy deal in the format. The deck takes both, every time.

***Chain-Crazed* does 130, and 130 more while this Pokémon is Poisoned.** That is 260 for [D][D][C], and 520 into anything weak to Darkness. It one-shot a Dhelmise, a Spiritomb, and a Mew ex on Live, and it does the same to Alakazam, Munkidori, and Jellicent ex.

**Poison ends the moment the dog leaves the Active Spot.** A retreat, a Switch, an AZ's, or their Boss's Orders all end it, and *Chain-Crazed* is back to 130 until Janine's or another *Musculature* turn restores it. The dog wants to stand still.

**Two copies, one in play.** It opens 22% of the time, and 16% of hands hold a dog with an Energy; Neo Upper counts as that Energy, since *Musculature* costs [C]. The second stays off the Bench while the first is alive, because every benched dog is a Pokémon ex their Boss's Orders can find. A dog that is going to die anyway swings first.

### Gastly

70 HP, which keeps it inside Poffin and Poke Pad range. It is Darkness, so Risky Ruins never touches it and Janine's can feed it.

**Four copies, the highest-leverage count in the deck.** Five Stage 2 cards and two Haunter sit on this one Basic, and Grimsley's is the only way around it. One goes to the Bench on the deck's first turn whether it is needed or not, because Rare Candy can't evolve a Pokémon that arrived this turn.

The Live list plays the Temporal Forces Gastly for the art. That print is 60 HP and mark H, so the paper list runs Perfect Order 048 at 70.

### Haunter

**Two copies, and two jobs.** It is the no-Candy route, since Gastly to Haunter to Gengar ex needs only time. It is also the safe step, turning an exposed Gastly into a 100 HP, one-Prize Haunter that becomes a Gengar next turn.

*Sinister Surge* reaches a benched Haunter, so Surges load a Haunter that evolves into a Gengar ex already holding Energy. Candy is the speed route and lands empty; Haunter is the Energy route. The simulator also says the last Haunter carries real weight. Cutting to zero cost about seven points of Gengar speed by turn 5 in every test.

### Gengar ex

**Stage 2, 280 HP, Fighting Weakness, Retreat 2. 30th Celebration 090, mark J.** The deck.

***Chaotic Pain* costs [D][D] and places 13 damage counters on 1 of the opponent's Pokémon.** [Who Chaotic Pain hits](#who-chaotic-pain-hits) has the target order.

**280 HP is the line, and there is no Cape to move it.** It survives every hit at 270 or under, including *Phantom Dive* at 200, *Nebula Beam* at 210, *Vengeful Anchor* at 170, and a bare *Aura Jab* at 260. Everything at 280 or over kills it, from *Aura Jab* with Premium Power Pro at 320 to *Mega Brave* at 540. Against those hits the answers are AZ's for 80, the Mega's tax, and *Fainting Spell*.

***Fainting Spell* flips a coin when this Pokémon is Knocked Out by damage from an attack, and on heads the Attacking Pokémon is Knocked Out too.** It works against any attacker, ex or not. On Live it took a Mega Zygarde, a Mega Floette, and a Dhelmise. [When to stand and when to pull](#when-to-stand-and-when-to-pull) has the rule.

Three copies, because this is the card that attacks, and so the card that dies.

### Mega Gengar ex

**Stage 2 from Haunter. 350 HP. Gives up 3 Prizes, or 2 once its own Ability applies.**

***Shadowy Concealment* takes one Prize off every Knock Out the opponent's Pokémon ex score on the deck's Darkness Pokémon with damage from an attack.** It covers the whole board including itself, it works from the Bench, and it doesn't stack. Everything in this deck is Darkness except Chansey and Blissey ex, which it never covers.

**The trigger is exact.** It needs damage, from an attack, from a Pokémon ex. It does nothing against single-Prize attackers, nothing against counters placed by *Powerful Hand* or *Spiritual End*, and nothing against counters moved by Munkidori. In those rooms the Mega is 3 Prizes with no upside, and it stays in the deck. A Gastrodon on their Bench or an Iron Thorns ex in their Active Spot switches it off entirely; see [Cards to Watch Out For](#cards-to-watch-out-for).

**Grimsley's Move is the fastest way to it.** From the deck's second turn, Grimsley's benches a Mega straight out of the deck with no Gastly and no Candy spent. That only works while a Mega is still in the deck, so when Grimsley's is the plan, Hilda leaves the Megas where they are. Two copies, one to play and one in case the first is prized or dies.

***Void Gale* does 230 for [D][D] and moves an Energy to a Benched Pokémon.** It is the finisher when no Gengar ex is up. Boss's Orders brings up a target, Switch brings in the Mega, one attachment arms it, and it swings. That line closed the Charizard game on Live. It doubles to 460 into anything weak to Darkness, the one number *Chaotic Pain* can't reach, because counters ignore Weakness.

**Happy Switch closes the loop.** *Void Gale* pushes an Energy to the Bench, and next turn Blissey ex moves a Basic one back onto the Mega, so a Mega holding two swings every turn without a new attachment. [The ferry](#8-the-ferry) has the line.

**At 350 it is the biggest body in the list**, and nothing here survives Mega Zygarde's 400 or *Mega Brave* at 540.

### Toxel

***Call for Family* benches two Basic Pokémon from the deck for [D].** It reaches Okidogi ex, which Poffin and Poke Pad can't. [Build order](#build-order) has what to take.

Three copies, down from four; the Chansey keep the Basic count at eleven. It is the going-second opener when no dog showed up, and a 70 HP body that pays nothing under Concealment.

### Toxtricity

***Sinister Surge* attaches a Basic Darkness Energy from the deck to a Benched Darkness Pokémon once a turn, and puts two damage counters on it.** It is an Ability, so it works on turns the Supporter is busy elsewhere.

Two copies, and each Surges on its own; the card carries no "you can't use more than one" clause. Grimsley's and Poke Pad both find it, and it has no Candy clock, so it never needs the turn-1 search. [Where the Energy goes](#where-the-energy-goes) covers the targets.

With Blissey ex in play, a Surge doesn't have to land on the attacker. It lands on a benched body that can carry the 20, and *Happy Switch* walks the Energy forward when it's needed.

> [!CAUTION]
> **The two damage counters have a breakpoint.** A bare Gengar ex at 280 survives a bare *Aura Jab* at 260, and one Surge makes that exactly lethal. Toxtricity at 140 survives *Chaotic Pain*'s 130, and one Surge on itself puts it inside one Pain. **Against Fighting and Metal, Surge feeds the dogs and never the ghosts. Against *Chaotic Pain*, Surge the Mega and never Toxtricity.**

### Chansey

**120 HP Basic, Colorless, Fighting Weakness, Retreat 2.** The cradle for Blissey ex, and the 11th Basic.

***Lucky Attachment* costs [C] and attaches a Basic Energy from the hand to any of the deck's Pokémon.** It is a second manual attachment, paid for with the turn's attack. ***Boundless Power*** does 80 for [C][C][C], and Chansey can't attack the next turn.

**It is not Darkness, and that changes four things.** The deck's own Risky Ruins puts 2 counters on it when it is benched, so it goes down before the Ruins does, or while their Stadium is up. Janine's and Surge can't feed it. Grimsley's can't find it. And Concealment never covers it, so it is always 1 Prize.

Two copies. Poke Pad, Dawn, and *Call for Family* find it; Poffin can't, at 120 HP. [Build order](#build-order) says when to go get it.

### Blissey ex

**Stage 1 from Chansey, 300 HP, Colorless, Fighting Weakness, Retreat 4. Twilight Masquerade 134, mark H.**

***Happy Switch* moves a Basic Energy from one of the deck's Pokémon to another, once a turn.** It is an Ability, so it works from the Bench, it costs no card, and it reaches the Active Spot, which Surge never does. A Surge onto a benched body plus a *Happy Switch* is an Energy on the Active every turn without the manual attachment. It can't move Neo Upper Energy, which is Special. Team Rocket's Watchtower and an Active Iron Thorns ex switch it off; see [Cards to Watch Out For](#cards-to-watch-out-for).

***Return* does 180 for [C][C][C], and then draws until the hand holds six.** The draw is optional.

**It is a hole in the tax.** Colorless means Concealment never covers it, so it costs 2 Prizes to anything that knocks it out, and Retreat 4 means it leaves the Active only by Switch or AZ's. It sits on the Bench and works from there. The Azumarill loss had it dragged up by Boss's Orders and taken for 2.

**Check *Happy Switch* before every attack.** It is the easiest Ability in the deck to forget. In the Metagross win it fired once in five turns, and in the Azumarill loss a turn-3 *Happy Switch* moving the Toxel's Energy would have readied the Gengar ex.

Two copies, so one survives a Prize card. Hilda and Dawn find it; Grimsley's can't, since it isn't Darkness. Only one goes on the board at a time, because a second is two more Prizes that nothing discounts.

# Trainers — Supporters

### Lillie's Determination

**It shuffles the hand into the deck and draws six, or eight while all six Prize cards remain.** Three copies.

It comes down early for eight, with two exceptions the Live games taught. The first is a hand that already holds its line, which stays unshuffled. In the Charizard game, the opening hand had Gastly, Gengar ex, Dawn, Hilda, Toxtricity, and two Energy, and a turn-1 Lillie's pushed the first *Chaotic Pain* back a turn. The second is lethal. Lillie's shuffles away every card in hand along with the dead ones, so the check for lethal comes before the dig.

Blissey's *Return* draws to six as well, so a Lillie's isn't the only refill once Blissey is down.

### Grimsley's Move

**It looks at the top 7 cards of the deck and puts a Darkness Pokémon found there onto the Bench, and it can't be played on the first turn.** Two copies, Phantasmal Flames 090, mark I. Both copies in the sleeve are the player's-choice stamped holos from the last two league nights, and those two prizes are why this card got a look at all.

**It benches any stage.** The ruling reads "you can place any stage of {D} Pokémon you find directly onto your bench" (Phantasmal Flames FAQ, TPCi Rules Team, 2025-11-13), and Live honors it. Eighteen of the 22 Pokémon are Darkness, so it hits something about 93% of the time. Chansey and Blissey ex are Colorless and never come up for it. What it hits is what matters.

**What it benches can't be devolved.** Every devolve effect in Standard reads "evolved Pokémon" and removes "the highest Stage Evolution card on it." A Pokémon Grimsley's puts straight onto the Bench never evolved and has nothing under it, so Espeon's *Miraculous Shine*, Espeon ex's *Amazez*, and Claydol's *Devolution Ray* all pass over it. Against a devolve deck, Grimsley's is the safe way to land a Stage 2.

| It benches | Which means |
| :--- | :--- |
| Mega Gengar ex | *Shadowy Concealment* on turn 2, with no Gastly and no Candy spent |
| Toxtricity | a Surge the same turn, since it arrives on the Bench |
| Gengar ex | a second attacker, arriving empty; Surge and the turn's attachment arm it |
| Okidogi ex | the shield, when no dog opened |
| Haunter or Gastly | next turn's Gengar, or a fresh Candy target |

The odds, and when to play it, are in [Grimsley's Move odds](#grimsleys-move-odds). The six cards it passes over go to the bottom of the deck and stay there until something shuffles.

### Dawn

**It searches the deck for a Basic, a Stage 1, and a Stage 2 Pokémon.** Two copies. One card sets up a whole line: Gastly, Haunter, and a Gengar, or Toxel, Toxtricity, and a Gengar, or Chansey, Blissey ex, and a Gengar. [Build order](#build-order) says which line comes first; in the mirror loss it took Chansey, Blissey ex, and the Mega on turn 4, once Toxtricity was Surging, and the Mega landed by Candy the same turn.

**Its Stage 2 pick is the matchup call**, from [Which Stage 2 gets the Candy](#which-stage-2-gets-the-candy). When Grimsley's is the plan for the Mega, Dawn takes Gengar ex and leaves the Mega in the deck.

### Hilda

**It searches the deck for an Evolution Pokémon and an Energy card.** Two copies. It is the Energy-in-hand card for the 22% of hands that open without one, and it reads *Evolution Pokémon*, which here is eleven cards: two Haunter, three Gengar ex, two Mega Gengar ex, two Toxtricity, and two Blissey ex. Its Energy can be Neo Upper.

| Board | Hilda takes | Then |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a Gastly benched last turn | Gengar ex or the Mega, and an Energy | Candy, attach; Neo Upper on a fresh Gengar ex is a *Chaotic Pain* that turn |
| a Toxel benched last turn | Toxtricity, and an Energy | the Surge engine a turn early |
| a Chansey benched last turn | Blissey ex, and an Energy | evolve, and *Happy Switch* is live the same turn |
| a Gastly and a Toxtricity | Haunter, and an Energy | evolve, attach, Surge the Haunter, and it becomes a loaded Gengar next turn |

**It comes before the Candy.** Hilda can change which Stage 2 the board wants.

### Team Rocket's Petrel

**It searches the deck for a Trainer card.** Two copies. It makes every one-of behave like a two-of.

Same-turn targets are the Items and the Stadium: Rare Candy, Poke Pad, Switch, Energy Recycler, and Risky Ruins. The Supporters it finds wait for next turn: Grimsley's, Boss's Orders, AZ's, and Janine's. **This list runs one Boss's Orders and one Energy Recycler, so Petrel is the second copy of each**, and the fastest way to a Risky Ruins when their Stadium is the problem. It can't find Neo Upper, which is an Energy.

Against Team Rocket decks, their own Team Rocket's Factory draws two more cards whenever this deck plays Petrel. That won a turn in the Toxtricity Box game.

### Janine's Secret Art

**It attaches a Basic Darkness Energy from the deck to each of up to two Darkness Pokémon, and Poisons the Active Pokémon if it took one.** One copy, mark H.

It does three jobs in one Supporter. It Poisons a dog again after it left the Active Spot, which is worth 130 damage. It puts the second Energy on an Active Gengar that is one short, which Surge can't do and *Happy Switch* does only from a body that already holds one. And it pulls two Energy out of the deck in a drought. It can't feed Chansey or Blissey ex, which are Colorless.

**One Energy per Pokémon, never two on one.** A judge confirmed it.

> [!NOTE]
> **TCG Live now plays it as printed.** Live used to force one Energy onto the Active and one onto the Bench. A September 2026 update fixed that, so two Benched Pokémon work online too, and the Active is Poisoned only when it takes one of the Energy.

### Boss's Orders

**It switches one of the opponent's Benched Pokémon into the Active Spot.** One copy; the second went for the Blissey line, and Petrel finds this one.

*Chaotic Pain* does most of a gust's job for free. The reasons to drag are the ones counters can't cover:

- **Munkidori.** It is weak to Darkness, so once it is Active any real attack kills it, even an un-Poisoned *Chain-Crazed*. The answer doesn't need a Gengar in play.
- **A target behind Battle Cage**, which protects their Bench and nothing else.
- **The last Prize.** A damaged body or a Pokémon ex on their Bench, for a dog or *Void Gale*.
- **An attacker they just loaded**, stranded in the Active Spot.

> [!WARNING]
> **Boss's Orders stays in hand while a Mega Lucario ex that just used *Mega Brave* is Active.** Its lockout is an effect of the attack, and it comes off the moment the Lucario leaves the Active Spot. Sending it to the Bench hands them a fresh 540 a turn early, and Lucario decks carry Air Balloon to bring it straight back.

### AZ's Tranquility

**It swaps the Active Pokémon with a Benched one, and heals 80 from the one it moved if that one is a Pokémon ex.** One copy, Chaos Rising 076, mark J.

**Switch is the rotation on turns the Supporter slot is spoken for, and AZ's is the rotation on turns it isn't**, or when the board needs one of the three things only AZ's does:

- **It works under Item lock.** Budew's *Itchy Pollen* and Jellicent ex's *Oceanic Curse* turn off Switch and Rare Candy, and AZ's still moves.
- **It is the dog's exit.** A dog that leaves by AZ's keeps its Energy and comes back 80 healthier.
- **It is the Mega's return ticket.** The Mega swings *Void Gale*, takes the hit, and AZ's sends it home with Concealment still on.
- **It is Blissey's exit.** Blissey ex costs four to retreat, and AZ's pulls it back healed 80. In the mirror loss, AZ's healed the Mega to full and put Blissey in front, and the opponent's next turn found no Knock Out.

Three misuses tempt everyone. AZ's on the attacker wastes the attacker unless the next one is loaded. AZ's on a Gengar ex that would die to the hit anyway throws away the coin, since standing flips and pulling doesn't. And AZ's over Lillie's on a bricked hand draws no cards.

# Trainers — Items

### Rare Candy

**Four copies.** Five Stage 2 cards sit over four Gastly, and Candy is the speed route. Two timing rules decide openings: never on the first turn, and never on a Basic that entered play this turn. **The search comes first, then the Candy.**

### Buddy-Buddy Poffin

**Two Basic Pokémon with 70 HP or less, straight to the Bench.** Gastly and Toxel are the targets. It is the only Item that benches two on turn 1, and the only way to fill the Bench going first. Two copies, mark H; Poke Pad is its April replacement.

### Poke Pad

**It searches the deck for a Pokémon without a Rule Box.** Two copies, printed Poké Pad, Perfect Order 081, mark J, and reprinted in 30th Celebration.

It finds Gastly, Toxel, Chansey, Haunter, and Toxtricity, never an ex. **On the first turn it takes Gastly**, because Gastly is the only card on a clock; later it takes whatever the line is missing. Together with the 11th Basic, it cut the chance of an empty Bench after the first turn from 34% to 22% going first, and from 10% to 6% going second.

### Switch

**It swaps the Active Pokémon with a Benched one.** Two copies. Retreat Costs are paid in Energy, and this board's are 4 for Blissey ex, 3 for a dog, 2 for either Gengar, Toxtricity, or Chansey, and 1 for Gastly, Haunter, or Toxel; Switch pays in a card instead. It also clears Poison, so it belongs to the ghosts, not to a Poisoned dog.

### Energy Recycler

**It shuffles up to 5 Basic Energy from the discard pile back into the deck.** One copy, down from two, and Petrel is the second. It can't return Neo Upper, which is Special.

| Engine | Where it looks |
| :--- | :--- |
| *Sinister Surge* | deck |
| *Janine's Secret Art* | deck |
| *Poisonous Musculature* | deck, two at a time |
| Hilda | deck, to hand |

Every engine searches the deck, so Energy in the discard pile is invisible to all of them. Knock Outs, retreats, and Crushing Hammer all bury it. On Live, one long game emptied the deck by turn 9 and *Sinister Surge* found nothing until Recycler put five back. Petrel finds one.

# Trainers — Tool & Stadium

The list runs no Tool. The ACE SPEC slot went to [Neo Upper Energy](#neo-upper-energy), and Jamming Tower has nothing here to switch off.

### Risky Ruins

**Two damage counters on every Basic non-Darkness Pokémon either player benches.** Everything here is Darkness but Chansey, so it is one-sided as long as the Chansey hit the Bench before the Ruins does. A Chansey benched under the deck's own Ruins takes the 20, and the Blissey ex it becomes starts at 280. The 20 stays through evolution on their side too, which is what puts Alakazam and Crustle inside one *Chaotic Pain*.

**Two copies, because Stadiums are the other half of its job.** A Stadium only leaves play when another replaces it, and a Stadium can't be played over one with the same name.

| Their Stadium | What it does to the deck | When to replace it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Battle Cage | stops *Chaotic Pain* on their Bench | when the snipe is needed; Charizard decks replay it, so the second copy waits |
| Forest of Vitality | Weedle to Beedrill ex in one turn | at every chance; it is a tax, not a lock, since they run four |
| Spikemuth Gym | searches their Marnie's Pokémon every turn | the turn after they build |
| Ange Floette | Mega Floette ex at 400 | before the swing at it; it drops back to 250 |
| Gravity Mountain | every Stage 2 in play gets 30 less HP | the turn before their big hit, not the turn it lands |
| Full Metal Lab | the dogs and *Void Gale* do 30 less to Metal Pokémon; counters ignore it | the turn a hit needs the 30 |
| N's Castle, Prism Tower, Team Rocket's Factory | free retreats and extra draws | when nothing above is in play |

> [!NOTE]
> **Against Dragapult the deck's Ruins is usually unplayable, and that's fine.** Most Dragapult lists run Risky Ruins themselves, and theirs does nothing to the deck's Bench except chip a Chansey, so bench the Chansey before theirs lands.

# Energy

### Basic Darkness Energy

Ten, and the count is deliberately lower than it looks. *Poisonous Musculature*, *Sinister Surge*, and Janine's all search the deck, so the only Energy that needs to be in hand is the one manual attachment per turn. One Energy Recycler, two Switch, and *Happy Switch* keep ten honest, and Neo Upper is the eleventh. Any Basic Darkness print works, holos included.

### Neo Upper Energy

**It provides [C] on anything, and on a Stage 2 it provides every type of Energy, 2 at a time.** The ACE SPEC, Temporal Forces 162, mark H.

**On a Gengar ex or the Mega it is a whole attack.** One attachment pays [D][D], so a Gengar ex that lands by Candy swings *Chaotic Pain* the same turn. On a dog it is [C], which still pays *Musculature*.

**It took the Cape's slot on Live and kept it.** One Neo Upper paid for three straight Pains in the Hop's Zacian game, paid for a turn-3 Pain alone in the Igglybuff win, and paid for both Pains on turns 3 and 4 of the mirror loss with no other Energy in hand. In the N's Zoroark win it bought a turn-7 attack outright, again with no other Energy in hand.

**Most of the deck can't touch it.** Hilda finds it, and nothing else does. *Happy Switch*, Energy Recycler, Janine's, and Surge all read Basic Energy, and Petrel reads Trainers. *Void Gale* can move it, but off a Stage 2 it drops back to [C], so the Mega moves a Basic instead.

## The Thesis

The format runs on Mega Evolution Pokémon ex. They carry 330 to 380 HP, they hit for 270 and up, and nothing in this deck wins a straight damage race against one. The deck does not try. It kills them before they exist.

***Chaotic Pain* costs [D][D] and places 13 damage counters on 1 of the opponent's Pokémon.** Any one they have, Active or Benched. Placing counters is not dealing damage, and most of what matters about this card follows from that one distinction.

**Almost every Mega grows out of something, and 130 kills nearly all of those somethings.**

| Their Mega | Grows from | HP | One Chaotic Pain |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | :---: |
| Mega Lucario ex | Riolu | 80 | ✓ |
| Mega Excadrill ex | Drilbur | 70 | ✓ |
| Mega Starmie ex | Staryu | 70 | ✓ |
| Mega Greninja ex | Froakie, Frogadier | 70, 100 | ✓ |
| Mega Charizard X ex | Charmander, Charmeleon | 80, 110 | ✓ |
| Mega Venusaur ex | Bulbasaur, Ivysaur | 80, 110 | ✓ |
| Mega Gardevoir ex | Ralts, Kirlia | 70, 100 | ✓ |
| Mega Chandelure ex | Litwick, Lampent | 70, 90 | ✓ |
| Dragapult ex | Dreepy, Drakloak | 70, 90 | ✓ |
| Beedrill ex | Weedle, Kakuna | 50, 80 | ✓ |

A few Megas grow from nothing. Mega Zygarde ex, Mega Floette ex, and Mega Kangaskhan ex are Basics, and those rooms are won by [the Prize tax](#the-prize-tax), not the snipe.

**The thesis has limits, and they are arithmetic.** One Pain a turn can't keep up with a deck that benches four cradles on turn 1 and searches two more every turn, which is Mega Excadrill. Going second, it can't reach a Stage 1 Mega that evolves on the opponent's second turn, which is Mega Starmie and Mega Lucario. And it finds nothing to shoot when Forest of Vitality takes Weedle all the way to Beedrill ex on the turn it lands. [Versus the Card Shop](#versus-the-card-shop) has the plan for each.

**Very little stops the counters.** Damage prevention in this format is worded as *damage*, and counters walk through it. The Tera rule on Dragapult ex's Bench, Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon ex, Crustle's *Mysterious Rock Inn*, Neutralization Zone, and Full Metal Lab all miss. What does stop them is effect prevention: Mist Energy, Rocky Fighting Energy, *Hide 'n' Sneak*, Battle Cage, and a few Pokémon that shield themselves or their Bench. Attack damage isn't an effect, so the dogs and *Void Gale* get through every one of them. [Cards to Watch Out For](#cards-to-watch-out-for) has the full list and the way around each.

**The other half of the deck is the price of dying.** *Shadowy Concealment* takes a Prize off every Knock Out an opposing Pokémon ex scores on the deck's Darkness Pokémon, and *Fainting Spell* flips a coin at whatever kills a Gengar ex. Between them, the deck's losses cost one Prize while theirs cost two or three.

| Attacker | Cost | Output | Prizes it gives up |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | ---: |
| **Gengar ex**, *Chaotic Pain* | **[D][D]** | **130 as counters, any target** | **1** |
| Mega Gengar ex, *Void Gale* | [D][D] | 230, and 460 into Darkness Weakness | 2 |
| Okidogi ex, *Chain-Crazed* | [D][D][C] | 260 while Poisoned, 520 into Darkness Weakness | 1 |
| Blissey ex, *Return* | [C][C][C] | 180, then draw to six | 2 |

The Prize column assumes Mega Gengar is on the Bench and their attacker is a Pokémon ex. Against a single-Prize attacker, or counters moved by an Ability, add one to every row but Blissey's, which Concealment never covers. **Blissey ex is the engine, not the attacker.** *Return* is the swing for a turn when nothing else is loaded, and its real work is *Happy Switch* from the Bench.

## The Hard Rules

Every Live loss with this deck traces back to one of these. Each has a number behind it. The card sections above explain the cards; this section explains the decisions.

### Which Stage 2 gets the Candy

| They attack with | Build first | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Pokémon ex and Megas | **Mega Gengar ex, parked on the Bench** | *Shadowy Concealment* takes a Prize off every Knock Out their ex scores. Against Mega Zygarde it would have turned their 6 Prizes into 2. |
| Mega Lucario ex, with three Boss's Orders | **Gengar ex** | *Mega Brave* kills anything, and a parked Mega is 2 Prizes they can drag up and take. The Mega comes only when Haunter hands it over free. |
| Cynthia's Garchomp ex | **Mega Gengar ex, parked** | Concealment still pays against an ex attacker, even though *Draconic Buster* does 640 to a Mega. |
| Single-Prize attackers | **Gengar ex and the dogs, never the Mega** | Concealment never applies, so the Mega is 3 Prizes for nothing. One dragged-up Mega lost the Dhelmise game. |
| Munkidori on their Bench | **Gengar ex first** | Moved counters skip Concealment and *Fainting Spell*, and *Chaotic Pain* is what kills Munkidori. |

Four more rules sit under the table:

- **Search before the Candy.** Play Dawn, Hilda, and Petrel first, then pick the Candy's target. The Zygarde loss spent its Candy on a Gengar ex one card before Hilda brought the Mega.
- **One Mega on the board, never two.** Concealment doesn't stack, so a second one is two more Prizes in the open for nothing.
- **Grimsley's can't reach the hand.** It only looks at the deck. Once both Megas are in hand, the only route left is a Gastly that has sat a turn plus a Rare Candy, so think twice before Hilda pulls a Mega that Grimsley's could have benched.
- **Haunter is the safe step.** When a Gastly is exposed and the Candy has no good target yet, Haunter takes it to 100 HP, stays one Prize, and becomes Gengar ex next turn without a Candy.
- **Blissey ex never takes a Candy.** It is a Stage 1, so a Chansey that has sat a turn evolves by hand, and the Candies stay with the Gastly.

### Where the Energy goes

| Rule | Why |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Musculature takes both Energy, every time.** | It reads "up to 2". Against Crushing Hammer, the spare is what keeps *Chain-Crazed* live after a heads. |
| **Surge the next attacker, and prefer a body that shrugs off 20.** | A benched dog (250) or the parked Mega (350) takes Surge's counters without changing a breakpoint. A benched Haunter is the classic target, because it evolves into a Gengar ex already holding [D]. |
| **Against Fighting and Metal, feed the dogs and never the ghosts.** | Surge's 20 turns a 280 Gengar ex into a 260 one, and a bare *Aura Jab* into Fighting Weakness is exactly 260. So is a *Corkscrew Dive* with one Roserade or Premium Power Pro. Against Mega Excadrill, a Surged Mega Gengar is exactly 330. |
| **Bank a Surge, then *Happy Switch* it forward.** | Surge only reaches the Bench, and *Happy Switch* moves a Basic Energy anywhere, Active included, once a turn for free. Bank on the parked Mega (350) or a benched dog, where the 20 changes nothing. |
| **Bank on Toxtricity itself only when nothing places counters.** | Opponents drag Toxtricity up to strand it, and Energy it banked pays its own Retreat Cost of 2. But Toxtricity lives through *Chaotic Pain* at 140 and dies to it at 120, and that 20 lost the mirror on Live. Once it is Active, it can't Surge itself; Live used to allow that, and a September 2026 update fixed it. |
| **Against Bench damage and counter-multipliers, Surge only what attacks this turn.** | *Jetting Blow*'s 50 plus Surge's 20 is a dead Gastly. *Spiritual End* quadruples counters already on the board, and *Matcha Spin* adds four to everything. |
| ***Happy Switch* or Janine's is how an Active Gengar gets its second Energy.** | Surge never reaches the Active Spot. *Happy Switch* moves one there from any body holding a Basic Energy. Janine's reaches any two Darkness Pokémon, Active or Benched, and Poisons the Active only if it took an Energy. |
| **Retreat is Energy.** | Retreat Costs are paid in Energy, 4 for Blissey ex, 3 for a dog, and 2 for either Gengar, Toxtricity, or Chansey. One Live game burned 7 Energy on retreats. Count every retreat as a self-inflicted Crushing Hammer, and use Switch or AZ's. |

### Who Chaotic Pain hits

| Order | Targets | Why |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Munkidori (110), Froslass (90) | They move or place counters, which switches off Concealment and *Fainting Spell* and undoes damage already dealt. |
| 2 | The Basic or Stage 1 their Mega grows from | A Mega they can't deploy is three Prizes stuck in their hand. The table in [The Thesis](#the-thesis) has every one. |
| 3 | Draw, search, and Energy engines: Lunatone (110), Dunsparce (60 or 70), Metang (100), Kadabra (80), Cynthia's Gabite (100) | Cut the fuel and their big attacker arrives late or not at all. |
| 4 | Eevee, before it becomes Espeon | *Miraculous Shine* devolves every evolved Pokémon the deck has in play. Anything Grimsley's benched never evolved, so it stays. |

- **Read the Ability before picking a target.** One Live loss came from a turn-2 *Chaotic Pain* into a Poltchageist, which ignores it. The Eevee beside it was a free Prize, and the game ended one Prize short.
- **Read their Energy and their Stadium too.** Mist Energy, Rocky Fighting Energy, and Battle Cage each make a target untouchable; [Cards to Watch Out For](#cards-to-watch-out-for) lists every card that does.
- **It can't one-shot** Mew ex (160), Dhelmise (140), Fezandipiti ex (210), a healthy Stage 2 ex, or any Cynthia's Pokémon wearing Cynthia's Power Weight (+70). Alakazam (140) dies to it only after a Risky Ruins chip on the Abra. Crustle (150, or 170 with Growing Grass Energy) needs a chip too, and Haunter's *Spooky Shot* for 40 covers either.
- **Counters ignore Weakness.** Against a Darkness-weak target, a dog or *Void Gale* doubles and *Chaotic Pain* doesn't.

### When to stand and when to pull

| The body | Their next hit | Do this |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A damaged Gengar ex | kills it, and their attacker is a Pokémon ex | **Stand and die.** With the Mega down it costs 1 Prize, and *Fainting Spell* flips at something worth 2 or 3. |
| A Gengar ex at 280 | leaves it alive | Attack, take the hit, then AZ's it home for 80. |
| A damaged Gengar ex | any hit from a single-Prize attacker | **AZ's it**, if 80 keeps it alive. The coin is only for 1 Prize and Concealment pays nothing. |
| An Okidogi ex that dies either way | reaches the Bench too | **Swing on the way out.** *Cruel Arrow*, *Phantom Dive*, and Munkidori all reach the Bench, so retreating a doomed dog costs 3 Energy and an attack for nothing. |
| Mega Gengar ex | anything | Active only for *Void Gale*, then AZ's it back to the Bench. |
| Blissey ex | anything | Bench by default, since *Happy Switch* works from there. In front it is 2 Prizes nothing discounts, so it comes up for *Return* only when the swing finishes something, and leaves by AZ's or Switch. |

### Opening turns

| When | Do this |
| :--- | :--- |
| Setup | Lead Okidogi when the hand has one. Bench every Gastly and Toxel in hand, and a Chansey, which setup puts down before any Ruins can chip it. |
| First turn | Poke Pad and Poffin take Gastly first. Rare Candy needs it to have sat a turn, and Toxtricity and Haunter have no such clock. Grimsley's can't be played. |
| First turn, going second | With a dog, attach, use *Poisonous Musculature*, and take both. With a Toxel, *Call for Family*; [Build order](#build-order) has the picks. Play Lillie's for 8 only when the hand doesn't already hold its line. |
| First turn, going first | Items only. |
| Second turn | Grimsley's comes online. Search first, then Candy. |
| Every turn | Check for lethal before digging. Lillie's shuffles away the whole hand. Then check *Happy Switch* before attacking. |
| Against Judge, Special Red Card, or Unfair Stamp | Bench Pokémon as they're drawn. The Bench can't be shuffled away. |

### Build order

| Order | Build | Found by | Why here |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Gastly, two when the Bench allows | Poffin, Poke Pad, *Call for Family*, Dawn | Rare Candy needs it a turn early, and every attacker and the Mega grow from it |
| 2 | Toxel | Poffin, Poke Pad, *Call for Family*, Dawn | Toxtricity next turn; Surge is the only engine that adds Energy every turn |
| 3 | The first attacker | Candy and Hilda for Gengar ex; *Call for Family* for a dog | Neo Upper makes a fresh Gengar ex swing the turn it lands |
| 4 | Toxtricity | by hand onto a Toxel that sat; Poke Pad, Grimsley's, Hilda | the Surge engine |
| 5 | Mega Gengar ex, against ex attackers | Grimsley's, Candy, Haunter | [Which Stage 2 gets the Candy](#which-stage-2-gets-the-candy) |
| 6 | Chansey, then Blissey ex | Poke Pad, Dawn, *Call for Family*; Hilda for Blissey | *Happy Switch* needs Surge Energy on the Bench to move |
| 7 | The second Toxtricity | by hand, when it's free | a second Surge is good, but on a full Bench the next Gastly keeps the spot |

**Basics on turn 1, the attacker and Toxtricity on turn 2, Blissey once Surge is running.** Everything here evolves from a Basic that sat a turn, so the turn-1 Bench decides turn 2.

- **The order is for searches. Free plays go down whenever.** A Chansey in the opening hand goes to the Bench at setup, where Ruins can't chip it. A Toxtricity in hand over a Toxel that sat evolves at once.
- **Leave a Bench spot for Grimsley's.** It benches a Mega straight out of the deck with no Gastly and no Candy, and a full Bench turns it into a dead Supporter.
- **Okidogi is a lead, not a build.** *Call for Family* takes it only when none is out.
- **Dawn takes an attacker line before the Chansey line.** Gastly or Toxel, then Toxtricity or Haunter, then a Gengar. Chansey, Blissey, and a Gengar wait until Toxtricity has Surged.
- **Two Toxels and two Toxtricity:** evolve both, since they cost nothing, and the next search goes to the attacker, then to Blissey.

**What *Call for Family* takes.** It benches any two Basics, dogs and Chansey included. Before picking, ask whether their next attack can kill a 70 HP Active, because the Toxel doing the calling is the whole engine until a second one lands. That is the one place the call skips the Gastly: the dog is the first attacker and needs no Gastly, and a Gastly in hand still goes down the same turn.

| The board when the Toxel calls | Take |
| :--- | :--- |
| the Toxel alone, going second | Okidogi ex and a Toxel when their next attack can kill it; Okidogi ex and a Gastly when it can't |
| the Toxel alone, going first | a Gastly and a Toxel when it can die; Okidogi ex and a Gastly when it can't |
| a dog already in play | a Gastly and a Toxel |
| never on the first call | Chansey |

- **The calling Toxel is the engine.** When it died on their next turn, a dog-and-Gastly call got Toxtricity down by turn 4 only 37% of the time in the simulator, against 79% when the call took a second Toxel. With a dog already in play, a Gastly and a Toxel matched or beat two Gastly in every opponent model.
- **Going first, the call comes on turn 2,** so a Gastly beats the dog as the Toxel's partner. A Gastly called on turn 2 is a Gengar ex on turn 3; the dog can't swing before turn 4.
- **If the engine dies anyway, [Grimsley's](#grimsleys-move) is the way back.** It benches a Toxtricity straight from the deck, and it Surges that turn.
- **Move the Toxel out of the Active Spot before evolving it.** Toxel retreats for 1 and Toxtricity for 2, and a Toxel that sat still evolves from the Bench. Switch keeps its Energy for a later *Happy Switch*.

### Grimsley's Move odds

| Targets left in the deck | 45 cards left | 35 cards left | 25 cards left |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 2 | 29% | 36% | 49% |
| 3 | 41% | 50% | 65% |
| 4 | 50% | 61% | 76% |
| 5 | 59% | 70% | 84% |
| 6 | 66% | 77% | 90% |

That is the chance the top seven holds at least one target. Playing it later helps only because the deck is smaller, and pulling targets out first hurts it. If Dawn or Hilda already put two Stage 2s in hand, a turn-2 Grimsley's drops from about 59% to about 41%.

- **Count before playing it.** A hit is Mega Gengar, Gengar ex, Toxtricity, or an Okidogi or Haunter when that is what the board is missing. Chansey and Blissey ex never count. With four or fewer left early, play a different Supporter.
- **Play it before the Items that pull its targets out.** Grimsley's first, then Poke Pad for whatever it missed.
- **A miss buries six cards** on the bottom of the deck. The next Surge, Poffin, Poke Pad, or Petrel shuffles them back in.
- **For one exact card, use Petrel, Dawn, or Hilda.** Grimsley's is a roughly 60% card, built for the turns when any of several Pokémon will do.

## The Prize Tax

*Shadowy Concealment* decides how the Bench gets built, so the arithmetic is worth doing.

| Board, against a Pokémon ex attacker | What it pays them |
| :--- | ---: |
| Mega Gengar ex, Gengar ex, Okidogi ex, Toxtricity, Gastly | 2 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 0 = **4** |
| Gengar ex, Okidogi ex, Toxtricity, Gastly, with no Mega | 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = **6**, the whole game |
| Mega Gengar ex, two Gengar ex, Okidogi ex, Toxtricity | 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 0 = **5** |
| Two Mega Gengar ex, two Gengar ex, Okidogi ex | 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 = **7** |
| Mega Gengar ex, Gengar ex, Blissey ex, Toxtricity, Gastly | 2 + 1 + 2 + 0 + 0 = **5** |
| Mega Gengar ex, Gengar ex, Okidogi ex, Chansey, Gastly | 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 0 = **5** |

**Every benched Pokémon ex is a hole in the tax, and Blissey ex is the one the Mega can't patch.** It is Colorless, so it costs its full 2 with the Mega down, and Chansey costs 1. Keep one Mega on the board, one Blissey ex, at most one other ex beside them, and zero-Prize bodies everywhere else.

### Stand and die

| | |
| :--- | ---: |
| Cost of letting a Gengar ex die, with the Mega on the Bench | **1 Prize** |
| Chance *Fainting Spell* Knocks Out the attacker | **50%** |
| What that attacker is usually worth | **2 or 3 Prizes** |

That is about one Prize of expected value per death, in the deck's favor. On Live the coin took a Mega Zygarde, a Mega Floette, and a Dhelmise. Retreating throws away both halves and costs Energy on top.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Concealment only covers Knock Outs by damage from an attack by a Pokémon ex.** It does nothing against:
>
> - single-Prize attackers like Dhelmise, Alakazam, Slowking, Crustle, and Spiritomb.
> - counters placed by an attack, like *Powerful Hand*, *Spiritual End*, *Matcha Spin*, and the Bench half of *Phantom Dive*.
> - counters moved by an Ability. Munkidori silences *Fainting Spell* too.
> - Poison and other damage between turns.
>
> In those rooms this is a midrange deck in an honest Prize race, and the Mega stays in the deck.

## The Energy Engine

Nine sources put Energy where it matters, and only the attachments from the hand spend a card.

| Source | From | To | Costs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Manual attachment | hand | anywhere | the attachment for the turn |
| Neo Upper Energy | hand | a Stage 2, as two of any type | the attachment for the turn |
| *Poisonous Musculature* | deck, two | the Active dog | its attack; Poisons it |
| *Sinister Surge* | deck | a Benched Darkness Pokémon, once per Toxtricity | two damage counters |
| *Happy Switch* | a Basic Energy already in play | any other Pokémon, Active included | nothing; once per turn per Blissey ex |
| Janine's Secret Art | deck, one each | up to two Darkness Pokémon | the turn's Supporter; Poisons the Active if it took one |
| *Void Gale* | the Mega | a Benched Pokémon | nothing extra; it rides the attack |
| *Lucky Attachment* | hand | any Pokémon | Chansey's attack |
| Hilda | deck | the hand | the turn's Supporter |

*Chaotic Pain* and *Void Gale* cost two, which the engine reaches in one turn, and Neo Upper reaches in one card. *Chain-Crazed* costs three, which *Musculature* delivers on its own a turn ahead. *Happy Switch* adds no Energy, but it puts the Energy Surge banked on the Bench where the attack is.

**Four things drain the pool.** Knock Outs bury whatever the Pokémon held. Retreat Costs bury 4 from Blissey ex, 3 from a dog, and 2 from a Gengar or Toxtricity. Crushing Hammer buries one on every heads, and Toxtricity Box runs four. *Musculature* pulls two out of the deck at a time. Switch and AZ's keep the retreat bill off the pool, and [Energy Recycler](#energy-recycler) puts the rest back where the engines look.

**Against Crushing Hammer, the spare matters.** A dog with four Energy still swings after one heads; a dog with three doesn't.

# Game Plans

## 1. Turn one, going second

Going second, the deck can attack on its first turn, and it has two openers.

**With a dog and an Energy**, attach, use *Poisonous Musculature*, and take both. The dog ends the turn holding three and Poisoned, and turn 2 is *Chain-Crazed* for 260.

**With a Toxel instead**, attach and *Call for Family*. What it takes depends on whether their next attack can kill the Toxel; [Build order](#build-order) has the table.

**With only a Chansey up front**, attach and *Lucky Attachment*. That puts a second Energy from the hand on the Gastly or dog behind it, which is the best a Chansey opener does.

Either way, bench every Gastly the hand or a Poffin can reach, because turn 1's Gastly is turn 2's Gengar. Play Lillie's while it still draws eight, unless the hand already holds its line. Grimsley's can't be played yet.

## 2. Turn one, going first

**No attack, no Supporter, and no Grimsley's, but every Item is still legal.** Poffin and Poke Pad take Gastly first. A Hilda or a Dawn in the opening hand is turn 2's play, and it goes down before the Candy does.

## 3. Rearming after a knockout

The attacker dies. The question is whether the next one is ready.

**If one is, promote it.** A Haunter that Surge fed evolves into a Gengar ex already holding Energy. A benched dog with Energy on it comes up swinging.

**If a Gengar ex is Active and one Energy short, the first out is *Happy Switch*.** Any benched body holding a Basic Energy can give it up, and it costs no card. The second out is Janine's, which puts an Energy on each of two Darkness Pokémon, the Active included. Play one of them instead of passing with a loaded Gengar doing nothing.

**If nothing is ready, send out a dog.** It needs no line and no Candy. One Energy and *Musculature* give it three and a Poison, and 250 HP buys the turn the ghosts need.

**Use Switch or AZ's, not Retreat.** Rotating by retreat discards Energy equal to the Retreat Cost, and this board's run from 1 for a Gastly to 4 for Blissey ex.

## 4. Getting the Mega down

Against Pokémon ex attackers, the Mega is due before anything on the board takes a real hit. There are three routes, fastest first.

1. **Grimsley's Move, from the second turn.** Straight from the deck to the Bench, with no Gastly or Candy spent. Count the targets first; [the odds table](#grimsleys-move-odds) has the numbers.
2. **Rare Candy on a Gastly that has sat a turn.** Search first, so the Candy goes on the right Stage 2.
3. **Haunter, the turn after.** Slow but free, and a benched Haunter can take a Surge on the way.

**Keep one Mega in the deck while Grimsley's is the route.** A Mega in hand needs a Gastly and a Candy. The Zygarde loss had both Megas in hand by turn 3 and no Gastly to put them on.

The exceptions are in [Which Stage 2 gets the Candy](#which-stage-2-gets-the-candy). Never build it against single-Prize attackers, and build Gengar ex first against Munkidori and against Mega Lucario.

## 5. When the dog gets gusted

They drag a Gastly into the Active Spot. The dog is on the Bench with its Energy and without its Poison, and it costs three Energy to retreat.

**Often, do nothing.** The Gastly dies for zero Prizes under Concealment, and the dog comes back up next turn having lost only the Poison.

**When waiting isn't an option**, use Switch or AZ's, never Retreat. Then Janine's restores the Poison, and *Chain-Crazed* is 260 again.

**When the dog itself is doomed, swing with it.** A dog that retreats to the Bench still dies there to *Cruel Arrow*, *Phantom Dive*, or Munkidori, and it paid three Energy for nothing. The Toxtricity Box game retreated a Poisoned dog holding four Energy instead of hitting their 120 HP Scrafty for 260, and the dog died on the Bench a turn later.

## 6. Choosing the stadium

Two Risky Ruins, and Petrel works like a third, so the question is usually when, not which. [Risky Ruins](#risky-ruins) has the table of what to replace.

- **Lead it against evolution decks.** The 20 on every Basic they bench carries through evolution.
- **Bench the Chansey first.** The deck's own Ruins chips a Chansey benched under it, and the 20 follows it into Blissey ex.
- **Hold one for Battle Cage or Gravity Mountain**, and replace them the turn before they matter. Jamming Tower can stay; this list has no Tool for it to stop.
- **Against four-copy Stadium decks, treat it as a tax.** Beedrill has to play a Forest every turn to keep going; make it spend one.
- **Against Dragapult, don't bother.** Their Ruins blocks this one by name, and on this Bench it only chips a Chansey.
- **Against Neutralization Zone, replace it once.** It is an ACE SPEC and can't come back.

## 7. Bench discipline

Keep the board's payout at four or less against ex attackers.

- One Mega Gengar ex, never two.
- One Blissey ex, never two. It is 2 Prizes the Mega can't discount.
- One dog in play. The second waits until the first is gone.
- At most one other Pokémon ex beside the Mega and Blissey.
- A spare Gastly, because that is next turn's Gengar. Against *Jetting Blow* and *Phantom Dive*, bench small Basics only when they evolve next turn.
- Against Judge, Special Red Card, and Unfair Stamp, bench Pokémon as they're drawn. Hands full of held Pokémon got shuffled away in the N's Zoroark, Mega Lucario, and Toxtricity Box games.

## 8. The ferry

*Void Gale* has always pushed an Energy to the Bench. Blissey ex brings it back, so the ferry runs both ways.

1. **Surge a benched body that carries the 20.** The parked Mega, or a dog. Not Toxtricity against *Chaotic Pain*.
2. ***Happy Switch* that Energy onto the attacker**, then make the turn's attachment. The Active gains two Energy, and only one came from the hand.
3. **Swing.** A *Void Gale* sends one Energy back to the Bench, and next turn's *Happy Switch* returns it.

**The mirror loss ran it on turn 5.** A Surge and a *Happy Switch* put two Energy on a benched Mega with none in hand, Switch brought it up, and *Void Gale* took Pecharunt ex. The opponent in the first mirror loss ran the same loop with their own Blissey and swung *Void Gale* five turns running.

**The ferry only works while Blissey ex lives on the Bench.** It is 2 Prizes and an easy Boss's Orders target, so the board keeps a second Energy route ready: Janine's, Neo Upper on the next Stage 2, or a Haunter that Surge fed.
