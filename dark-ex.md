## What The Research Says

Mega Gengar ex has three homes in the August 2026 Standard format, and none of them is "Mega Gengar plus whatever."

**A package inside N's Zoroark ex.** The most common competitive home. Zoroark draws and attacks cheap; a 2-2 Gengar line fixes the prize race. [City League Tokyo, 3rd place, March 2026](https://limitlesstcg.com/decks/list/jp/64649).

**A package inside Marnie's Grimmsnarl ex.** The best-performing variant. Grimmsnarl chips, three Munkidori relocate the chip, Gengar tanks. [City League Iwate, 1st place, February 2026](https://limitlesstcg.com/decks/list/jp/60245).

**The standalone dark box.** Gengar as the centerpiece, Toxtricity as the engine, one-prize Darkness attackers doing the trading. This is the budget archetype and the one this file builds, because it is the shape your binder already holds. [Limitless archetype page](https://limitlesstcg.com/decks/358), and a [current-format online list](https://play.limitlesstcg.com/decks/mega-gengar-ex?format=standard) it borrows from.

Be honest about the ceiling. The standalone box is a league-night deck, not a Regional winner; the two shells above are where the card wins events. What this build has that they do not is **Seviper**, and Seviper is the best reason to run the standalone version at all.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Format check, August 2026.** Standard is regulation marks **H, I, and J** ([2026 rotation announcement](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-news/2026-pokemon-tcg-standard-format-rotation-announcement)). Every card in this list carries one of the three. The H cards, marked in the deck list, rotate out around April 2027; the deck survives that with swaps, but know it going in.

## Key Card Text

Generic staples are covered card by card in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md); this section covers what is new or non-obvious here.

### Mega Gengar ex — Phantasmal Flames 056 · Reg I

**Stage 2 from Haunter. Gives up 3 Prize cards.** Unlike the XY-era "M" cards, playing it does not end your turn; it evolves like any other Stage 2. ([Bulbapedia](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Mega_Gengar_ex_(Phantasmal_Flames_56)))

Read *Void Gale* precisely. It moves **one Energy, of any type**, from this Pokémon to a Benched Pokémon. Attack for 230, then bank an Energy on whoever attacks next. The attack never strands its own fuel.

Three printings exist and they are the same card: Phantasmal Flames 056 (cheapest), Ascended Heroes 125, and the Mega Evolution promo 073. They share one four-copy limit because the limit counts names. Ignore anything numbered above the set size; those are the collector prints.

> [!TIP]
> **"Mega Gengar ex" and "Gengar" are different names**, so this list legally runs three of one and one of the other, and both evolve from the same Haunter line. One Gastly feeds either payoff.

### Gastly — Phantasmal Flames 054 · Reg I

The Basic everything is built on, and deliberately the **Phantasmal Flames print**, because Gengar Gang runs the Perfect Order print. Keeping the two decks on different printings means both can stay sleeved at once. Same 70 HP, so it stays a legal Buddy-Buddy Poffin target.

### Haunter — Perfect Order 049 · Reg J

***Haunt* places 3 damage counters for one Energy**, and placed counters ignore Weakness and Resistance. This print replaces the *Spooky Shot* Haunter from the first draft; a flat 40 is fine, but 30 that finishes what *Void Gale* started is better. The chip math section below is built on this card.

### Gengar — Perfect Order 050 · Reg J

The 1-prize attacker with *Infinite Shadow*, shared with Gengar Gang. You own three; this deck borrows one.

> [!WARNING]
> *Infinite Shadow* returns the card to your hand when it is Knocked Out. **It does not deny the Prize.** The zero-prize trick on this card comes from *Shadowy Concealment*, and only when the thing that Knocked it Out was an opposing Pokémon ex.

### Toxel — Phantasmal Flames 067 · Reg I

The bottom of the Energy engine, and *Call for Family* is a real turn-one attack. Going second with nothing better to do, fetching two Basics onto the Bench beats 10 damage every time.

### Toxtricity — Phantasmal Flames 068 · Reg I

The engine. ***Sinister Surge*** attaches a Basic Darkness Energy from the deck to one of your Benched **Darkness** Pokémon, then puts 2 damage counters on that Pokémon. Once per turn, no attack spent, no Supporter spent.

The drawback is smaller than it reads. The counters land on a 350 HP Mega that shrugs them off, or they become Munkidori ammunition and get thrown back at the opponent.

Note the word *Darkness*. Sinister Surge cannot feed Munkidori, who is Psychic; his Energy arrives by hand attachment or Energy Switch.

### Seviper — Phantasmal Flames 062 · Reg I

**The best reason to build the standalone box.** *Excited Power* adds 120 to Seviper's attacks while you have any Darkness Mega Evolution Pokémon ex in play, so *Pitch-Black Fangs* hits for **240** off a 120 HP Basic that gives up **one** Prize, and **zero** to an ex under *Shadowy Concealment*.

240 one-shots Dragapult ex's support pieces, every Fezandipiti, and most Stage 1 attackers in the format. Your opponent spends a 2-prize attacker to answer it and collects nothing. That trade, repeated, is the whole deck.

Three Energy is real money. Toxtricity feeds it on the Bench, Void Gale banks Energy onto it, and Energy Switch tops it off.

### Sableye — Phantasmal Flames 059 · Reg I

*Cocky Claw* does 90 for a single Energy while a Stage 2 Darkness Pokémon sits **on your Bench**. The condition describes this deck's default board: Mega Gengar resting in the back while a one-prizer fights. A 90-damage, one-Energy, one-prize attacker is a fine early lead and a fine late-game finisher.

### Munkidori — Twilight Masquerade 095 · Reg H

***Adrena-Brain*** moves up to 3 damage counters from one of your Pokémon to one of your opponent's, every turn, while Munkidori has Darkness Energy attached. Toxtricity's self-damage becomes free spread; Void Gale's 230 becomes 260.

One honest caveat the first draft missed. **Munkidori is Psychic, not Darkness**, so *Shadowy Concealment* does not protect him and *Sinister Surge* cannot fuel him. He is worth the slot anyway; every serious Gengar list runs one to three.

### Pecharunt ex — Shrouded Fable 039 · Reg H

***Subjugating Chains* is a free pivot, once per turn**: switch a Benched Darkness Pokémon (not a Pecharunt ex) into the Active Spot, and it becomes Poisoned. The poison is ten a turn on a 350 HP Mega, a price worth paying to bring the right attacker up without spending retreat or a Switch.

*Irritated Outburst* does 60 for each Prize your opponent has taken. In a deck built to slow their Prize count it is a late-game button, not a plan.

### Fezandipiti ex — Shrouded Fable 038 · Reg H

*Flip the Script* draws 3 whenever one of your Pokémon was Knocked Out on their last turn, and this deck plans to lose a cheap body most turns. A 2-prize liability that Concealment discounts to 1.

### Grimsley's Move — Phantasmal Flames 090 · Reg I

Look at the top 7, bench a Darkness Pokémon you find. Not usable turn one. It is the Supporter version of what the deck already wants: more bodies, fewer dead turns. You own one from the Mega Battle Deck.

### Energy Switch — Mega Evolution 115 · Reg I

Moves a **basic** Energy between your Pokémon. It cannot move Legacy Energy. This is how a stranded Energy reaches Seviper, and how Munkidori gets his Darkness without costing your attachment for the turn.

### Air Balloon — Ascended Heroes 181 · Reg I

Retreat cost minus two. It lives on Mega Gengar, whose retreat of 2 becomes free. Your two Japanese starter-set copies are the same card but are not legal at US events; this buys one English print.

### Legacy Energy — Twilight Masquerade 167 · Reg H

The ACE SPEC. It provides one Energy of any type, and **if the Pokémon holding it is Knocked Out by an attack, the opponent takes one fewer Prize card, once per game.**

That clause stacks with *Shadowy Concealment*. A Seviper holding Legacy Energy, Knocked Out by an opposing ex, awards **zero** prizes twice over; a Fezandipiti ex holding it awards zero; Mega Gengar itself holding it awards one instead of three. Night Stretcher cannot recover it, so the once per game is truly once.

## The Real Card Is the Ability, Not the Attack

*Void Gale* is a fine attack. ***Shadowy Concealment* is the deck.**

> If 1 of your Darkness Pokémon is Knocked Out **by damage from an attack from your opponent's Pokémon ex**, that player takes 1 fewer Prize card. The effect doesn't stack.

Here is the board it creates when your opponent attacks with ex Pokémon, which is most of the format:

| Your Pokémon | Prizes normally | Prizes when KO'd by an opposing ex |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Gastly, Haunter, Gengar, Toxel, Toxtricity, Seviper, Sableye | 1 | **0** |
| Munkidori *(Psychic, not covered)* | 1 | 1 |
| Fezandipiti ex, Pecharunt ex | 2 | **1** |
| **Mega Gengar ex** | 3 | **2** |

They Knock Out your 240-damage Seviper with a Dragapult ex and collect nothing. You have deleted a Prize card from every exchange on the board, and Legacy Energy deletes one more, once, wherever it hurts most.

> [!WARNING]
> **Read the trigger twice.** The Knock Out must come from an attack, by a Pokémon ex. Against a deck that attacks with non-ex Pokémon, *Shadowy Concealment* does nothing at all. Roughly a third of the current meta attacks with single-prize Pokémon precisely to beat abilities like this one. [Game plan 5](#5-when-concealment-is-blank) is about those games.

## Game Plans

---

### 1. The Energy River

Energy in this deck flows in a loop, and the loop is the engine.

```
deck  --Sinister Surge-->  benched Darkness Pokémon  (+2 counters, once per turn)
hand  --attachment----->   whoever attacks next
Mega Gengar --Void Gale--> benched attacker  (every attack, free)
board --Energy Switch--->  wherever the math needs it
discard --Night Stretcher-> hand
```

Toxtricity is the only piece that needs setting up, and it is a Stage 1 off a Basic that searches itself with *Call for Family*. Get one Toxtricity going by turn two or three and the deck never spends a hand attachment on Seviper again; Sinister Surge and Void Gale carry the fuel to the front while your one attachment per turn goes to the next Mega or to Munkidori.

The counters Sinister Surge leaves behind are not a cost. They are Munkidori's ammunition, thrown at the opponent three at a time.

### 2. Seviper Does the Fighting

The deck's default posture, most turns of most games. **Mega Gengar sits on the Bench; Seviper sits in front.**

While the Mega is in play, Seviper hits 240 and gives up nothing to an ex. So the opponent faces a choice with no good answer. Kill the Seviper and collect zero Prizes; ignore it and lose a 2-prize attacker every other turn; or come after Mega Gengar on the Bench with a gust effect, which is the one line you must respect. When they spend Boss's Orders dragging your Mega forward, that is your cue to be somewhere else next turn: Pecharunt pivots it back out for free.

Send Mega Gengar itself forward when the numbers demand 230, when a second Seviper is not ready, or when 350 HP needs to absorb two hits while the Bench rebuilds. Just count first. Its death hands over 2 Prizes even under its own ability, a third of their game in one Knock Out.

### 3. Chip Math

The two-shot problem from the first draft is still the deck's central math problem. *Void Gale* does 230 and the format's big Megas carry 330 to 380 HP. The fix is that this list now places free damage counters from three directions, and counters ignore Weakness and Resistance.

| Source | Counters per turn | Cost |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Haunt (Haunter) | 3 | one Energy and your attack |
| Adrena-Brain (Munkidori) | 3, moved from your board | free |
| Sinister Surge (Toxtricity) | 2, onto your own Pokémon | free, becomes Adrena-Brain ammo |

The standard kill sequences, worth memorizing:

- **230 + 30 = 260.** Void Gale plus one Adrena-Brain. Kills Fezandipiti ex (210) with room, kills Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex (280) with one more chip.
- **240 + 30 + 30 = 300.** Seviper plus Haunt plus Adrena-Brain across two turns. Kills Crobat ex (310) with one more counter, kills every Stage 1 ex in the format.
- **Two Void Gales = 460.** Nothing printed survives it. The question is only whether Mega Gengar survives the turn in between, and at 350 HP against most attackers, it does.

Haunter earns its Active turns early, before Rare Candy arrives. Three counters a turn from a 100 HP body your opponent feels silly killing is how 230 becomes lethal later.

### 4. The Pecharunt Pivot

*Subjugating Chains* is the deck's tempo. Once every turn, for free: the damaged Mega walks to the Bench and a fresh Seviper walks out Poisoned, or the Seviper that just traded walks back and Mega Gengar comes forward with Air Balloon already attached.

The Poison costs 10 a turn. On a 350 HP Mega or a Seviper that expects to trade anyway, that is nothing; on a 70 HP Gastly it is not, so pivot the big bodies, not the seeds.

The pivot also un-does the opponent's Boss's Orders. They drag Mega Gengar Active to force bad trades; you pivot it right back out on your turn without spending a card. That exchange, their Supporter against your free ability, wins long games.

### 5. When Concealment Is Blank

Against single-prize decks the ability does nothing, and this is the matchup class the deck genuinely fears. The plan changes.

**Stop leading with the Mega.** Against one-prize attackers, Mega Gengar is pure liability; two Knock Outs on it is their whole game. It becomes a Bench-sitting battery for Seviper's ability and Void Gale turns only when lethal.

**Trade one for one with better bodies.** Seviper at 240 and Sableye at 90-for-one out-stat almost every single-prize attacker in the format. Without Concealment the deck is still a fair deck with above-rate attackers; play the fair game.

**Spend Legacy Energy here.** Its prize denial works against any attack Knock Out, not just ex attacks. Against single-prize decks it is the only denial you have; make the once per game count.

That is a real league-night deck and a fringe tournament deck, which matches its [online record](https://play.limitlesstcg.com/decks/mega-gengar-ex?format=standard).

## Versus the Kitchen Table

**Fox's Team Rocket's Mewtwo deck is the matchup this deck was born for.** Nearly every attacker in it is a Pokémon ex, so *Shadowy Concealment* is always on. His Mewtwo Knocks Out your Seviper and collects zero Prizes; you answer with 230 plus chip into his 280. His Crobat ex at 310 needs the two-turn sequence from game plan 3. The one thing to respect is Erasure Ball's 280 ceiling; it two-shots your Mega, so keep the pivot loop running and make him hit a different 350 each time.

**Fox's Charizard deck turns Concealment off completely.** No ex attackers, no discount. You still have a 240 Seviper into a field of 170 HP Pokémon, so the games are fine; they are just fair. *Royal Blaze* with one Leon discarded one-shots everything here except Mega Gengar, so expect one-for-one trades and win them on stats.

## How To Play ex Style

**This is the part that transfers.** Everything below applies to any ex deck, including Fox's.

### 1. You are not fighting. You are racing to six.

The single biggest mental shift from the casual deck. In Gengar Gang every Pokémon is worth 1 Prize, so "knock out more stuff than they do" and "win" are the same sentence. In an ex format they come apart:

| Pokémon type | Prizes |
| :--- | :--- |
| Non-ex | **1** |
| Pokémon ex | **2** |
| **Mega Evolution Pokémon ex** | **3** |

**Two Mega Knock Outs is a whole game.** You are not playing a war of attrition; you are playing a sprint where each turn is worth a third of the game.

### 2. Build a Prize map before you start

Before the first attack, decide which six Prizes you are taking. Three Knock Outs on their 2-prize attackers is the standard plan. Two on their Megas is faster if the chip math is there. Six on single-prize Pokémon is a race you will lose; do not plan it.

Then ask the mirror question, which *Shadowy Concealment* rewrites in your favor: which of yours can they afford to Knock Out? In this deck the answer is "almost everything," and that asymmetry is the strategy.

### 3. The trade is the whole game

| You lose | They lose | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1-prizer (0 under Concealment) | their 2-prize ex | **The dream.** This deck's whole design. |
| 2-prize ex | their 2-prize ex | Even. Fine. |
| **3-prize Mega** | their 2-prize ex | **Bad.** You won the fight and lost the exchange. |
| 1-prizer | nothing | Bad, unless it bought setup. |

### 4. The two-shot problem beats HP every time

If your attacker needs two turns to Knock Out and theirs needs one, you lose, regardless of HP. Chip damage is not a nice extra in ex decks; it is the difference between one-shotting and losing. This deck's answer is [game plan 3](#3-chip-math).

### 5. Gust effects are the most valuable cards in the format

**Boss's Orders wins more ex games than any attacker**, because in an ex format the whole game is deciding what you are allowed to Knock Out. This list runs three, and drawing one starts a plan; it does not fill a turn.

### 6. Rules that bite harder in ex decks

- Going first, you cannot attack. Going second, Toxel's *Call for Family* makes the free attack a real turn.
- **ACE SPEC: exactly one per deck, total.** Not one per name.
- *Shadowy Concealment* does not stack. Three Megas in play still reduce by exactly 1.
- Rule Box Pokémon cannot be discarded to Gwynn. This deck does not run her, and her presence in Gengar Gang is one more reason the two decks stay separate.

### 7. Errors to expect on the way over

| Habit from Gengar Gang | Why it fails in ex |
| :--- | :--- |
| Leading with the best attacker | It is worth 3 Prizes. Lead with something cheap. |
| Trading freely because everything is 1 Prize | Trades have prices now. Score them first. |
| Building the widest possible Bench | Wide boards feed Dragapult's spread and every Bench-damage attack in the format. |
| Treating chip damage as optional | It is how 230 beats 350. |
| Saving Boss's Orders for the end | The end arrives on turn four. |
