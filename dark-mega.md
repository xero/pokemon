## The Thesis

**Seviper is the only card in Standard whose text names a Darkness Mega Evolution Pokémon ex, and we own the Mega it names.** _Excited Power_ adds 120 to Seviper's attacks while Mega Gengar ex is anywhere in play, so _Pitch-Black Fangs_ swings for **240 from a body worth one Prize**. The Mega walls at 350, _Shadowy Concealment_ shrinks every Prize the opponent tries to take, and the snake does the killing they cannot afford to punish.

The third build changes how the snake gets fed and what fights on the turns it is not ready. **Two Toxtricity on the Bench is two Surges a turn.** _Sinister Surge_ reads "Once during your turn, you may use this Ability" and stops there; it does not carry the "you can't use more than 1" clause that Fezandipiti's _Flip the Script_ does, so each Toxtricity is its own Energy from the deck every turn. With the hand attachment and _Void Gale_'s export, four Energy can land on the Bench in one turn. That is how a 3-Energy attacker gets paid on schedule, and how the second snake stays loaded behind the first.

**Chi-Yu is the cheap attacker.** One Surge onto a Benched Chi-Yu hands it the one Energy and the two damage counters _Whirling Envy_ wants, and it steps forward hitting 110. It costs a quarter of what the snake costs, it has no Ability so it is the only attacker here that damages Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon ex, and when it dies it takes one Energy to the discard instead of three. [Game plan 3](#3-how-to-play-chi-yu) is the manual for it.

_Shadowy Concealment_ is still the spine. When a Darkness Pokémon of yours is Knocked Out by damage from an opposing Pokémon ex's attack, they take one fewer Prize card, and **every Pokémon in this 60 is Darkness**. Against a deck that attacks with ex Pokémon, the math collapses:

| Your Pokémon | Prizes normally | KO'd by their ex's attack |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Gastly, Haunter, Toxel, Toxtricity, Seviper, Chi-Yu | 1 | **0** |
| Gengar ex | 2 | **1** |
| Mega Gengar ex | 3 | **2** |

The [Rulings Compendium](https://compendium.pokegym.net/category/4-abilities/shadowy-concealment/) confirms the two clauses this deck leans on. The Ability applies to the Mega itself, so an ex that Knocks Out Mega Gengar ex takes two Prizes, not three. And it only fires on Knock Outs from **attack damage by a Pokémon ex**: damage counters, Special Conditions, instant Knock Out effects, and attacks from non-ex Pokémon all collect full Prizes. Both halves matter tonight, because the field's biggest decks split exactly along that line.

Add it up. Their six Prizes against this list are **three Mega Knock Outs**, because two Megas and the Gengar ex only reach five. Night Stretcher rebuilds a fallen Mega four times over, and Wally's Compassion heals a standing one to full. Every ex they play is worth two or three Prizes to a snake that costs them nothing to lose.

Against a deck that attacks with single-prize Pokémon, Concealment is a blank card and the Mega is a three-Prize gift. The first build handed those rooms to [Gengar Gang](./dark.md) entirely; this build keeps Gengar ex and Chi-Yu for them, and [game plan 4](#4-reading-the-room) is how you tell which room you are in before the Candy is spent.

### Prizes

Their six against this list, when their attackers are ex: **three Mega Knock Outs**, at two apiece. Two Megas plus the Gengar ex is five. Every other body pays nothing. Your six against them: two Mega kills, or one Mega and two ex, or three ex.

The cage holds only while a Mega is **in play**, Bench or Active. When the last Mega leaves, every one of your Pokémon goes back to full price. Keep a Gastly on the Bench so the next Mega arrives before the current one dies.

Twenty-two out, twenty-two in, and the other thirty-eight never move: 4 Gastly, 2 Haunter, 2 Toxel, 2 Toxtricity, 3 Dawn, 2 Lillie's, 2 Boss's, 3 Poffin, 3 Rare Candy, 4 Night Stretcher, 1 Switch, 2 Risky Ruins, and 8 Basic Darkness. The ACE SPEC changes with the mode, Scramble Switch in the Gang and Prime Catcher here, so count to exactly one before you shuffle.

Three things fall out of the swap.

**The plain Gengar leaves entirely.** The second build kept two for the single-prize rooms. This build hands that job to Gengar ex and Chi-Yu, because a Stage 2 that needs a Gastly and a Candy every time it comes back was not the repeatable cheap attacker it looked like. A Basic that Night Stretcher returns is.

**Shadowy Darkness Energy leaves.** Surge cannot search it, Night Stretcher cannot return it, and the whole trainer line is now built around Energy that can be recovered. The turn-one Gastly is less safe for it; Rare Candy it early.

**The Stadium war is even again.** Two Risky Ruins in both modes, so Eternatus is not missed.

> [!TIP]
> **Optional two-card sub.** The binder holds two Perfect Order Haunter (_Haunt_: place 3 damage counters on their Active). Swapping them in for the two Phantasmal Flames copies turns the middle stage into a counter-placer, and placed counters ignore Weakness, Resistance, and Tera Bench protection. Haunt is exactly the 30 that turns Fangs into a 270 kill on Flareon ex, Ceruledge ex, or Milotic ex. Skip the sub when teaching; take it when playing to win.

### Gastly

The same four Perfect Order copies, doing the same job: sit on the Bench and become a ghost. The menu is shorter now, a 350 HP wall or a 310 HP Energy tax, and the choice waits until the Rare Candy is played. Four copies feed at most four ghosts a game, so every Candy is a real decision, and the ghost that matters most is the second Mega, the one that has to be standing before the first one falls.

Gastly is Fighting-weak and 70 HP, and it has no Ability, so Froslass ignores it and Cornerstone cannot block it. Neither fact will ever matter; it exists to evolve. Against Dragapult, bench it and Candy it the same turn you can, because six Phantom Dive counters leave it on 10.

The backup route when Rare Candy is late. Two copies, Phantasmal Flames, flat 40, and Dawn's Stage 1 slot finds it when the hand has the Mega but no Candy. See [The Mode Swap](#the-mode-swap) for the _Haunt_ substitution.

Two rather than the Live list's three, because Dawn's middle slot is usually better spent on Toxtricity, and three Candy plus two Haunter is five routes for the two or three ghosts a game actually evolves.

### Mega Gengar ex

**The wall the cage leans on.** 350 HP, Stage 2 from Haunter, worth three Prizes when it falls and two under its own Ability. It evolves like any other Stage 2; modern Mega cards do not end your turn.

**_Shadowy Concealment_ is the deck.** One fewer Prize whenever your Darkness Pokémon dies to an opposing ex's attack, and every Pokémon here is Darkness. It works from the Bench and the Active Spot alike, it covers the Mega itself, and it does not stack. It does **not** cover Knock Outs from damage counters, Special Conditions, or non-ex attackers; the field section names the decks that use those.

**_Void Gale_ is 230, then it moves an Energy from this Pokémon to one of your Benched Pokémon.** The move is not optional, and this deck turns the tax into the engine: aim the export at the charging Seviper. Attack with the Mega and you are simultaneously loading the snake. That loop is [game plan 2](#2-the-charmers-loop).

**Three copies, because 27% of games prize one.** The honest damage number has not changed: 230 one-shots nothing in the current ex tier except what Weakness doubles it into and what Ruins softens, so the Mega is a wall that counterattacks, not a sweeper. Everything it fails to kill is 230 closer to Fangs or 110 away from Chi-Yu. Air Balloon lives on this card so the wall steps back for free.

### Gengar ex

**The tax ghost, and the second Candy target.** Stage 2 from the same Haunter, 310 HP, worth two Prizes normally and one under the cage.

**_Gnawing Curse_ punishes the fundamental action of the game.** Whenever your opponent attaches an Energy card from their hand to one of their Pokémon, 2 damage counters go on that Pokémon. It runs from your Bench all game. It is brutal against Festival Lead, where Dipplin's _Energy Loop_ returns Energy to hand every attack and the re-attachment bites again, and against any deck whose acceleration is hand-based.

**_Tricky Steps_ is 160 and a disruption.** You may move an Energy from their Active to a Benched Pokémon of your choice. 160 is the number that clears the single-prize rooms' bodies: Dusknoir, Hariyama, Toucannon, Alakazam, and Dudunsparce all fall to it, and none of them fall to Chi-Yu.

One copy, because it is the second ghost, not the first, and the Basics it gave up fixed the mulligan rate. Candy it against single-prize rooms and hand-attachment engines; leave it in the deck against decks that accelerate from the discard or the deck, which never trigger it. [Curse Toll](./dark-curse.md) runs the pair.

### Toxel

**Three copies, and the deck's best going-second play.** _Call for Family_ benches any two Basics with no HP cap, which here means the Seviper and Chi-Yu that Poffin cannot fetch. Going second with Toxel Active, that attack builds the whole Bench on turn one.

Three copies because the engine wants two Toxtricity standing, and because every Basic in this list pulls the mulligan rate down: 35% at the eight Basics the sleeved list had, 19% at twelve. It has no Ability, so Froslass leaves it alone and Cornerstone cannot stop its 20; neither will come up.

Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#toxel).

### Toxtricity

**Three copies, and the third is the whole reason the list changed.** _Sinister Surge_ is one Basic Darkness from the deck onto a Benched Darkness Pokémon, once per turn per Toxtricity, plus 2 damage counters of rent on whatever it feeds. Two Toxtricity on the Bench is two Energy a turn from the deck. Read the printed text: it says "Once during your turn, you may use this Ability" and does not add the once-per-turn-per-name clause Fezandipiti carries, so both fire.

Surge feeds **Benched Darkness Pokémon only**, and everything here qualifies. The targets, in order:

- **The charging Seviper.** Three Energy is the price of 240, and two Surges plus a Void Gale export pay it in one turn.
- **A Benched Chi-Yu.** One Surge is the entire setup: one Energy, two counters, 110 next turn. Never Surge it twice unless you want the second Energy for a retreat.
- **The Benched Mega.** So _Void Gale_ has an Energy to export without spending the hand attachment.

Surge searches the **deck**, which is why Energy Recycler is in this list and Energy Retrieval is not. Ten Energy start in the deck; two Surges a turn empty it fast, and the Recycler refills it from the discard.

Toxtricity has an Ability, so Froslass's _Freezing Shroud_ ticks it every Checkup and Cornerstone blocks its 100. Neither changes what it is for.

Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#toxtricity).

---

### Seviper

**The reason this file exists.** A 120 HP Basic common with the only Ability in Standard that looks for a Darkness Mega Evolution Pokémon ex. While the Mega is in play, _Excited Power_ adds 120, so _Pitch-Black Fangs_ does **240 before Weakness**. One Prize when it dies, zero when an ex kills it under Concealment.

240 one-shots every single-prize body in the format and every non-Mega ex up to Raging Bolt ex at 240, and Weakness turns it into 480 on Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex and Mega Gardevoir ex. With the Mega it two-turns everything printed: 230 plus 240 is 470.

The cost is the cost. Three Darkness on a 120 HP body, and each Surge that pays it leaves 20 damage behind: a snake fed three times stands on 60 HP. Do not pay it from hand in front. Surge feeds it on the Bench, Void Gale exports onto it, and it steps forward already loaded. Three copies, so a second snake charges behind the first while it fights, and Night Stretcher is not the only way to see one after the first trade.

The check reads "in play," not "on your Bench." The Mega can be fighting in front and the snake still hits 240 behind it. Nothing fetches Seviper except Toxel's _Call for Family_ and Dawn's Basic slot, which is the other reason there are three.

### Chi-Yu

**The one-Energy attacker, new to the list.** A 90 HP Basic, Darkness, Weak to Grass rather than Fighting, and it has no Ability, which is rarer than it sounds in this deck.

**_Whirling Envy_ is 20, or 110 once Chi-Yu has 2 or more damage counters on it, and the damage is not affected by Weakness.** The condition is a feature here: _Sinister Surge_ puts 2 counters on whatever it feeds, so one Surge onto a Benched Chi-Yu delivers the one Energy and the two counters in a single Ability use. Nothing else in Standard arms it that cleanly.

What it is for, in order of how often it comes up:

- **Finishing.** 110 after a 230 Void Gale is 340; after a 240 Fangs it is 350. That is Mega Excadrill ex, Mega Lucario ex, Cynthia's Garchomp ex, Dragapult ex, Marnie's Grimmsnarl ex, Blaziken ex, and Mega Greninja ex, each in two turns, with the Mega or the snake taking the first swing and a body that cost one Energy taking the second.
- **The rest turn.** The turn you play Wally's Compassion, the Mega heals on the Bench and something has to attack. Chi-Yu does, for one Energy that Surge already paid.
- **The single-prize rooms.** Froslass, Munkidori, Dusclops, Dipplin, and Seaking all fall to 110. Spending a three-Energy snake on a one-Prize 90 HP body is how those rooms beat you; spending one Energy is how you beat them.
- **Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon ex.** _Cornerstone Stance_ prevents all attack damage from Pokémon that have an Ability. Every other attacker here has one. Chi-Yu hits it for 110, twice is 220, and its HP is 210.

**Where it must not go.** Into anything Psychic while a Mega or a loaded Seviper is available; Weakness is the deck's biggest number and Chi-Yu turns it off. Into the Active Spot unarmed; Surge only reaches the Bench, so a Chi-Yu promoted with no counters swings for 20 until something damages it. Its Weakness is Grass, so Dipplin and Dhelmise hit it for double, though at 70 HP armed it dies to anything anyway.

**When it dies it costs you one Energy**, not the snake's three, and under the cage it costs zero Prizes. Night Stretcher brings it back for another Surge. Two copies, because a one-of tech is prized one game in ten and only Dawn and _Call for Family_ can find it. [Game plan 3](#3-how-to-play-chi-yu) is the turn-by-turn.

# Trainers — Supporters

### Dawn

The line assembler: a Basic, a Stage 1, and a Stage 2 in one card. In this deck the best Dawn is **Seviper, Toxtricity, and Mega Gengar ex**, three cards from three different engines, with the Candy already in hand. When the Candy is missing, the middle slot becomes Haunter. Four copies, because it is the best turn-one Supporter and the best turn-two Supporter, and 68% of games have it or Hilda in hand by turn two.

Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#dawn).

### Hilda

Search your deck for an Evolution Pokémon and an Energy card, both to hand. That is "find the ghost and the turn's attachment" in one Supporter, or "find the Toxtricity and the Energy it will Surge for." Two copies beside four Dawn. Mind the word _Evolution_: she fetches any Haunter, either ghost, or a Toxtricity, never a Basic. Seviper and Chi-Yu are Toxel's and Dawn's problem.

The reset button. Shuffle your hand into your deck, draw 6, or draw 8 while all six of your Prizes are still up. Two copies. The hand this deck clogs with is ghosts, and Lillie's puts them back where Dawn and Hilda can find them again. Play it before the Items only when the Items are dead too.

### Wally's Compassion

**The reason the wall holds.** Heal all damage from one of your Mega Evolution Pokémon ex; if you healed anything, all Energy attached to it returns to your hand.

The numbers: Mega Excadrill ex hits 330 into 350, Dragapult ex hits 200, Grimmsnarl ex hits 180. Every one of them needs two turns to kill the Mega, and Wally's between the two hits resets the count to zero. A 350 HP Pokémon that heals to full is a four-shot, and nobody has four turns to spend on a wall that pays two Prizes. The Energy comes back to hand and goes straight back out as the turn's attachment.

Two copies. The turn you play one, Chi-Yu or Seviper fights in front while the Mega recovers behind. It does nothing for Chi-Yu or Seviper; they are not Megas.

# Game Plans

> Six plans. The first two are the deck, the third is new and is the one to read twice, and the fourth is how you play a room you have never seen.

---

## 1. The Cage

**The Prize math, done in both directions before every trade.**

Their side, against ex attackers. Concealment turns every one-Prize body here into a zero-Prize body, discounts Gengar ex from two to one, and discounts the Mega from three to two. Their six requires Knocking Out **all three Megas**; two Megas plus the Gengar ex stops at five. Night Stretcher rebuilds a fallen Mega four times over, and Wally's Compassion means even the first Knock Out is behind schedule.

Your side. Their ex Pokémon pay two or three apiece, so your six is two or three kills. Void Gale plus Chi-Yu is 340 across two turns; Void Gale plus Fangs is 470; Boss's Orders and Prime Catcher pick which body does the paying.

**The discipline.** The cage only holds while a Mega is in play. Keep a Gastly benched as the next Mega's body, and never send the Mega forward when 230 is not lethal and the Bench cannot rebuild it. A Mega that attacks into nothing volunteered a third of their game.

**The three escape hatches, and who uses them.**

- **Non-ex attackers.** Toucannon, Hariyama, Alakazam, Slowking, Dipplin, Dhelmise. Full Prizes off everything, and the Mega gifts three. [Game plan 4](#4-reading-the-room) is how you spot them early.
- **Damage counters.** Dusknoir's _Cursed Blast_ (13 counters), Alakazam's _Powerful Hand_, Munkidori's _Adrena-Brain_, Mega Greninja's _Mortal Shuriken_, Froslass's _Freezing Shroud_, and Phantom Dive's six Bench counters. A Seviper Knocked Out by counters pays a full Prize even with the Mega standing.
- **Instant Knock Outs and Special Conditions.** Hydrapple's _Hydra Breath_ and Poison. Rare, but a Mega Knocked Out that way pays all three.

---

## 2. The Charmer's Loop

**How three Energy costs get paid without three turns of attachments.**

```
hand attachment      --> the front, whoever is fighting
Sinister Surge (x2)  --> the Benched Seviper or Chi-Yu, from the deck, plus 2 rent each
Void Gale            --> 230, and the Mega's Energy lands on the snake
Air Balloon          --> the Mega steps back for free; the loaded attacker steps up
Energy Recycler      --> the discard goes back into the deck, where Surge looks
```

Two Surges plus the hand attachment is three Energy a turn onto the board, and Void Gale is a fourth that moves. The snake charges while the wall fights.

**The sequence the deck wants.** Turn one, Poffin for Gastly and Toxel, Energy on the Gastly. Turn two, Candy the Gastly into the Mega, Balloon on it, evolve Toxel to Toxtricity, Surge the Benched Seviper. Turn three, evolve the second Toxel, Surge twice onto Seviper, and it is standing on three Energy while the Mega hits for 230 in front and exports a fourth. Turn four, the Mega retreats free, the snake bites for 240, and the target has taken 470.

**Going second is faster.** Toxel in the Active Spot on turn one uses _Call for Family_ for Seviper and Chi-Yu, and the Bench is finished before the opponent has attacked.

**Where the rent goes.** Surge's two counters land on whoever got the Energy. On Seviper it is 20 off a body that pays zero into an ex anyway. On Chi-Yu it is the arming. On the Mega it is 20 off 350 that Wally's refunds in full. Never bank rent on Gengar ex; it is the one body that still pays a Prize under the cage.

---

## 3. How To Play Chi-Yu

**The turn-by-turn for a card you have never run.**

**Get it down.** Chi-Yu is 90 HP, so Poffin cannot fetch it. It reaches the Bench three ways: drawn and played, Toxel's _Call for Family_, or Dawn's Basic slot. It sits there unarmed and does nothing, and that is fine; an unarmed Chi-Yu on the Bench is not a liability, it is a one-Prize body that pays zero under the cage.

**Arm it on the Bench.** One _Sinister Surge_ aimed at Chi-Yu attaches one Energy and places 2 damage counters. That is the whole setup. It now has 70 HP left, one Energy, and a 110 attack. Do not Surge it again; the attack costs one, and a second Surge only takes it to 50 HP. If the opponent's Bench snipe puts counters on it first, it is armed for free.

**Walk it forward.** Four ways, cheapest first. When their attack Knocks Out your Active, promote Chi-Yu; it costs nothing. Retreat the Balloon Mega for free and promote it. Prime Catcher, which also drags up the target. Switch. Never promote it unarmed unless you want a body to absorb a hit while the Bench works.

**Attack.** _Whirling Envy_ for 110. Check the target's HP first, and the two-hit table: anything at or under 110 dies now, anything at or under 340 dies to Void Gale next turn, anything at or under 350 dies to Fangs next turn.

**Let it die.** Under the cage it pays zero Prizes and one Energy. Against a single-prize attacker it pays one Prize, same as Seviper would have, for a third of the Energy. Night Stretcher brings it back for another Surge, and the second copy is already in the deck.

| Turn shape | Chi-Yu or the snake? |
| :--- | :--- |
| Target has 110 or less HP | **Chi-Yu.** Froslass, Munkidori, Dusclops, Dipplin, Seaking, Drilbur, any Basic under a ghost line. |
| Target is Psychic | **Snake or Mega.** Weakness doubles them to 480 and 460; Chi-Yu's 110 is flat. |
| Target took 230 or 240 last turn | **Chi-Yu.** 110 finishes anything up to 350, and a 90 HP body absorbs the return hit. |
| Mega is healing this turn (Wally's) | **Chi-Yu.** The rest-turn attacker. |
| Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon ex is Active | **Chi-Yu, only.** Everything else here has an Ability and does zero to it. 110 twice is 220 into 210. |
| A 2- or 3-Prize ex at 240 or less | **Snake.** The Prize is worth the three Energy. |
| Seviper is one Energy short and Surge can finish it | **Snake next turn, Chi-Yu now**, if a Chi-Yu is armed. Otherwise Surge the snake and let the Mega hit. |

**Two habits.** Say the target's HP out loud before you choose the attacker. And count Surges before you use them: two Toxtricity is two Surges, and the second one is usually Chi-Yu's.

**Mega Excadrill is not the Fighting nightmare the earlier pages called it.** Mega Excadrill ex is a **Metal** type, Weak to Fire, and its attacks are Metal; nothing on your side takes double from it. _Maximum Drilling_ with five Energy is 330, which Knocks Out Gengar ex and every attacker but leaves the Mega standing on 20, and Wally's Compassion heals it to 350. It is a three-Prize Mega, so the cage is fully on: Seviper and Chi-Yu pay it nothing. Two-shot it with Chi-Yu and Void Gale, 340 into 340, and make it kill three Megas to win. This is a fair fight that the earlier pages wrote off, and it is the most common deck in the data.

**Dragapult is the good matchup with a Bench problem.** Every attacker is an ex, so the cage is on, and 320 HP falls to Fangs then Chi-Yu. The problem is _Phantom Dive_'s six damage counters on your Bench every turn: a benched Gastly is on 10 after one Dive and dies to the second, for a full Prize, because counters are not attack damage. Candy the Gastly the turn you legally can, keep the Bench to what you need, and in the Dusknoir build gust the Duskull with Boss's Orders before it becomes 13 counters.

**The single-prize rooms are a quarter of the field, and the second build had nothing cheap for them.** Now it does. Chi-Yu kills Dipplin, Seaking, Froslass, and Munkidori for one Energy; Tricky Steps kills Slowking, Alakazam, Dudunsparce, Toucannon, and Hariyama; the Mega stays in the deck. Your Prizes are honest here, one per body, so the game is a race of one-Energy attackers against theirs, and yours hit harder. Do not Candy the Mega into these rooms unless the third Gastly is the only ghost left.

**The Dark mirrors are decided by the ghost line.** N's Zoroark ex and Marnie's Grimmsnarl ex are both ex attackers at 280 and 320, so the cage is on and their bodies pay full. Zoroark's _Night Joker_ copies N's Darmanitan's _Back Draft_, 30 per Basic Energy in your discard, which is the one matchup where Energy Recycler is a defensive play. Grimmsnarl's Froslass puts a counter on every Pokémon with an Ability at every Checkup; Chi-Yu, Gastly, Haunter, and Toxel do not have one, and Chi-Yu kills Froslass outright.

**The Fighting rooms are the bad 5%.** Mega Lucario's _Mega Brave_ is 540 into anything you own, Hariyama's _Wild Press_ is 420 from a one-Prize body, and Garchomp's _Draconic Buster_ is 520. Nothing walls. Trade Chi-Yu and Seviper for their attackers, keep the Mega on the Bench for the cage and the snake's 120, and take the Prizes you can. Two-shot Mega Lucario with Chi-Yu and Void Gale, 340 into 340, on the turn after _Mega Brave_, because it cannot use that attack twice in a row.

## Versus the Kitchen Table

**Fox's Hostile Takeover is the matchup this mode was built for.** Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex is Psychic, 280 HP, Weak to Darkness: _Void Gale_ lands 460 and _Pitch-Black Fangs_ lands 480, so either ghost or snake one-shots his best card while Concealment zeroes almost everything he can kill in return. _Erasure Ball_ tops out at 280, which kills any attacker and leaves the Mega on 70. Team Rocket's Crobat ex is 310 Darkness, no Weakness to you, and its _Biting Spree_ counters ignore the cage; Fangs then Chi-Yu is 350. Chi-Yu is the wrong attacker into Mewtwo, because Weakness is doing the work there, and the right one into his one-Prize support pieces.

**Fox's Metal deck plays the cage straight, and the type is Metal.** Mega Excadrill ex hits 330 and the Mega survives it at 350; Wally's Compassion then undoes the whole hit, which breaks his two-shot math completely. Nothing he attacks with is Fighting, so nothing of yours takes double. His Fezandipiti and Munkidori pay him nothing back when they die to your ex, and your Seviper pays him nothing either. It becomes a pure wall race, and only one side heals.

**Fox's Flareon engine is the trap matchup.** Plain Flareon does 180 against ex Pokémon and is not an ex, so it dodges Concealment entirely and two-shots the Mega for one Prize of risk. Candy the Gengar ex, kill Flareon with Tricky Steps, and keep the Mega home. Seviper still one-shots every Flareon print he owns except Flareon ex at 270, which one Ruins counter or one Haunt puts in range.

## Upgrades
