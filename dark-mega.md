# Xero's Snake Charmer

### Gengar Gang's ex mode, second build. Eighteen cards swap, three ghosts share one line, and the snake still hits for 240.

> [!NOTE]
> **What this file is for.**
>
> This is not a fourth deck. It is [Gengar Gang](./dark.md) re-tuned for the rooms where the opponent attacks with Pokémon ex, the way [the lantern deck](./psychic-lanterns.md) becomes the Night Parade at home and [the Eevee box](./eevee-standard.md) swaps ten cards between Sun and Moon. Forty-two cards never leave their sleeves. Eighteen swap. Every card in both modes is a legal English print that is owned or in the 2026-08-20 mail, so either 60 can walk into a tournament the week the envelope lands.
>
> **This is the second build**, and it was tested before it was written: the list grew out of the "new gengar gang" brew that ran on the TCG Live app ahead of the tournament. Three things changed. The ghost line became a toolbox, with plain Gengar and Gengar ex joining the Mega on one Gastly-Haunter line, so the deck picks its Stage 2 per matchup instead of per sleeve-up. Hilda arrived in the mail and replaced the Poke Pad and Mega Signal search suite, because one Supporter that fetches the ghost and its Energy beats three Items that fetch one or the other. And Fezandipiti ex took over the draw seam plus a new job the app testing found for it: the 210 HP body that banks *Sinister Surge* Energy and shrugs off the rent, with Team Rocket's Petrel riding along as the one-card Trainer tutor. The costs were real too. Eternatus left and took the deck's only Stadium removal with it, Punk Helmet left and took the free chip damage, and Sableye left outright.
>
> Game words live in [the table rules](./rules.md); the ex-format habits live in [How To Play ex Style](./rules.md#how-to-play-ex-style). The full-detail entries for the cards shared with Gengar Gang live in [that file](./dark.md) and are not repeated here; each entry below says what the card does *in this mode*.
>
> [The Curse Toll](./dark-curse.md) is the bigger two-ghost plan. Its Gengar ex pair was ordered 2026-08-20 and is in the mail, and this build already puts one of the two to work. Curse Toll comes off hold when the envelope lands and the sleeves get sorted.

## The Thesis

**Seviper is the only card in Standard whose text names a Darkness Mega Evolution Pokémon ex, and we own the Mega it names.** That finding from the first build stands. *Excited Power* adds 120 to Seviper's attacks while Mega Gengar ex is anywhere in play, so *Pitch-Black Fangs* swings for **240 from a body worth one Prize**. The Mega walls at 350, *Shadowy Concealment* shrinks every Prize the opponent tries to take, and the snake does the killing they cannot afford to punish.

The second build adds a third leg. **One Gastly line now carries three different ghosts**, because Gengar, Gengar ex, and Mega Gengar ex are three separate card names that all evolve from the same Haunter, each with its own 4-copy limit. The deck no longer commits to one answer at sleeve-up; it looks at the board and Rare Candies the ghost that beats it. The Mega cages ex rooms. Plain Gengar farms wide single-prize benches with *Mind Jack*. Gengar ex taxes every Energy the opponent attaches from hand, all game, from your Bench. Choosing between them is [game plan 3](#3-three-ghosts-one-line).

*Shadowy Concealment* is still the spine. When a Darkness Pokémon of yours is Knocked Out by damage from an opposing Pokémon ex's attack, they take one fewer Prize card, and **every Pokémon in this 60 is Darkness**. Against a deck that attacks with ex Pokémon, the math collapses:

| Your Pokémon | Prizes normally | KO'd by their ex |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Gastly, Haunter, Gengar, Seviper, Toxel, Toxtricity | 1 | **0** |
| Gengar ex, Fezandipiti ex | 2 | **1** |
| Mega Gengar ex | 3 | **2** |

Their path to six Prizes runs through the ghosts themselves: three Mega kills exactly, or two Megas plus both of the two-Prize bodies. Night Stretcher rebuilds a fallen Mega, [Wally's Compassion](#wallys-compassion) heals a standing one to full, and every ex they play is worth two or three Prizes to a snake that costs them nothing to lose.

Against a deck that attacks with single-prize Pokémon, Concealment reads as a blank card. The first build handed those rooms to [Gengar Gang](./dark.md) entirely; this build can also pivot mid-game, because the same Gastly that would have become the Mega becomes a *Mind Jack* Gengar instead. Reading which room you are in is [game plan 4](#4-reading-the-room).

## The Mode Swap

Both modes live in one sleeved deck. Converting takes a couple of minutes at the table.

| Out (Gengar Gang) | In (Snake Charmer) |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 Gengar (Perfect Order 050) | 3 Mega Gengar ex (Phantasmal Flames 056) |
| 3 Koffing (Journey Together 091) | 1 Gengar ex (Temporal Forces 104) |
| 3 Weezing (Journey Together 092) | 2 Seviper (Phantasmal Flames 062) |
| 2 Sableye (Phantasmal Flames 059) | 1 Toxel (Phantasmal Flames 067) |
| 2 Gwynn (Pitch Black 078) | 1 Fezandipiti ex (Shrouded Fable 038) |
| 1 Boss's Orders (Mega Evolution 114) | 3 Hilda (White Flare 084) |
| 1 Buddy-Buddy Poffin (Temporal Forces 144) | 1 Lillie's Determination (Mega Evolution 119) |
| 2 Dark Bell (Pitch Black 075) | 2 Wally's Compassion (Mega Evolution 132) |
| 2 Punk Helmet (Phantasmal Flames 092) | 1 Team Rocket's Petrel (Destined Rivals 176) |
| 1 Risky Ruins (Mega Evolution 127) | 1 Rare Candy (Mega Evolution 125) |
| | 1 Energy Switch (Mega Evolution 115) |
| | 1 Basic Darkness Energy |

Eighteen cards out, eighteen in, and the other forty-two never move. Gang keeps three Gengar and this mode keeps two of the same copies, so only one physically leaves the sleeves; the same one-out arithmetic covers Boss's Orders, Buddy-Buddy Poffin, and Risky Ruins. The ACE SPEC stays Scramble Switch in both modes, so the one-per-deck rule holds without thinking about it. The English Prime Catcher stays with [the lantern deck](./psychic-lanterns.md), which already claims it.

Three things fall out of the swap that are worth saying out loud.

**Infinite Shadow no longer leaves with the Gengars.** Two Perfect Order Gengar stay in this mode, so the self-recycling ghost is available when the room calls for it. The Mega is still a three-Prize investment and plays like one; the plain Gengar is the one you spend freely.

**Dark Bell leaves because the fuse left.** The Confusion engine served Weezing's two-turn combo, and Weezing is a Gang card. The slots became the toolbox and the Supporters that find it.

**The Stadium answer left with Eternatus.** This mode's only response to an opposing Stadium is overwriting it with the single Risky Ruins, so hold that card until it hurts them to lose theirs.

> [!TIP]
> **Optional two-card sub.** The binder holds a spare Perfect Order Haunter (*Haunt*: place 3 damage counters) and a promo Haunter (*Spooky Shot* 40). Swapping them in for the two Phantasmal Flames copies turns the middle stage into a counter-placer, and placed counters ignore Weakness and Resistance. Haunt is exactly the 30 that turns Seviper's 240 into a 270 kill. Skip the sub when teaching; take it when playing to win.

### Gastly

The same four Perfect Order copies, doing the same job: sit on the Bench and become a ghost. What changed is the menu. One Gastly can now grow into a 130 HP recycler, a 310 HP Energy tax, or a 350 HP wall, and the choice waits until the Rare Candy is played. Four copies feed at most four ghosts a game, so every Candy is a real decision. The [Shadowy Darkness Energy](#shadowy-darkness-energy) still goes on the Benched Gastly first; whichever ghost it becomes, it must arrive.

Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#gastly).

### Haunter

The backup route when Rare Candy is late, same as ever. Two copies, Phantasmal Flames, flat 40, and now the middle stage under three different tops. See the note in [The Mode Swap](#the-mode-swap) for the *Haunt* substitution, which is stronger in this mode than in Gengar Gang because this deck does its killing with big single hits that keep landing 10 or 30 short of a number.

Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#haunter).

### Gengar

**The single-prize ghost, new to this mode.** *Mind Jack* costs one Darkness and scales with the opponent's Bench: 130 at four Benched Pokémon, 160 at five. *Infinite Shadow* returns the whole Knocked Out stack to your hand, minus the attached cards.

In an ex room this is the wrong Candy target, because 160 threatens nothing they play and the Mega does the same job with 350 HP under it. Its room is the wide single-prize board: the Festival Lead and Slowking style decks that blank *Shadowy Concealment* on purpose. Against those, a one-Energy attack that clears their whole working tier, from a body that rebuilds itself when it dies, is the game plan the Mega cannot be. Two copies, because Infinite Shadow makes two play like more.

One quiet bonus: under the cage, a plain Gengar that dies to an opposing ex pays **zero** Prizes, so even in the Mega matchups a spare Gengar is a free wall-mate. Count the Bench *after* a Boss's Orders, not before; dragging a target off their Bench costs Mind Jack 30.

Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#gengar).

---

### Gengar ex

**The tax ghost, borrowed from [Curse Toll](./dark-curse.md).** Stage 2 from the same Haunter, 310 HP, worth two Prizes normally and one under the cage.

***Gnawing Curse* punishes the fundamental action of the game.** Whenever your opponent attaches an Energy card from their hand to one of their Pokémon, 2 damage counters go on that Pokémon. There is no Active Spot requirement, so the Curse runs from your Bench all game, and the counters land on their attackers before the fight starts. Against decks that accelerate from hand every turn, this single card is a running head start on every kill the snake lines up.

***Tricky Steps* is disruption wearing a damage number.** 160, and you may move an Energy from their Active to a Benched Pokémon of their choice becomes *your* choice; send it to a support piece and the Energy is functionally discarded. They re-attach from hand, and the Curse bites again.

One copy, because it is the third ghost, not the first. Candy it against single-prize rooms and attachment-heavy engines; leave it in the deck against decks that accelerate from the discard pile, which never trigger it. [Curse Toll](./dark-curse.md) runs the pair and stacks the toll; this deck borrows one for the toolbox.

---

### Mega Gengar ex

**The wall the cage leans on.** 350 HP, Stage 2 from Haunter, worth three Prizes when it falls, two under its own ability. It evolves like any other Stage 2; modern Mega cards do not end your turn.

***Shadowy Concealment* is the deck.** One fewer Prize whenever your Darkness Pokémon dies to an opposing ex's attack, and every Pokémon here is Darkness. The ability works from the Bench and the Active Spot alike, and it does not stack.

***Void Gale* is 230, then it moves an Energy from this Pokémon to one of your Benched Pokémon.** The move is not optional, and this deck turns the tax into the engine: the Energy lands on the Benched Seviper, which needs three. Attack with the Mega and you are simultaneously loading the snake. That loop is [game plan 2](#2-the-charmers-loop).

**Three copies now, up from two.** The third arrived in the 2026-08-20 mail, and it fixes the first build's quiet loss condition: a two-copy core starts with one copy prized about one game in five, and Concealment cannot fire from the Prize cards. The honest numbers have not changed. 230 one-shots nothing in the current ex tier except what Weakness doubles it into, so play the Mega as a wall that counterattacks, not a sweeper. Wally's undoes the hits it takes, and everything it fails to kill is 230 closer to Seviper's range.

---

### Seviper

**The reason this file exists.** A 120 HP Basic common with the only ability in Standard that looks for a Darkness Mega Evolution Pokémon ex. While the Mega is in play, *Excited Power* adds 120, so *Pitch-Black Fangs* does **240 before Weakness**. One Prize when it dies, zero when an ex kills it under Concealment.

240 one-shots every single-prize body in the format and most non-Mega ex up to Team Rocket's Mewtwo, which its 480 after Weakness obliterates. Together with the Mega it two-turns everything printed: 230 plus 240 is 470, and no card has 470 HP.

The cost is the cost. Three Darkness is a lot of Energy for a 120 HP body, and paying it from hand takes three turns. Do not pay it from hand. *Sinister Surge* feeds the Benched Seviper from the deck, *Void Gale* banks the Mega's Energy onto it, and Scramble Switch carries a dying attacker's Energy forward. A Seviper that steps into the Active Spot with fangs already paid is the deck working; a Seviper you are charging in front is the deck stalling.

The check reads "in play," not "on your Bench." The Mega can be fighting in front and the snake still hits 240 behind it. Nothing fetches Seviper except Toxel's *Call for Family*, so going second that attack usually names a snake.

### Toxel

Unchanged, and still the deck's best going-second play. *Call for Family* benches any two Basics with no HP cap, which here means the Seviper nothing else can fetch or the Fezandipiti that anchors the draw. Three copies now, because the app testing kept wanting the turn-one attack more often than two delivered it. The binder holds two, so the third is on [the buy list](#what-to-buy).

Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#toxel).

---

### Toxtricity

Unchanged in text, promoted in importance. *Sinister Surge* is the engine that pays Seviper's three-Energy cost on schedule: one Basic Darkness from the deck to a Benched Darkness Pokémon every turn, free, plus 2 damage counters of rent on whatever it feeds.

Surge feeds **Benched Darkness Pokémon only**, and everything here qualifies. The default target is the charging Seviper. The second-best target is the Benched Mega, so *Void Gale* has an Energy to export without spending your hand attachment. The third is the bank: park the Energy on [Fezandipiti ex](#fezandipiti-ex), whose 210 HP barely notices the rent, and withdraw it later with [Energy Switch](#energy-switch).

Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#toxtricity).

---

### Fezandipiti ex

**The draw seam and the Energy bank, straight from the app testing.** 210 HP Basic, Darkness, worth two Prizes normally and one under the cage.

***Flip the Script* reads narrower than it plays, so read it exactly.** Once during your turn, *if any of your Pokémon were Knocked Out during your opponent's last turn*, draw 3 cards. It is not draw-three-every-turn; it fires only on the turns after you lost something. In this deck that is most of the mid and late game, because the cage invites knockouts that pay nothing, but it is blank on turns one and two and blank after any turn they whiff. Plan the draw around the trades, not instead of them.

**The bank job.** *Sinister Surge* must feed a Benched Darkness Pokémon, and sometimes the right recipient is not down yet or is standing in the Active Spot. Fezandipiti holds the deposit: 210 HP absorbs the 2-counter rent all game, and [Energy Switch](#energy-switch) moves the Basic Energy wherever it is needed, including to the Active. Two cautions. Against single-prize rooms the cage is off, so a rent-loaded Fezandipiti is a two-Prize Boss's Orders target; bank on the ghosts instead. And *Cruel Arrow* is a real exit for the savings: [C][C][C], 100 damage to any of their Pokémon, Weakness and Resistance skipped on the Bench. Three banked Energy plus a wounded target behind their wall is a Prize.

# Trainers — Supporters

> The one-Supporter rule does not change between modes. Dawn when the board is missing line pieces, Hilda when the ghost and its Energy are missing, Lillie's when the hand is bad, Boss's Orders when a Prize is on the table, Wally's Compassion when the wall is wounded, and Petrel when the card you are missing is a Trainer.

### Dawn

Still the line assembler: a Basic, a Stage 1, and a Stage 2 in one card, which in this deck spells Gastly, Haunter, and the ghost of your choice. Three copies, and it is the best turn-one Supporter exactly as it is in Gengar Gang. The Stage 2 slot is where [game plan 3](#3-three-ghosts-one-line) happens standing up; name the ghost that beats the board you are looking at.

Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#dawn).

### Hilda

**The standing hunt, landed.** Search your deck for an Evolution Pokémon and an Energy card, both to hand. That is "find the ghost and the turn's attachment" in one Supporter, and the real Mega Gengar lists on Limitless run her for exactly this reason. The first build wanted her and could not have her; the 2026-08-20 order fixed that with copies to spare.

Three copies replace the whole Poke Pad and Mega Signal suite from the first build. The trade is deliberate: Items search without spending the Supporter, but each found one card and none found Energy. Hilda finds the two cards a ghost deck is actually missing. Mind the word *Evolution*: she fetches any Haunter, any of the three ghosts, or a Toxtricity, but never a Basic. Seviper and Fezandipiti are Toxel's and Poffin's problem.

### Lillie's Determination

The reset button. Shuffle your hand into your deck, draw 6, or draw 8 while all six of your Prizes are still up. Three copies now, up from two, because this build's draw seam is otherwise Fezandipiti's conditional 3; when the toolbox hand goes wrong, this is what fixes it.

Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#lillies-determination).

### Boss's Orders [Ghetsis]

The gust, at two copies in this mode, and it aims at Energy as often as at HP. Dragging up the support piece their acceleration loaded, or the half-charged attacker hiding from Seviper, forces a rebuild while the cage taxes everything they kill in return. If you are counting on a *Mind Jack* number the same turn, do the math after the gust; the target you pull up stops being Benched.

Two rather than Gang's three paid for the toolbox. Spend them on Prizes, never on tempo.

Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#bosss-orders-ghetsis).

---

### Wally's Compassion

**The reason the wall holds.** Heal all damage from one of your Mega Evolution Pokémon ex; if you healed anything, all Energy attached to it returns to your hand.

Read the Energy clause as a small tax. Void Gale already exports the Mega's Energy every attack, so a healing Mega is usually holding one or two, and they come back to hand where the next attachment wants them anyway. The play pattern: the opponent two-shots at 180 or 230 a turn, you heal on the rest turn, and their math never completes. A 350 HP Pokémon that heals to full is not a two-shot, it is a four-shot, and almost nobody has four turns to spend on a wall that pays two Prizes.

Two copies. The turn you play one, Seviper fights in front while the Mega recovers behind, which is exactly the rhythm the attackers were chosen for.

---

### Team Rocket's Petrel

**A universal tutor wearing a villain costume.** Search your deck for a Trainer card, any Trainer card, and put it into your hand. The Team Rocket branding restricts nothing; the card works in any deck, which is exactly why it is here.

One copy, because it is the fifth copy of everything. This deck runs three one-of Items that games swing on, and Petrel is the extra out to each: the Scramble Switch for the loop's big turn, the Energy Switch when the bank needs to pay out, the lone Risky Ruins when their Stadium is winning, or the Rare Candy that finishes a ghost. Spending the turn's Supporter on a search is a real cost, so reach for it when the found card wins more than a Lillie's draw would.

The owned copy floats in the box: Fox's Hostile Takeover rebuild cut it from his 60, and only game-night [Smog Signals](./dark-smog.md) borrows it. The [buy list](#what-to-buy) carries this deck's claim, so if that ever changes, the wishlist will say so.

### Energy Switch

**New in this build, and the bank's withdrawal slip.** Move a Basic Energy from one of your Pokémon to another of your Pokémon, no restrictions on which.

It exists to undo the one rule everything else obeys: *Sinister Surge* cannot feed the Active Spot, and *Void Gale* exports blind to the front line. Energy Switch takes a banked Energy off Fezandipiti, or a stranded export off the wrong body, and puts it on the attacker that needs it right now, Active included. The turn Seviper steps forward one Energy short, this is the card that closes the gap without waiting.

**Basic Energy only.** A Shadowy Darkness Energy does not move. One copy; the second English copy stays with [Curse Toll](./dark-curse.md)'s plan.

### Switch

One copy, one job: rescue whatever Boss's Orders dragged up. The Mega and Toxtricity both retreat for two, which is exactly the Energy this deck refuses to waste.

### Scramble Switch

The ACE SPEC in both modes, and better in this one. The dream play: a wounded Mega holding two Energy swaps out, both Energy land on the incoming Seviper, and the snake attacks for 240 the same turn. You repositioned, rescued the Energy, and paid the fangs in one Item.

Spend it on the Energy transfer, never as a second Switch. Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#scramble-switch).

# Game Plans

> Six plans. The first two are the deck, the third is what the second build added, and the fourth is the reason the mode swap exists.

---

## 1. The Cage

**The Prize math, done in both directions before every trade.**

Their side, against ex attackers. Concealment turns every one-Prize body here into a zero-Prize body, discounts Gengar ex and Fezandipiti from two to one, and discounts the Mega from three to two. Add it up: their six requires Knocking Out three separate Megas, or two Megas plus both discounted ex. Night Stretcher rebuilds a fallen Mega four times over; Wally's Compassion means even the second Knock Out is behind schedule.

Your side. Their ex Pokémon pay two or three apiece, so your six is two or three kills. Void Gale plus one Surge counter-drop kills 250; Void Gale plus Pitch-Black Fangs kills 470 across two turns; Boss's Orders picks which body does the paying.

**The discipline.** The cage only holds while a Mega is in play, and the second build added two more bodies that pay Prizes even under it. Keep Gengar ex and Fezandipiti out of the crossfire when the wall can take the hit instead, and never send the Mega forward when 230 is not lethal and the Bench cannot rebuild it. A Mega that attacks into nothing volunteered a third of their game.

**The escape hatch to respect.** Concealment triggers on Knock Outs *by damage from an attack from a Pokémon ex*. A non-ex attacker on their side collects full Prizes off your board. One splashed single-prize attacker does not break the cage; a deck built on them does, and that deck is [game plan 3](#3-three-ghosts-one-line)'s problem now.

## 2. The Charmer's Loop

**How three Energy costs get paid without three turns of attachments.**

```
hand attachment      --> the front, whoever is fighting
Sinister Surge       --> the Benched Seviper or the bank, from the deck, plus 2 rent
Void Gale            --> 230, and the Mega's Energy lands on the snake
Energy Switch        --> a banked Energy moves to whoever needs it, even the Active
Scramble Switch      --> a dying attacker's Energy carries to the next one
```

Two attachments a turn is the baseline, and the Mega's attack is secretly a third: every Void Gale exports one Energy to the Bench, and the export is aimed. The snake charges while the wall fights.

**The sequence the deck wants.** Turn one, Poffin for Gastly and Toxel, Shadowy on the Gastly. Turn two, Candy the Gastly into the Mega, evolve Toxtricity, Surge the Benched Seviper. Turn three, the Mega attacks and banks an Energy onto Seviper; Surge pays the rest. By the time the wall has taken two hits, the snake is loaded and the opponent has to choose which threat to feed Prizes to.

**Where the rent goes.** Surge's two counters land on whoever got the Energy. On Seviper that is nothing; it pays zero into an ex anyway. On the Mega it is 20 off a 350 HP pool that Wally's refunds in full. On Fezandipiti it is 20 off 210 in the matchups where Fezandipiti pays one, and a slowly ripening Boss's Orders gift in the matchups where it pays two. Bank accordingly.

## 3. Three Ghosts, One Line

**The second build's addition: one Gastly, three possible futures.**

Gengar, Gengar ex, and Mega Gengar ex are three different card names on one evolution line. They share the four Gastly, the two Haunter, and the four Rare Candy, and each carries its own 4-copy limit. Nothing about the line commits you before the Candy is played.

| The room | The ghost | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ex attackers | **Mega Gengar ex** | The cage turns on and their Prizes shrink. The wall takes the hits the snake cannot. |
| Wide single-prize benches | **Gengar** | Mind Jack clears their working tier for one Energy, and Infinite Shadow means they have to kill it three times. |
| Hand-attachment engines, stall | **Gengar ex** | Gnawing Curse taxes every attachment from your Bench, and Tricky Steps strands the Energy they already paid. |

**The budget is four Gastly.** Every Candy spends a quarter of the line, so the first ghost is usually easy (the Mega against an ex room, Gengar against a cheap one) and the second is the real decision. A wrong first pick is not fatal; the Bench usually holds another Gastly with another future.

**The ghosts stack sideways.** A Benched Mega keeps the cage up while a plain Gengar farms in front, and a Benched Gengar ex taxes their recovery while the Mega walls.

**One warning.** Gnawing Curse never fires against decks that accelerate from the discard pile or the deck, and Mind Jack never threatens a narrow board. The toolbox only pays if you read the room before spending the Candy; when in doubt, the Mega is the default and the cage is the plan.

## 4. Reading the Room

**One sleeved deck, two modes. Choose before the game, not during.**

| They attack with | Sleeve | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Pokémon ex and Mega ex | **Snake Charmer** | The cage is on. Their Prizes shrink; yours double. |
| Single-prize Pokémon | **[Gengar Gang](./dark.md)** | Concealment is blank and the Mega gifts three Prizes. Weezing's 170 clears their whole tier instead. |
| A mix | Count their attackers | The cage pays off when most of their damage comes from ex. One Flareon does not break it; a deck of them does. |

The tell is their attacker lineup, not their deck's best card. A deck that runs one big ex but attacks with cheap bodies is a single-prize room wearing a costume.

The second build softened the penalty for guessing wrong. Caught in this mode against a single-prize room, skip the Mega entirely: Candy the plain Gengar, run Mind Jack and the Curse toll, and keep every body you field at one Prize or two. It is a worse Gengar Gang, but it is not a forfeit.

**Swap discipline.** The sixteen-card swap in [The Mode Swap](#the-mode-swap) is the whole conversion. Keep both lists printed with the box, count 60 after every swap, and never sleeve half a mode; a deck with one Weezing and one Mega does neither job.

## 6. Things That Will Cost You a Game

| Mistake | What actually happens |
| :--- | :--- |
| Sleeving this mode into a single-prize room | Concealment does nothing and the Mega gifts three Prizes. Gang is the right 60; the plain Gengar is the salvage plan. |
| Candying the Mega against a single-prize deck | Same mistake after the game started. Read the room, then spend the Candy. |
| Attacking with the Mega when 230 is not lethal and no rebuild is ready | You traded a third of their Prize requirement for chip damage. Wall first, sweep second. |
| Forgetting Void Gale's move is mandatory | The Mega attacks itself out of Energy. Aim the export at Seviper and it is a feature; forget to aim and the Energy lands somewhere useless. |
| Counting Flip the Script as draw-three-every-turn | It fires only if you lost a Pokémon during their last turn. Turns one and two it is blank, and so is every turn after they whiff. |
| Banking Surge rent on Fezandipiti against a single-prize deck | The cage is off, so it is a 210 HP two-Prize body you are pre-softening for their Boss's Orders. Bank on the ghosts instead. |
| Charging Seviper from hand in the Active Spot | Three turns of attachments in front of an attacker. Surge, Void Gale, and Energy Switch charge it; Scramble Switch and Switch bring it forward loaded. |
| Energy Switch on a Shadowy Darkness Energy | Basic Energy only. The shield stays where you put it. |
| Wally's Compassion on a full-HP Mega to reset Energy | It heals nothing, so the Energy clause never fires. The card is a heal with a rider, not an Energy bounce. |
| Poffin targeting Seviper or Fezandipiti | Illegal; both are over 70 HP. Call for Family is the route. |
| Rare Candy on the Gastly Poffin just benched | Illegal until next turn. Bench Basics one turn earlier than you need them. |
| Trying to Night Stretcher a Shadowy Darkness Energy | Special Energy is gone forever. Both copies are precious. |
| Spending Scramble Switch as a plain Switch | It is the only Energy-rescue in the deck and there is no second copy. |
| Playing the only Risky Ruins into an empty turn-one board | There is no second copy in this mode and no other Stadium answer. Wait until it taxes something. |
| Expecting Concealment against a non-ex attacker | The trigger reads Pokémon ex. Plain Flareon, plain Weezing, and their kin collect full Prizes. |

## Versus the Kitchen Table

**Fox's Hostile Takeover is the matchup this mode was built for.** Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex is Psychic, 280 HP, and Weak to Darkness: *Void Gale* lands 460 and *Pitch-Black Fangs* lands 480, so either ghost or snake one-shots his best card while Concealment zeroes almost everything he can kill in return. Crobat ex at 310 Darkness takes no Weakness and needs two hits; with Punk Helmet gone, the gap closers are Surge rent, Risky Ruins counters, and Gnawing Curse ticking as he powers it up. Remember half his board is Darkness, so Risky Ruins collects less than usual.

**Fox's Metal deck plays the cage straight.** Mega Excadrill ex hits 330, and the Mega survives it at 350; Wally's Compassion then undoes the whole hit, which breaks his two-shot math completely. His Fezandipiti and Munkidori pay him nothing back when they die to your ex, but your Seviper pays him nothing either, because his attacker is a Mega. It becomes a pure wall race, and only one side heals.

**Fox's Flareon engine is the trap matchup.** Plain Flareon does 180 against ex Pokémon and is not an ex, so it dodges Concealment entirely and two-shots the Mega for one Prize of risk. This is where the toolbox earns its slots: Candy the plain Gengar, farm his Bench with Mind Jack, and keep the Mega home so his 180 has no target worth three Prizes. Seviper still one-shots every Flareon print he owns except Flareon ex at 270, which one Ruins counter or one Haunt puts in range. Winnable now, but [reading the room](#4-reading-the-room) still says Gengar Gang plays it cleaner.
