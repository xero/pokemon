## The Thesis

**Seviper is the only card in Standard whose text names a Darkness Mega Evolution Pokémon ex, and we own the Mega it names.**

I searched the full legal pool, all 2,947 cards. One card keys off a Darkness Mega ex being in play, and it is a 120 HP common we already own two of. *Excited Power* adds 120 to Seviper's attacks while Mega Gengar ex is anywhere in play, so *Pitch-Black Fangs* swings for **240 from a body worth one Prize**. Nobody plays it online. Nobody plays standalone Mega Gengar online either; its recorded season is 3 wins and 17 losses, because everyone builds the Mega as an attacker and feeds three Prizes to whoever kills it. This deck builds it as a wall that happens to hit for 230, and hides the real damage behind a snake the opponent is embarrassed to attack.

The reason the opponent is embarrassed is *Shadowy Concealment*. When a Darkness Pokémon of yours is Knocked Out by damage from an opposing Pokémon ex's attack, they take one fewer Prize card. **Every Pokémon in this 60 is Darkness**, so against a deck that attacks with ex Pokémon, the math collapses:

| Your Pokémon | Prizes normally | KO'd by their ex |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Gastly, Haunter, Seviper, Eternatus, Sableye, Toxel, Toxtricity | 1 | **0** |
| Mega Gengar ex | 3 | **2** |

Their only path to six Prizes runs through the Mega itself, three separate times, against a 350 HP body that [Wally's Compassion](#wallys-compassion) heals to full and [Night Stretcher](#night-stretcher) rebuilds. Meanwhile every ex they play is worth two or three Prizes to a snake that hits 240 and costs them nothing to lose.

Against a deck that attacks with single-prize Pokémon, Concealment reads as a blank card and the Mega is a three-Prize gift. Those rooms belong to [Gengar Gang](./dark.md). Reading which room you are in is [game plan 3](#3-reading-the-room).

## The Mode Swap

Both modes live in one sleeved deck. Converting takes about a minute at the table.

| Out (Gengar Gang) | In (Snake Charmer) |
| :--- | :--- |
| 3 Gengar (Perfect Order 050) | 2 Mega Gengar ex (Phantasmal Flames 056) |
| 3 Koffing (Journey Together 091) | 2 Seviper (Phantasmal Flames 062) |
| 3 Weezing (Journey Together 092) | 1 Eternatus (Phantasmal Flames 069) |
| 2 Dark Bell (Pitch Black 075) | 2 Wally's Compassion (Mega Evolution 132) |
| | 3 Poke Pad (Perfect Order 081) |
| | 1 Mega Signal (Mega Evolution 121) |

Eleven cards out, eleven in, and the other forty-nine never move. The ACE SPEC stays Scramble Switch in both modes, so the one-per-deck rule holds without thinking about it. The English Prime Catcher stays with [the lantern deck](./psychic-lanterns.md), which already claims it.

Two things fall out of the swap that are worth saying out loud.

**Infinite Shadow leaves with the Gengars.** In this mode a Knocked Out ghost stays knocked out unless Night Stretcher fetches it back. Play the Mega like the three-Prize investment it is, not like the self-recycling 130 that used to live in that slot.

**Dark Bell leaves because the fuse left.** The Confusion engine existed to serve Weezing's two-turn combo. Without Weezing, the slots buy the search that a two-copy Stage 2 core cannot live without.

> [!TIP]
> **Optional two-card sub.** The binder holds a spare Perfect Order Haunter (*Haunt*: place 3 damage counters) and a promo Haunter (*Spooky Shot* 40). Swapping them in for the two Phantasmal Flames copies turns the middle stage into a counter-placer, and placed counters ignore Weakness and Resistance. Haunt is exactly the 30 that turns Seviper's 240 into a 270 kill. Skip the sub when teaching; take it when playing to win.

The [Shadowy Darkness Energy](#shadowy-darkness-energy) still goes on the Benched Gastly first; that Gastly is your wall, and the wall must arrive.

Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#gastly).

### Haunter

The backup route when Rare Candy is late, same as ever. Two copies, Phantasmal Flames, flat 40. See the note in [The Mode Swap](#the-mode-swap) for the *Haunt* substitution, which is stronger in this mode than in Gengar Gang because this deck does its killing with big single hits that keep landing 10 or 30 short of a number.

Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#haunter).

---

### Mega Gengar ex

**The wall the whole mode leans on.** 350 HP, Stage 2 from Haunter, worth three Prizes when it falls, two under its own ability. It evolves like any other Stage 2; modern Mega cards do not end your turn.

***Shadowy Concealment* is the deck.** One fewer Prize whenever your Darkness Pokémon dies to an opposing ex's attack, and every Pokémon here is Darkness. The ability works from the Bench and the Active Spot alike, and it does not stack, which is one reason two copies is the count rather than three. The other reason is that we own two.

***Void Gale* is 230, then it moves an Energy from this Pokémon to one of your Benched Pokémon.** The move is not optional, and this deck turns the tax into the engine: the Energy lands on the Benched Seviper, which needs three. Attack with the Mega and you are simultaneously loading the snake. That loop is [game plan 2](#2-the-charmers-loop).

The honest numbers. 230 one-shots nothing in the current ex tier except what Weakness doubles it into, and it needs a fresh Energy every turn to keep attacking. Play the Mega as a wall that counterattacks, not a sweeper. It survives every common hit below 350, Wally's undoes the ones it takes, and everything it fails to kill is 230 closer to Seviper's range.

---

### Seviper

**The reason this file exists.** A 120 HP Basic common with the only ability in Standard that looks for a Darkness Mega Evolution Pokémon ex. While the Mega is in play, *Excited Power* adds 120, so *Pitch-Black Fangs* does **240 before Weakness**. One Prize when it dies, zero when an ex kills it under Concealment.

240 one-shots every single-prize body in the format and most non-Mega ex up to Team Rocket's Mewtwo, which its 480 after Weakness obliterates. Together with the Mega it two-turns everything printed: 230 plus 240 is 470, and no card has 470 HP.

The cost is the cost. Three Darkness is a lot of Energy for a 120 HP body, and paying it from hand takes three turns. Do not pay it from hand. *Sinister Surge* feeds the Benched Seviper from the deck, *Void Gale* banks the Mega's Energy onto it, and Scramble Switch carries a dying attacker's Energy forward. A Seviper that steps into the Active Spot with fangs already paid is the deck working; a Seviper you are charging in front is the deck stalling.

The check reads "in play," not "on your Bench." The Mega can be fighting in front and the snake still hits 240 behind it. That is the difference between Excited Power and Sableye's *Cocky Claw*, and it is why Seviper is the primary attacker and Sableye is the spare.

---

### Eternatus

One copy, and it is here for one attack. *Shatter* does 50 and **discards a Stadium in play**, and this deck otherwise has no answer to an opposing Stadium beyond overwriting it with Risky Ruins. Two Darkness pays it, which the engine provides without strain.

The rest is furniture with 150 HP. *Power Rush* at 130 for three Energy with a coin-flip rider is legal but almost always worse than giving those three Energy to a Seviper. Bench Eternatus when the Bench has room and the opponent runs a Stadium worth killing; leave it in the deck otherwise, and let Gwynn eat it late.

---

### Sableye

Same two copies, same 90-for-one, and a better condition than it had in Gengar Gang: *Cocky Claw* wants a Stage 2 Darkness Pokémon **on your Bench**, and a benched Mega Gengar ex passes the check. While the Mega sits in the back healing or waiting, Sableye swings 90 for a single Energy from a body that pays zero Prizes into an ex.

The moment the Mega steps forward to fight, Cocky Claw drops to 20. The snake does not care where the Mega stands; the imp does. Send Sableye out on the wall's rest turns and Seviper out the rest of the time.

Grass Weakness still means Team Rocket's Spidops and Tarountula erase it. Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#sableye).

---

### Toxel

Unchanged, and still the deck's best going-second play. *Call for Family* benches any two Basics with no HP cap, which here means the Sableye that Poffin cannot lift, a Seviper, or the Eternatus. Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#toxel).

---

### Toxtricity

Unchanged in text, promoted in importance. *Sinister Surge* is one of only two ways this deck pays Seviper's three-Energy cost on schedule, and the two damage counters it charges in rent matter less than ever: they land on bodies that pay the opponent nothing when they die to an ex anyway.

Surge feeds **Benched Darkness Pokémon only**, and everything here qualifies. The default target is the charging Seviper. The second-best target is the Benched Mega, so *Void Gale* has an Energy to export without spending your hand attachment.

Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#toxtricity).

### Boss's Orders [Ghetsis]

The gust, three copies, and in this mode it aims at Energy as often as at HP. Dragging up the support Pokémon their acceleration loaded, or the half-charged attacker hiding from Seviper, forces them to rebuild while the cage costs them Prizes for everything they kill on your side.

The Mind Jack warning from Gengar Gang no longer applies; nothing here scales off their Bench count. Full entry in [Gengar Gang](./dark.md#bosss-orders-ghetsis).

---

### Wally's Compassion

**New in this mode, and the reason the wall holds.** Heal all damage from one of your Mega Evolution Pokémon ex; if you healed anything, all Energy attached to it returns to your hand.

Read the Energy clause as a small tax, not a cost. Void Gale already exports the Mega's Energy every attack, so a healing Mega is usually holding one or two, and they come back to hand where the next attachment wants them anyway. The play pattern: the opponent two-shots at 180 or 230 a turn, you heal on the rest turn, and their math never completes. A 350 HP Pokémon that heals to full is not a two-shot, it is a four-shot, and almost nobody has four turns to spend on a wall that pays two Prizes.

Two copies. The turn you play one, Sableye or Seviper fights in front while the Mega recovers behind, which is exactly the rhythm the attackers were chosen for.

# Game Plans

> Five plans. The first two are the deck; the third is the reason the mode swap exists.

---

## 1. The Cage

**The Prize math, done in both directions before every trade.**

Their side, against ex attackers. Concealment turns every one-Prize body here into a zero-Prize body and discounts the Mega from three to two. Add it up: the only Prizes on your board are two Megas at two apiece, which is four. **A pure ex deck cannot reach six Prizes without Knocking Out a rebuilt or healed Mega for the third time.** Night Stretcher rebuilds it four times over; Wally's Compassion means even the second Knock Out is behind schedule.

Your side. Their ex Pokémon pay two or three apiece, so your six is two or three kills. Void Gale plus one Surge counter-drop kills 250; Void Gale plus Pitch-Black Fangs kills 470 across two turns; Boss's Orders picks which body does the paying.

**The discipline.** The cage only holds while a Mega is in play, and the Mega's own death still pays two. Never send it forward when 230 is not lethal and the Bench cannot rebuild it. A Mega that attacks into nothing volunteered a third of their game.

**The escape hatch to respect.** Concealment triggers on Knock Outs *by damage from an attack from a Pokémon ex*. A non-ex attacker on their side collects full Prizes off your board. One splashed single-prize attacker does not break the cage; a deck built on them does, and that deck is [game plan 3](#3-reading-the-room)'s problem.

---

## 2. The Charmer's Loop

**How three Energy costs get paid without three turns of attachments.**

```
hand attachment      --> the front, whoever is fighting
Sinister Surge       --> the Benched Seviper, from the deck, plus 2 rent
Void Gale            --> 230, and the Mega's Energy lands on the snake
Scramble Switch      --> a dying attacker's Energy carries to the next one
```

Two attachments a turn is the baseline, and the Mega's attack is secretly a third: every Void Gale exports one Energy to the Bench, and the export is aimed. The snake charges while the wall fights.

**The sequence the deck wants.** Turn one, Poffin for Gastly and Toxel, Shadowy on the Gastly. Turn two, Candy the Gastly into the Mega, evolve Toxtricity, Surge the Benched Seviper. Turn three, the Mega attacks and banks an Energy onto Seviper; Surge pays the rest. By the time the wall has taken two hits, the snake is loaded and the opponent has to choose which threat to feed Prizes to.

**Where the rent goes.** Surge's two counters land on whoever got the Energy. On Seviper and Sableye that is nothing; they pay zero into an ex anyway. On the Mega it is 20 off a 350 HP pool that Wally's refunds in full. There is no bad answer, only a lazier one.

---

## 3. Reading the Room

**One sleeved deck, two modes. Choose before the game, not during.**

| They attack with | Sleeve | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Pokémon ex and Mega ex | **Snake Charmer** | The cage is on. Their Prizes shrink; yours double. |
| Single-prize Pokémon | **[Gengar Gang](./dark.md)** | Concealment is blank and the Mega gifts three Prizes. Weezing's 170 clears their whole tier instead. |
| A mix | Count their attackers | The cage pays off when most of their damage comes from ex. One Flareon does not break it; a deck of them does. |

The tell is their attacker lineup, not their deck's best card. A deck that runs one big ex but attacks with cheap bodies is a single-prize room wearing a costume.

**Swap discipline.** The eleven-card swap in [The Mode Swap](#the-mode-swap) is the whole conversion. Keep both lists printed with the box, count 60 after every swap, and never sleeve half a mode; a deck with one Weezing and one Mega does neither job.

## 4. Reading the Opening Hand

**Turn one, in order.**

| Your hand has | Play |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Buddy-Buddy Poffin** | First, always. Gastly and Toxel to the Bench. |
| **Dawn and a workable board** | Dawn for Gastly, Haunter, Mega Gengar ex. The whole wall in one card. |
| **A bad hand** | Lillie's Determination for 8 while all six Prizes are still up. |
| **Toxel, going second** | *Call for Family* for Seviper and Sableye. Two bodies beat 10 damage. |
| **Shadowy Darkness Energy** | Onto the Benched Gastly. That Gastly is the wall; nothing may touch it. |
| **Poke Pad, spare slot** | Fetch Toxtricity's Toxel half or the Seviper, whichever is missing. |

Going first you cannot attack, and this deck barely minds; nothing here wants to attack on turn one anyway. Build, protect, and let the wall arrive on schedule.

## 5. Things That Will Cost You a Game

| Mistake | What actually happens |
| :--- | :--- |
| Sleeving this mode into a single-prize room | Concealment does nothing all game and the Mega hands over three Prizes. Read the room first. |
| Attacking with the Mega when 230 is not lethal and no second Mega is coming | You traded a third of their Prize requirement for chip damage. Wall first, sweep second. |
| Forgetting Void Gale's move is mandatory | The Mega attacks itself out of Energy. Aim the export at Seviper and it is a feature; forget to aim and the Energy lands somewhere useless. |
| Charging Seviper from hand in the Active Spot | Three turns of attachments in front of an attacker. Surge and Void Gale charge it on the Bench; Scramble Switch and plain Switch bring it forward loaded. |
| Counting Cocky Claw's 90 with the Mega Active | The check reads Bench. Seviper's check reads in play. Send the right attacker for the board you have. |
| Gwynn pitching a Mega Gengar ex | Illegal; it has a Rule Box. Every other Pokémon here is legal fuel. |
| Wally's Compassion on a full-HP Mega to reset Energy | It heals nothing, so the Energy clause never fires. The card is a heal with a rider, not an Energy bounce. |
| Poffin targeting Seviper, Sableye, or Eternatus | Illegal; all three are over 70 HP. Call for Family and Poke Pad are the routes. |
| Rare Candy on the Gastly Poffin just benched | Illegal until next turn. Bench Basics one turn earlier than you need them. |
| Trying to Night Stretcher a Shadowy Darkness Energy | Special Energy is gone forever. Both copies are precious. |
| Spending Scramble Switch as a plain Switch | It is the only Energy-rescue in the deck and there is no second copy. |
| Expecting Concealment against a non-ex attacker | The trigger reads Pokémon ex. Plain Flareon, plain Weezing, and their kin collect full Prizes. |

## Versus the Kitchen Table

**Fox's Hostile Takeover is the matchup this mode was built for.** Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex is Psychic, 280 HP, and Weak to Darkness: *Void Gale* lands 460 and *Pitch-Black Fangs* lands 480, so either ghost or snake one-shots his best card while Concealment zeroes almost everything he can kill in return. Crobat ex at 310 Darkness takes no Weakness and needs two hits; Punk Helmet and Surge counters close the gap. Respect Spidops and Tarountula, which are Grass and erase Sableye, and remember half his board is Darkness, so Risky Ruins collects less than usual.

**Fox's Metal deck plays the cage straight.** Mega Excadrill ex hits 330, and the Mega survives it at 350; Wally's Compassion then undoes the whole hit, which breaks his two-shot math completely. His Fezandipiti and Munkidori pay him nothing back when they die to your ex, but your Seviper and Sableye pay him nothing either, because his attacker is a Mega. It becomes a pure wall race, and only one side heals.

**Fox's Flareon engine is the trap matchup.** Plain Flareon does 180 against ex Pokémon and is not an ex, so it dodges Concealment entirely and two-shots the Mega for one Prize of risk. Wally's resets the two-shot; Seviper one-shots every Flareon print he owns except Flareon ex at 270, which is one Helmet proc or one Ruins counter away from 240's reach. Winnable, but this is the room where [reading the room](#3-reading-the-room) says to think hard about sleeving Gengar Gang instead.
