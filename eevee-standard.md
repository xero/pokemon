## The Thesis

Every Eeveelution ex in Prismatic Evolutions is a **Stage 1 that evolves from Eevee**. Not from Flareon, not from Umbreon. From Eevee. One Basic turns into whichever of eight answers the board needs, and that is the whole deck.

That fact is worth stating loudly because it caused an argument at the table. There is no "base Flareon" to find. There cannot be: the only non-ex Flareon ever printed is Prismatic Evolutions 013, it carries Regulation **G**, and it rotated out in April 2026. Playing Eevee straight into Flareon ex was never a house rule. It is the card working as printed.

**`Rainbow DNA` exists for a narrower reason.** "Eevee ex" is a different card *name* from "Eevee", so by default nothing can be played on top of it at all. Rainbow DNA is the permission slip that lets Eevee ex become an Eeveelution ex. It reads "any Pokémon **ex** that evolves from Eevee," so the one line that genuinely is illegal is Eevee ex into a non-ex Eeveelution. Only a plain Eevee can do that.

### The keystone is the ACE SPEC

> **Sparkling Crystal.** When the Tera Pokémon this card is attached to uses an attack, that attack costs 1 Energy less. **(The Energy can be of any type.)**

That parenthetical is what makes this deck possible. It reduces **coloured** requirements, not just Colorless. Every one of the big "gem" attacks needs Lightning or Grass, and Fox plays neither:

| Gem attack | Printed cost | Payable from his colours |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Flareon ex *Carnelian* 280 | [R][W][L] | **R + W** |
| Umbreon ex *Onyx*, takes a Prize | [L][P][D] | **P + D** |
| Espeon ex *Amazez*, devolves their board | [G][P][D] | **P + D** |
| Glaceon ex *Euclase* | [G][W][D] | W + D |

One Crystal, one Pokémon, so he picks one gem per game. And the best line in the deck is not a gem at all. **Flareon ex holding the Crystal casts *Burning Charge* for a single [R]:** 130 damage, and search the deck for two Basic Energy of any types. That is the engine and the attacker in one card, and it happens to be his favourite Pokémon.

## Sun and Moon

**Espeon is the Sun evolution and Umbreon is the Moon evolution.** That is where the name comes from, and the pairing is aimed squarely at Dad's side of the table.

**Umbreon ex is the reason this build exists.** Every card in the lantern decks is Darkness Weakness ×2, top to bottom: Mega Chandelure ex, Gourgeist ex, Dusknoir, Litwick, Lampent, Pumpkaboo, Duskull. *Moon Mirage* does 160, which becomes **320**. That one-shots Gourgeist ex at 270, Dusknoir at 160, and every small body on the board. Mega Chandelure ex survives at 350, but only by 30.

**Espeon ex is a single copy doing a single job.** *Amazez* devolves **every** evolved Pokémon the opponent controls, shuffling the top Stage card back into their deck. Against a board of Mega Chandelure ex, Dusknoir, Gengar, and Crobat ex that erases an entire game's setup in one attack. It costs [G][P][D], which the Crystal turns into P and D, both of which this build already runs.

Espeon is Darkness Weakness ×2 and Dad's decks are full of Darkness attackers, so it dies the turn after it fires. That is a fair price for undoing his board, and it is why there is only one.

**Battle Cage is the sleeper.** Dad wins with damage counters placed by Abilities, and Tera bench-immunity does not stop those. Dusknoir's *Cursed Blast* puts 13 counters anywhere it likes; Froslass pings every Ability Pokémon each Checkup. Battle Cage prevents damage counters being placed on **Benched** Pokémon by the opponent's attacks and Abilities. Fox places no counters at all, so a card that reads as symmetric is entirely one-sided in his favour.

## Fire and Ice

Flareon and Glaceon, built for a field of strangers rather than for Dad.

**Flareon ex is the haymaker.** *Carnelian* does 280, and **Mega Excadrill ex is Metal**, which means 280 doubles to **560** into its 340 HP. Mega Excadrill is the most-played deck in the format. Fox's favourite Pokémon one-shots it, which is a nice thing to be able to tell him.

Fire also doubles into Mega Venusaur ex at 380, Dhelmise, and the Grass core of Festival Lead. Between them that is close to a fifth of the field.

**Glaceon here is the non-ex print, and that is deliberate.** Glaceon **ex** is Metal Weakness ×2, so it dies to the very Excadrill that Flareon beats. The plain Glaceon gives up **one** Prize instead of two, and *Permeating Chill* does 30 now plus **9 damage counters** at the end of the opponent's next turn. That is 120 for a single [W], and counters ignore Weakness and Resistance entirely.

Giving up two Prizes for every knockout is this archetype's real weakness, and a 1-Prize attacker is the honest fix. It also keeps Fox's third-favourite Eeveelution in the deck without the liability.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Eevee ex cannot become the non-ex Glaceon.** *Rainbow DNA* only permits Pokémon **ex**. Glaceon has to be played onto a plain Eevee.

## Pick Your Ten

**This part is for Fox.**

Your deck is 50 cards that never change, plus 10 cards you choose. Pick before we start shuffling.

**Playing Dad at home? Take Sun and Moon.** Espeon and Umbreon. Umbreon hits his ghosts for double damage, which means almost everything he plays falls over twice as fast. Espeon has one job: use *Amazez* and send his big evolved Pokémon back into his deck. He will not be happy. The two Battle Cage stop him sneaking damage onto your Bench.

**Going to game night? Take Fire and Ice.** Flareon and Glaceon. Flareon's *Carnelian* hits for 280, and doubles to 560 against the metal Pokémon lots of people play, which knocks out almost anything in one shot. Glaceon is not an ex, so if it gets knocked out your opponent only takes **one** Prize instead of two. That matters more than it sounds like it does.

**Either way, three things stay true.** Flareon ex fetches your Energy with *Burning Charge*. Put Sparkling Crystal on whichever Pokémon you want to attack with, because it makes that attack cost one less Energy. And keep your Eeveelutions on the **Bench** until you need them, because Tera means nothing can touch them there.

## Key Card Text

### Eevee ex — Prismatic Evolutions 075 · Reg H

**Basic, Tera, 200 HP, 2 Prizes.** *Rainbow DNA* lets it evolve into any Pokémon **ex** that evolves from Eevee. It cannot do this on your first turn or the turn you play it, so it needs a turn on the board first.

Its attack, *Coruscating Quartz*, costs [R][W][L] and will almost never be paid. Read it as a Basic that holds a decision, not as an attacker.

### Eevee — Prismatic Evolutions 074 · Reg H

*Boosted Evolution* lets it evolve **during your first turn or the turn you play it**, as long as it is Active. This is the turn-one Flareon ex, and it is why three copies are here.

### Eevee — Twilight Masquerade 135 · Reg H

*Ascension* searches your deck for a card that evolves from this Pokémon and puts it straight on. A free tutor into whichever Eeveelution the matchup wants, which matters in a deck built from single copies. One copy, because it costs you the turn.

### Flareon ex — Prismatic Evolutions 014 · Reg H

**Stage 1 from Eevee, Tera, 270 HP.** *Burning Charge* does 130 and searches the deck for up to 2 Basic Energy, attaching them to any one of your Pokémon. It does not say Fire. It says **Basic Energy**, which is how a deck with three colours only runs thirteen Energy cards.

With Sparkling Crystal attached it costs one [R]. One Fire Energy for 130 damage and two Energy off the deck is the strongest repeatable play in the list.

*Carnelian* does 280 and locks it out of attacking next turn. See [Fire and Ice](#fire-and-ice).

### Umbreon ex — Prismatic Evolutions 060 · Reg H

**280 HP**, the highest in the line, and Darkness. *Moon Mirage* does 160 and Confuses, which forces a coin flip on their attack and costs them 30 and their turn on tails.

*Onyx* discards all Energy from this Pokémon and **takes a Prize card**. No attack, no damage, no interaction with HP. With the Crystal it costs two Energy instead of three.

Weak to Grass ×2, which is worth remembering against Festival Lead.

### Espeon ex — Prismatic Evolutions 034 · Reg H

**Stage 1 from Eevee, 270 HP.** *Psych Out* does 160 and discards a random card from their hand. *Amazez* devolves each of the opponent's evolved Pokémon by shuffling the highest Stage card on it into their deck.

Weak to Darkness ×2. Against Dad it is a one-shot weapon that will not survive the reply.

### Glaceon — Prismatic Evolutions 025 · Reg H

**The non-ex, 120 HP, 1 Prize.** *Permeating Chill* does 30, then puts **9 damage counters** on the Defending Pokémon at the end of the opponent's next turn. Counters ignore Weakness and Resistance.

Note the delay. If they retreat the damaged Pokémon, the counters have nowhere to land.

### Sparkling Crystal — Stellar Crown 142 · Reg H

**The ACE SPEC, and one per deck total.** Attacks used by the Tera Pokémon it is attached to cost 1 Energy less, of **any type**, which is what lets a three-colour deck cast three-colour attacks. It is a Tool, so it lives on one Pokémon; moving the plan means moving to a different body, not moving the card.

### Battle Cage — Phantasmal Flames 085 · Reg I

**Stadium.** Prevents all damage counters being placed on **Benched** Pokémon, on both sides, by effects of attacks and Abilities from the opponent's Pokémon. Damage from attacks still applies normally.

In Sun and Moon this is the answer to a whole deck. See [Sun and Moon](#sun-and-moon).

## Game Plans

### 1. The Bench Is Safe, So Wait

Every Eeveelution ex is Tera, which prevents all attack damage while it is Benched. The only Pokémon that can be knocked out is the one currently Active.

So the deck's real skill is patience. Build the Bench, hold Eevee ex undeclared, and only commit to Umbreon or Espeon once you know what you are looking at. Rotate with Switch and it is genuinely possible to go a long game losing one body.

The exception, and it matters at home: **Tera does not stop damage counters placed by Abilities.** That is what Battle Cage is for.

### 2. Flareon ex Pays for Everything

*Burning Charge* is not a fallback attack, it is the Energy engine. Every turn it hits for 130 and puts two more Energy on the board from the deck, which is why the exotic colours can sit at three copies each and still be findable.

Lead with it, keep it Active while the Bench fills, and let it fund whichever gem attack the game asks for.

### 3. Three Knockouts and It Is Over

Every ex here gives up **2 Prizes**. Three knockouts and Fox has lost, which is a big change from a single-prize deck and the honest cost of the archetype.

Two mitigations. Tera keeps the Bench untouchable, so only sloppy rotation loses bodies. And in Fire and Ice, the non-ex Glaceon gives up one Prize rather than two, which is the only real structural fix available.

### 4. Choosing the Crystal's Home

One Sparkling Crystal, one Pokémon, and the choice is not obvious.

- **On Flareon ex** it makes *Burning Charge* a one-Energy 130 with two Energy attached. Best default, best in a long game.
- **On Umbreon ex** it turns *Onyx* into a two-Energy free Prize.
- **On Espeon ex** it makes *Amazez* castable at all from this Energy line.

Decide by asking whether the game is a race or a grind. Races want it on Flareon; grinds want it on the gem.
