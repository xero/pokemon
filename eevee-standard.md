## The Thesis

There is one card that makes this deck legal, coherent, and — I think — the most fun thing he could possibly play:

> **Eevee ex — *Rainbow DNA*.** This Pokémon can evolve into **any Pokémon ex that evolves from Eevee** if you play it from your hand onto this Pokémon.

Prismatic Evolutions printed **all eight Eeveelutions as Stellar Tera Pokémon ex**, plus Eevee ex itself. All nine are Regulation **H**. All nine are legal right now.

**One Basic Pokémon that turns into whichever of eight answers the board needs.** That is a toolbox deck, and toolbox decks are usually a nightmare to build because you can't find the pieces and you can't pay for six Energy types.

This deck solves both problems with cards he already owns.

### Problem 1: finding the pieces

**Noctowl — *Jewel Seeker*:** when it evolves, if you have any **Tera** Pokémon in play, search your deck for **2 Trainer cards**. Every eeveelution ex is Tera, so the condition is always on.

### Problem 2: paying for six Energy types

This is the real one, and the answer is the card he got for his birthday:

> **Flareon ex — *Burning Charge*** [R][C], 130 damage. Search your deck for up to **2 Basic Energy cards** and attach them to 1 of your Pokémon.

**It does not say Fire.** It says *Basic Energy* — any two types, attached to whichever Pokémon you choose. Flareon ex is not the attacker in this deck. **It is the rainbow generator**, and it deals 130 while doing it.

Add **Crispin** (Supporter: search 2 Basic Energy of *different types*, attach one, keep one) and the mana problem is simply solved.

## The Payoff: Umbreon ex Steals a Prize

This is the combo I'd build the deck around, and every piece is verified.

> **Umbreon ex — PRE 060.** Darkness, **280 HP**, Stage 1 from Eevee. Tera.
> - ***Moon Mirage*** [D][C][C], **160** — your opponent's Active Pokémon is now Confused.
> - ***Onyx*** [L][P][D] — **Discard all Energy from this Pokémon and take a Prize card.**

***Onyx* takes a Prize card without knocking anything out.** No attack, no damage, no interaction with HP at all. You simply take one of your six.

The cost is three Energy of three different types — Lightning, Psychic, and Darkness — which is normally a deckbuilding disaster. Here:

- **Burning Charge** fetches any 2 Basic Energy and attaches them to one Pokémon. That's two thirds of *Onyx* in one attack.
- **Crispin** fetches 2 of different types, attaches one.

And then the ACE SPEC closes it:

> **Sparkling Crystal — SCR 142 (ACE SPEC Tool).** If a **Tera** Pokémon with this attached uses an attack that requires **all the Energy on the Pokémon to be removed**, the attack can be used even if the Energy removed is **1 less** than required.

Read that against *Onyx*. It is an attack that discards all Energy from the Pokémon. **Sparkling Crystal makes *Onyx* cost two Energy instead of three.** The card was templated for exactly this.

```
Flareon ex → Burning Charge → fetch Lightning + Psychic, attach to Umbreon ex
Umbreon ex + Sparkling Crystal → ONYX → take a Prize card
```

Umbreon ex sits on the Bench at **280 HP behind Tera** — immune to attack damage — steps up, takes a free Prize, and steps back.

> [!WARNING]
> **Sparkling Crystal is an ACE SPEC: exactly one per deck, total, across all ACE SPEC cards.** It is doing two different jobs in this list (cheapening *Onyx* and cheapening *Burning Charge*) and you only get one. That is a genuine tension, not a rounding error.

## Which Eeveelution, and Why

The 1-ofs are the toolbox. Each answers something specific.

| Card | Type | What it answers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Flareon ex** | Fire | The engine. Always want one. |
| **Umbreon ex** | Darkness | The win condition — *Onyx* steals Prizes |
| **Leafeon ex** | Grass | ***Verdant Storm*** [G][C], **60× the total Energy attached to your opponent's Pokémon.** Against an Energy-heavy deck this is a one-shot. Also hits **Mega Zygarde ex**, which is Weak to Grass ×2. |
| **Glaceon ex** | Water | Water hits Fire for double — this is the card that beats [Build 1](./fire-standard.md) and any Charizard deck |

**Leafeon ex is the sleeper.** *Verdant Storm* scales on **the opponent's** Energy, which means it gets better the better their board is. Against a fully-loaded Charizard with three Energy attached and two more on the Bench, that's 300. Its second attack *Moss Agate* [G][R][W] heals **100 from each of your Benched Pokémon** — in a deck of 270 HP Tera bodies, that's an absurd amount of value.

**The two Psychic eeveelutions (Espeon ex, Sylveon ex) are the obvious flex additions** if you want a fifth colour. I left them out only to keep the Energy line honest.

## Game Plans

### 1. Eevee ex Is the Real Engine, Not Eevee

Plain Eevee has *Boosted Evolution* — it evolves the turn you play it, **in the Active Spot only** — so it's your speed. But it has 50 HP and dies to a stiff breeze.

**Eevee ex has 200 HP, Tera, and *Rainbow DNA*.** Put it on the Bench behind Tera where nothing can touch it, and it becomes whichever eeveelution the board demands, *at the moment you learn what that is.*

That's the deck's actual skill: **holding the decision.** You don't commit to Umbreon or Glaceon until you see what he's playing.

### 2. The Onyx Clock

*Onyx* takes a Prize for two Energy (with Sparkling Crystal) and no combat. Two Onyx turns is a third of the game, taken without ever winning a fight.

Meanwhile Umbreon's *Moon Mirage* does **160 and Confuses** — and Confusion means a coin flip to attack, with tails costing them 30 damage and their whole turn.

**So Umbreon ex alone presents two clocks the opponent has to answer differently:** one that ignores their board entirely, and one that hits hard and locks their Active.

### 3. Jewel Seeker Finds the Toolbox

With 1-ofs scattered through the deck, "search 2 Trainer cards" doesn't find your Glaceon ex — Noctowl only gets **Trainers**. So the Pokémon toolbox is found with **Ultra Ball** and the Trainers are found with **Noctowl**.

Practical consequence: **4 Ultra Ball is non-negotiable** and you should be willing to discard aggressively to it. Night Stretcher gets the pieces back.

### 4. Six Prizes, and Everything Costs Two

Every ex here is a **2-Prize** Pokémon. **Three knockouts and he loses.** That is the honest cost of the archetype and it is a big shift from his current all-1-Prize deck.

The mitigation is *Tera*. A Benched eeveelution ex takes **zero** damage from attacks — so the only Pokémon that can be knocked out is the one currently Active. Rotate with Switch, and he can genuinely go a whole game losing one or two.

## Honest Weaknesses

**It is the hardest of the three to pilot.** Six Energy types, a toolbox of 1-ofs, one ACE SPEC doing two jobs, and a win condition that requires assembling three specific Energy types. This is a deck for a kid who has already got comfortable with the basics — which, after a season of the Charizard deck, he might well be.

**It is also the most expensive.** Nine different eeveelution ex, four Noctowl-line cards, four Crispin, and a Sparkling Crystal. There's no way to build it cheaply.

**Weak to Fighting ×2 on Eevee ex** — so [Build 3](./fighting-standard.md) beats it, badly. Worth knowing if you end up building more than one of these.

**Consistency risk from the 1-ofs.** A toolbox with no way to search Pokémon reliably is just a pile of cards you never draw. Ultra Ball carries this deck; if you cut it, the whole thing collapses.
