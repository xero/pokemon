## The Thesis

**The cheapest attack in the deck scales off the opponent's own Bench, and it hands itself back when it dies.**

***Mind Jack* costs one [D] and does 10, plus 30 more for each of your opponent's Benched Pokémon.**

| Their Bench | Mind Jack |
| ---: | ---: |
| 3 | 100 |
| 4 | **130** |
| 5 | **160** |

Every deck worth beating benches wide. Dragapult needs Dreepy, Drakloak, Munkidori and Budew down. Mega Excadrill drops four bodies off a turn-one Precious Trolley. Thwackey only tutors while its Dipplin is Active, so they bench two or three of them. **The decks that beat you are the decks that feed this attack.**

***Infinite Shadow*: if this Pokémon is Knocked Out by damage from an attack, put it into your hand instead of the discard pile.** One Prize, and the card comes back to be rebuilt. That is the second half of the thesis, and it is why a 130 HP Stage 2 is the main attacker in a format of 340 HP Megas — **you are not trying to survive, you are trying to be cheap.**

**The dogs did not leave, they changed jobs.** [Okidogi ex](#okidogi-ex) is the closer now: *Chain-Crazed* under a [Binding Mochi](#binding-mochi) is **300**, which one-shots a 280 HP Gengar ex and two-shots every Mega in the format. It costs three Energy and two Prizes, so it comes out when something has to die *this turn*, not as the opener.

| Attacker | Cost | Output | Prizes it pays |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | ---: |
| **Gengar, *Mind Jack*** | **[D]** | **130–160, scaling off their Bench** | **1, and it returns to hand** |
| Okidogi ex, *Chain-Crazed* | [D][D][C] | 260, **300** with a Mochi | 2 |
| Toxtricity, *Gentle Slap* | [D][D][C] | 100 | 1 |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Mega Gengar ex is not in this deck, and the reason is arithmetic.** *Shadowy Concealment* only discounts a knockout "by **damage from an attack**." Half the decks in this room never deal any: Dipplin and Thwackey are single-prize so there is nothing to discount, and *Chaotic Pain* **places damage counters**, which is not damage from an attack at all. The tax collected nothing in two of four matchups, cost eleven cards, and bricked three games. See [Alternatives](#alternatives).

# Pokémon

### Okidogi ex

**250 HP Basic, Fighting Weakness, Retreat 3.** The closer, and the guard the deck is named for.

***Chain-Crazed* does 130, and 130 more while this Pokémon is Poisoned.** 260 on its own, **300** under a [Binding Mochi](#binding-mochi). That number is why it stays in the deck: 300 one-shots a 280 HP Gengar ex outright and two-shots every Mega in the format, and nothing else here comes close.

**It self-starts.** *Poisonous Musculature* costs one Colorless, searches two Basic Darkness Energy out of the deck, attaches them here and Poisons it. One Energy in hand becomes a dog holding three, already Poisoned, in a single turn without spending a Supporter.

**Bring it out to kill something, not to hold the Active Spot.** Poison is ten damage to itself at every Checkup, so a dog that stands there Poisoned for five turns has taken 50 from nobody. In testing a dog died to its own Poison plus chip while waiting for a target. Promote it the turn it has three Energy and a kill, not before.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Nothing in this deck can search for the dog except two Supporters.** [Hilda](#hilda) reads *Evolution Pokémon* and this is a Basic. [Poke Pad](#poke-pad) reads *no Rule Box* and this has one. [Buddy-Buddy Poffin](#buddy-buddy-poffin) reads *70 HP or less* and this is 250. That leaves [Dawn](#dawn) and [Grimsley's Move](#grimsleys-move), which is exactly why both are in the list. See [The Search Blind Spot](#the-search-blind-spot).

Four copies, because four is also how you draw one.

### Gastly

70 HP, Darkness, Retreat 1. Four copies, and every Gengar in the deck sits on top of it.

It is inside [Poffin](#buddy-buddy-poffin) range, [Poke Pad](#poke-pad) finds it, [Grimsley's](#grimsleys-move) benches it, and [Risky Ruins](#risky-ruins) never touches it because it is Darkness.

Bench one on turn one whether or not you need it. Rare Candy cannot be played on a Basic that arrived the same turn, so turn one's Gastly is turn two's Gengar.

### Haunter

**Two copies, and they are the bridge, not the backup.**

A game was lost in testing holding a Gastly, two Stage 2s and a Hilda with no way to connect them, because the list ran zero Haunter and every Rare Candy was gone. Hilda searches for an *Evolution Pokémon* — with no Stage 1 in the deck there is nothing for it to fetch that helps, and Rare Candy becomes a single point of failure.

Two Haunter makes Hilda and Poke Pad into Mega-line enablers and gives Candy a fallback. *Sinister Surge* can also feed a benched Haunter, which then evolves into a Gengar that is already holding its one Energy.

### Gengar

**Stage 2 from Haunter, 130 HP, Darkness, Retreat 1. Perfect Order 050, regulation mark J.** The attacker.

***Mind Jack* costs one [D]** and does 10 plus 30 for each of your opponent's Benched Pokémon. [The Thesis](#the-thesis) has the table; the short version is 130 at a four-Bench and 160 at five.

***Infinite Shadow*: if this Pokémon is Knocked Out by damage from an attack from your opponent's Pokémon, put it into your hand instead of the discard pile.** Attached cards still go to the discard, so you lose the Energy and keep the Gengar.

**It is the only Gengar in Standard that this deck can actually find.** Both Gengar ex prints carry a Rule Box, so [Poke Pad](#poke-pad) cannot search either one. This print has none, which means the whole line — Gastly, Haunter, Gengar — is tutorable at Item speed off three Poke Pad. That single fact is why this card replaced two better-looking ones.

Three copies. It dies constantly and that is the plan; *Infinite Shadow* means the third copy is mostly insurance against the Prizes.

> [!CAUTION]
> ***Infinite Shadow* reads "damage from an attack," and so does the exception.** A Gengar removed by *Chaotic Pain* is knocked out by placed damage counters rather than damage, so it goes to the discard like anything else. [Night Stretcher](#alternatives) is the answer if that matchup becomes common.

### Toxel

*Call for Family* searches two Basic Pokémon out of the deck and benches them for one Darkness Energy. **It is the only card in the deck besides [Grimsley's](#grimsleys-move) and [Dawn](#dawn) that can put an Okidogi ex on the board**, because Poffin cannot reach 250 HP.

Three copies. It holds the Basic count and it is a zero-Prize body.

**Do not retreat it.** Toxel is Retreat 1 and retreating **discards** that Energy, which is the one you just spent on *Call for Family*. Let [Poffin](#buddy-buddy-poffin) bench it instead and it never needs to be Active at all.

### Toxtricity

***Sinister Surge* attaches a Basic Darkness Energy from the deck to a Benched Darkness Pokémon once a turn**, and puts two damage counters on it. It is an Ability, so it accelerates on turns your Supporter is busy.

**Two copies, down from three, because the engine outran the deck.** With three copies and ten Energy, testing produced hands with Toxel and Toxtricity to spare and Energy sitting idle. *Mind Jack* costs one; the engine does not need to deliver three a turn any more.

**The Toxtricity does not have to be Benched — the target does.** An Active Toxtricity still Surges, just not onto itself. *Gentle Slap* does 100 for three Energy and is a one-Prize emergency attack, but it is a three-shot on anything that matters, so reach for the dog instead when you need a body dead.

# Trainers — Supporters

### Lillie's Determination

Shuffle your hand into your deck and draw six, or **eight while you still hold all six Prizes**. Four copies, and it is the entire draw package.

Play it early rather than saving it. Before you take your first Prize it is the biggest card in the deck.

### Dawn

**Search your deck for a Basic Pokémon, a Stage 1 Pokémon, and a Stage 2 Pokémon.** Two copies, and it does two jobs nothing else here does.

It is **one of two cards that can find an Okidogi ex**, and it is the only one that puts it in your hand rather than on the Bench. In the same card it takes a Gastly and a Haunter, or a Haunter and a Gengar, so it builds the attacking line from nothing.

No tournament Toxtricity list runs Dawn, and the reason is that those lists have no evolution line for it to fetch. This one does, and the Basic it finds is the card nothing else reaches.

### Grimsley's Move

**Look at the top 7 cards of your deck and put a Darkness Pokémon you find there onto your Bench.** Two copies, Phantasmal Flames 090.

**Every Pokémon in this deck is Darkness**, so it never whiffs on colour — with eighteen Pokémon in sixty it finds one **95.6%** of the time. What it puts on the Bench is a Gastly, a Toxel, or the Okidogi ex that no Item in the deck can reach.

It is the second Okidogi finder and the cheaper one, because it benches the dog directly instead of putting it in hand to be manually played.

**It cannot be used on your first turn**, so it is a turn-two card. The other seven cards go to the **bottom** of the deck, which is a real cost in a deck that wants its Rare Candy.

### Team Rocket's Petrel

**Search your deck for a Trainer card.** Two copies, and it is what lets this list run one-ofs at all.

Every single-copy card here is a Trainer — [Hilda](#hilda), [Janine's](#janines-secret-art), [AZ's](#azs-tranquility), [Energy Switch](#energy-switch), [Energy Recycler](#energy-recycler), [Scramble Switch](#scramble-switch) — which means none of them is ever really drawn at the wrong time. You hold Petrel and the one-of arrives on the turn it is live.

**That rule is why there are no situational Pokémon in this deck.** Petrel cannot find a Pokémon, so a conditional Pokémon can only be fetched by spending a whole Supporter turn on Dawn or Hilda. A conditional Trainer is fine; a conditional Pokémon is a dead draw.

### Boss's Orders

Drags a Benched Pokémon into the Active Spot. Two copies, and without them Dragapult ex is untouchable — it takes no damage at all while it sits on their Bench.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Gusting costs you damage now.** *Mind Jack* scales off the number of Pokémon on their Bench, so pulling one into the Active Spot drops your own attack by **30**. Gust to kill a specific thing — a Munkidori before it moves counters, a Budew before it locks your Items, the Dragapult you cannot otherwise reach — and never on reflex.

### Hilda

**Search your deck for an Evolution Pokémon and an Energy card, and put them into your hand.** One copy.

It is the Energy-in-hand card, and in a nine-Energy deck that matters on the turns the manual attachment is the whole plan. Its search half reaches Haunter, Gengar and Toxtricity.

One copy rather than two, because [Poke Pad](#poke-pad) now covers the same search at Item speed and for free, and because the Energy half matters less once the main attack costs one. Petrel is the second copy.

### Janine's Secret Art

Choose up to two of your Darkness Pokémon, search a Basic Darkness Energy out of the deck for each, attach them, and if one of them was your **Active**, it is now Poisoned.

**One Energy per Pokémon, two different Pokémon, never two on one.** One copy, and its job is narrow now: it is the card that Poisons a dog on the turn you promote it, so *Chain-Crazed* is 260 and the [Mochi](#binding-mochi) makes it 300 without spending the dog's own attack on *Musculature*.

### AZ's Tranquility

**Switch your Active Pokémon with one of your Benched Pokémon. If the Pokémon you moved to the Bench is a Pokémon ex, heal 80 damage from it.** One copy.

It is the dog's exit: a dog that leaves by Retreat pays three Energy out of a nine-Energy deck and buries them, and one that leaves by AZ's keeps every Energy and arrives 80 healthier.

**It works when the Items do not.** Budew's *Itchy Pollen* shuts off Item cards for a turn and it is in every winning Dragapult list; under it, AZ's is the only rotation in the deck that still happens.

One copy rather than two, because *Infinite Shadow* is a free AZ's that the Gengar performs on itself.

# Trainers — Items

### Rare Candy

Four copies, and the count is not negotiable after testing. Three Gengar over four Gastly, and Candy is the road; the game that got away was the game the Candy ran out. Never on your first turn, and never on a Basic that entered play this turn.

### Poke Pad

**Search your deck for a Pokémon that doesn't have a Rule Box.** Three copies, Perfect Order 081. The printed card is *Poké Pad*; the database drops the accent.

**Every Pokémon in this deck except Okidogi ex has no Rule Box**, so this one Item tutors Gastly, Haunter, Gengar, Toxel and Toxtricity — the entire attacking line and the entire Energy engine, at Item speed, with no discard and no Supporter spent.

Tournament Toxtricity lists run it at four almost without exception, and it is the single card that made the Perfect Order Gengar the right Gengar. Both Gengar ex prints have Rule Boxes and this cannot see either of them.

### Buddy-Buddy Poffin

**Two Basic Pokémon with 70 HP or less, straight to the Bench.** Two copies, and the count is two rather than one because a single copy is prized **10%** of games and cannot be fetched from there.

Gastly and Toxel are the only legal targets, seven bodies between them, and that is the turn-one play going first. **It cannot reach Okidogi ex at 250 HP** — that is [Grimsley's](#grimsleys-move) job.

It also removes the reason to ever put a Toxel in the Active Spot, which saves the Energy that retreating it would discard.

### Energy Switch

Moves a Basic Energy from one of your Pokémon to another. One copy.

*Sinister Surge* only ever reaches the Bench, so this is the pipe that brings that Energy up to the Active — and with the engine banking on the Bench every turn, a deck with no pipe strands its own fuel. Petrel is the second copy; [Scramble Switch](#scramble-switch) is the version that moves all of it at once.

### Energy Recycler

**Shuffle up to 5 Basic Energy cards from your discard pile into your deck.** One copy.

All three accelerators — *Sinister Surge*, *Janine's Secret Art*, *Poisonous Musculature* — search the **deck**, so Energy in the discard is not merely spent, it is invisible to the engine. One copy rather than two because nine Energy drains slower than ten did and *Mind Jack* only ever buries one at a time.

### Scramble Switch

**The ACE SPEC.** Switch your Active Pokémon with one of your Benched Pokémon; if you do, you may move **any amount of Energy** from the Pokémon you moved to your Bench to the new Active Pokémon. Surging Sparks 186.

It is AZ's Tranquility and three Energy Switches in one Item, and it answers the deck's real leak: two Toxtricity bank Energy onto Bench bodies every turn, and the Active is where it needs to be. Promote a fresh dog, bring three Energy with it, Janine for the Poison, *Chain-Crazed* for 300 — all in one turn.

**Prime Catcher lost this seat in testing.** Its second switch is mandatory once you gust, so it rotates your own dog and clears its Poison, and in the game that decided it there was nothing on the board worth switching to. Two Boss's Orders carry the gust.

# Trainers — Tool & Stadium

### Binding Mochi

**Attacks by the Poisoned Pokémon this card is attached to do 40 more damage to your opponent's Active Pokémon.** Two copies.

It is the 260-to-300 breakpoint, and 300 is what one-shots a 280 HP Gengar ex. It does nothing on a Gengar — *Mind Jack* has no Poison and no interest in one — so both copies belong on dogs.

**It is blank under Festival Grounds**, where nothing can be Poisoned at all.

### Risky Ruins

**Two damage counters on every Basic non-Darkness Pokémon either player benches.** Every Pokémon in this deck is Darkness, so it is completely one-sided.

The 20 stays on the card when it evolves, which is how a chipped Drilbur becomes part of a lethal number on a 340 HP Excadrill.

| Their Basic | HP | After Ruins |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| **Applin** | 40 | **20** |
| Drilbur, Dreepy, Grookey | 70 | 50 |
| Riolu | 80 | 60 |

It also taxes exactly the wide Benches that *Mind Jack* is pricing. Two copies.

### Battle Cage

**Prevent all damage counters from being placed on Benched Pokémon, both yours and your opponent's, by effects of attacks and Abilities from the opponent's Pokémon.** Two copies, Phantasmal Flames 085.

**It has two jobs and both of them matter here.**

- **Dragapult.** *Phantom Dive* is 200 to your Active **plus six damage counters onto your Bench**. The Cage deletes all six, and Munkidori's *Adrena-Brain* cannot move counters onto your Bench either. Five winning Dragapult lists in six run Risky Ruins themselves, so yours is unplayable in that room and the Cage is the stadium you *can* put down.
- ***Chaotic Pain*.** The 30th Celebration Gengar places 13 damage counters on any Pokémon, and on your Bench the Cage stops it dead. It forces their Gengar to spend its attack on your Active, where a 250 HP dog shrugs off 130. This is confirmed from testing.

**Four stadiums, two of each, and either one bumps Festival Grounds** — which is the whole Applin matchup. [Game plan 4](#4-choosing-the-stadium) has the leads.

## The Search Blind Spot

This deck lost a game it was three Prizes ahead in, and the cause was a hole in the search rather than a bad draw. It is worth writing down once, because every list in this family has it.

| Searcher | Reads | Finds Okidogi ex? |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| Hilda | an **Evolution** Pokémon | ✗ it is a Basic |
| Poke Pad | a Pokémon with **no Rule Box** | ✗ it has one |
| Buddy-Buddy Poffin | a Basic with **70 HP or less** | ✗ it is 250 |
| Toxel, *Call for Family* | 2 Basic Pokémon | ✓ but needs Toxel Active and an Energy |
| **Dawn** | a Basic, a Stage 1, a Stage 2 | **✓** |
| **Grimsley's Move** | a **Darkness** Pokémon in the top 7 | **✓** |

Four copies of your closer and two cards that can go and get it. That is the ratio, and it is why [Dawn](#dawn) and [Grimsley's](#grimsleys-move) are both maindecked even though no tournament list runs either.

**The mirror of the same rule is what picked the Gengar.** Poke Pad reads *no Rule Box*, which excludes both Gengar ex prints and includes the Perfect Order one. Cutting Mega Gengar ex made the entire evolution line no-Rule-Box, and the line went from "hope you draw it" to "three Items tutor any piece of it."

## The Energy Engine

Four sources put Darkness Energy on the board, and only one uses your hand.

| Source | From | To | Costs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Manual attachment | hand | anywhere | your attachment for the turn |
| *Sinister Surge* | deck | a **Benched** Darkness Pokémon, once per Toxtricity | nothing, it is an Ability |
| *Janine's Secret Art* | deck | up to 2 Darkness Pokémon, one each, Poisons your Active | your Supporter |
| *Poisonous Musculature* | deck | Okidogi ex only, Poisons it | your attack |

**Three of the four deliver to the Bench, and your attacker is in the Active Spot.** That is the leak, and [Energy Switch](#energy-switch) and [Scramble Switch](#scramble-switch) are the pipes. A deck with the engine and no pipe banks fuel it cannot spend.

**Mind Jack costs one, which is the whole reason nine Energy is enough.** The three-Energy *Chain-Crazed* is paid for in a single turn by *Poisonous Musculature*, or by Scramble Switch moving a Bench pile onto a fresh dog.

# Game Plans

---

## 1. The opening

**Going first:** Poffin two bodies onto the Bench, attach, pass. Gastly first if you have the choice — turn one's Gastly is turn two's Gengar.

**Going second:** attach to a Gastly and start building, or attach to an Active Okidogi and use *Poisonous Musculature* for three Energy and the Poison in one turn.

**Turn two is the Gengar turn.** Rare Candy a Gastly that was already down, attach one Energy, *Mind Jack*. At a three-Bench that is already 100 and it costs one card of fuel.

Play Lillie's before you take a Prize, while it still draws eight. Grimsley's Move cannot be used on your first turn.

## 2. Leading with the ghost

**Mind Jack is the default attack and Gengar is the default Active.** One Energy, one Prize, and it comes back to your hand when it dies. Against every deck in the room it trades better than anything else you own:

| | Their attacker kills your Gengar for | Your Gengar kills their support for |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| any Pokémon ex | 1 Prize, and you get the card back | 1–2 Prizes |

Rebuild it with a Gastly and a Rare Candy, or with the same copy *Infinite Shadow* just returned. **You want their Bench wide**, which is the opposite of what every other deck wants, so do not clear it out for them.

## 3. When to bring out a dog

The dog is a scalpel. Promote it when all three are true:

1. Something needs to die **this turn** and 300 is the number that does it.
2. The dog has three Energy, or can get there this turn via *Musculature*, Janine plus an attachment, or [Scramble Switch](#scramble-switch).
3. It is Poisoned, or Janine is in hand — 260 without the Poison is usually the wrong number.

**Then get it out again.** Poison is ten a turn to itself, and a dog parked in the Active Spot chips itself to death waiting for the next target. [AZ's](#azs-tranquility) keeps its Energy; Retreat burns three.

## 4. Choosing the stadium

| Room | Lead | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dragapult** | **Battle Cage** | deletes six *Phantom Dive* counters and shuts off Munkidori. Their own Ruins makes yours unplayable |
| **Chaotic Pain Gengar** | **Battle Cage** | it cannot snipe your Bench; the 130 has to go into a 250 HP dog |
| **Mega Excadrill** | **Risky Ruins** | the Drilbur chip is part of the lethal number |
| **Applin / Thwackey** | **either, immediately** | any stadium turns off *Festival Lead*. Ruins is better, because Applin is 40 HP |

**Against Festival Grounds the stadium war is the matchup.** With it up, Dipplin attacks twice for 200 a turn and your Mochi is blank; bumped, it attacks once for 100. Four stadiums plus two Petrel is six outs against their three or four.

## 5. Bench discipline

Your own Bench wants **zero-Prize bodies and no spare Okidogi**. A benched dog is two Prizes sitting in the open with no tax to discount it, and this deck has no tax any more. Keep one Gastly spare — that is next turn's Gengar — plus the Toxtricity, and leave the dogs in the deck until they have a job.
